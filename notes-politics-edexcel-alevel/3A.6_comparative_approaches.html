3A.6 Comparative Approaches

Politics — Edexcel (9PL0) — Component 3A: Comparative Politics — USA

This topic is tested directly by Section B of the paper: one compulsory 12-mark comparative question (AO1 6 / AO2 6). It is the one topic on the route you cannot avoid, so it deserves more attention than a sixth of your revision time.

The three approaches are tools for explaining similarities and differences between the UK and US systems, not extra content to describe. The marks come from applying them.

Rational approach — explains behaviour by the calculations of individuals pursuing their own interests.
Cultural approach — explains behaviour by shared ideas, values, beliefs and group attachments.
Structural approach — explains behaviour by the institutions, rules and processes that shape and constrain action.
Comparative politics — the systematic comparison of political systems to explain their similarities and differences.

1. The three approaches

ApproachAsksTypical explanationLimitation
RationalWhat is in this actor's interest?US legislators defy their party because re-election depends on a district and a primary electorate, not on the leadershipTakes preferences as given; struggles with behaviour that is not self-interested; risks unfalsifiability
CulturalWhat do people in this society believe and value?Americans accept judicial authority over contested questions; the British treat it as overreachRisks circularity; struggles to explain change within one country over time
StructuralWhat do the rules permit, reward and prevent?UK party discipline is strong because the executive is drawn from the legislature, so a government's survival and an MP's promotion both depend on supporting itCannot explain where institutions came from, or why identical rules produce different results in different societies

They are complementary, not rival: institutions set the field of play, culture determines what is thinkable within it, and individuals calculate given both. An answer relying on only one will mistake a feature of the design for a feature of the people, or the reverse.

2. Constitutional arrangements compared

UKUSA
FormUncodified, unentrenchedCodified, entrenched
AmendmentOrdinary Act of ParliamentTwo-thirds of Congress, three-quarters of states
SovereigntyParliamentary — legally undividedPopular, expressed through a constitution binding all branches
TerritorialUnitary with devolution; powers rest on statuteFederal; the division is entrenched
RightsHRA 1998 — an ordinary statute; declarations of incompatibilityBill of Rights — entrenched; courts may strike down

Structural explanation: the UK's arrangements emerged incrementally without a founding moment; the US Constitution was written at one, by delegates bargaining between states that would not otherwise have united. Cultural explanation: American constitutional patriotism — a nation without a single ethnic identity using the Constitution as a focus of allegiance — gives the document and its interpreters standing no UK equivalent possesses.

3. The executives compared

UK Prime MinisterUS President
SelectionLeader of the party commanding Commons confidenceSeparately elected via the Electoral College
Relationship to legislatureFused — sits in and commands itSeparated — must bargain with it
TenureInsecure — removable by their own party at any timeFixed four-year term; only impeachment removes, and never has

CabinetCollective responsibility binds; senior colleagues can remove the PMAdvisory; the President is not bound by it

The formulation worth memorising: the PM is more powerful domestically and less secure in office; the President is less able to legislate and cannot be removed by their own party. Fusion concentrates power and makes the leader dependent on the party; separation disperses power and makes the leader independent of it. That is a single structural cause producing both differences.

4. The judiciaries compared

UK Supreme CourtUS Supreme Court
Power over legislationMay interpret and declare incompatibility; may not strike downMay declare acts of Congress unconstitutional and void
BasisStatute — CRA 2005, HRA 1998Asserted in Marbury (1803)
AppointmentIndependent selection commissionPresidential nomination, Senate confirmation — overtly political
TenureTo retirement ageLife

Structural explanation (the stronger one): a codified entrenched constitution gives the US Court a higher law to enforce against Congress; parliamentary sovereignty leaves the UK Court interpreting statute it cannot override. Cultural explanation: differing attitudes to judicial authority — but this risks circularity, since Americans may accept judicial authority because their judges have long exercised it.

5. Electoral and party systems compared

UKUSA
SystemFPTP for Westminster; proportional systems in devolved bodiesFPTP for Congress; Electoral College for the presidency
Candidate selectionLocal and national partyPrimary voters
Party disciplineStrong — whips, patronage, confidenceWeaker, though rising with polarisation
Third partiesFail nationally; succeed regionally (SNP)Fail almost entirely; insurgents capture a major party instead
Campaign financeCapped national spending; PPERA 2000Spending is protected speech; Super PACs

Rational explanation of the discipline difference: in the UK the party controls selection, so loyalty is a precondition of a career; in the US primary voters control it, so defying the leadership carries little cost while cooperating with the other party may carry a great deal. Same type of actor, opposite incentives — which is rational explanation at its strongest, since it requires no assumption that legislators differ in character.

6. Pressure groups and civil rights compared

US groups have more access points: federalism creates fifty state governments, the separation of powers makes executive, both chambers and the courts independent routes, and judicial review makes litigation effective. UK groups must generally work on the government, because parliamentary sovereignty largely closes the judicial route and the executive controls the legislature.

On rights, the US entrenches them and gives courts the power to enforce against the legislature; the UK protects them by ordinary statute and political restraint. The trade-off is the same one that recurs across every comparison: entrenchment secures rights against majorities at the cost of handing contested questions to unelected judges.

7. Applying the approaches

A Section B answer should name the approach, apply it, and assess it. For example: "The structural approach best explains stronger UK party discipline, because the executive is drawn from the legislature, so both a government's survival and an MP's promotion depend on supporting it. A cultural explanation — British deference — is weaker, because it cannot explain why rebellion rates have risen sharply while the culture has not obviously changed." That sentence pattern earns AO1, AO2 and the evaluation in one paragraph.

Quick recall

  • Rational (interests) · cultural (values) · structural (institutions) — complementary, not rival
  • Structural is usually the strongest, because the same actors behave differently under different rules
  • Cultural risks circularity; rational takes preferences as given
  • PM: more powerful domestically, less secure. President: less able to legislate, cannot be removed by their party
  • UK Court declares incompatibility; US Court strikes down — because one has a higher law to enforce
  • US pressure groups have more access points because federalism and separation create them
  • Section B is compulsory and comparative — name the approach, apply it, assess it
