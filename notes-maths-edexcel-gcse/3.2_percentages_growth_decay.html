3.2.1 Percentages as Parts per Hundred (R9)

A percentage is a number of parts per hundred: $48\% = \dfrac{48}{100} = 0.48$.
Percentages above 100% describe amounts larger than the whole: $250\%$ of a quantity is $2.5$ times the quantity.

Percentages of amounts on the non-calculator paper

Find 10% by dividing by 10, then halve for 5%, halve again for 2.5%, or divide by 100 for 1%. Combine these building blocks.

Work out $22.5\%$ of £280.

$10\% = 28$, so $20\% = 56$; $2.5\% = 7$. Therefore $22.5\% = 56 + 7 = \mathbf{£63}$.

Expressing one quantity as a percentage of another

$A$ as a percentage of $B$ $= \dfrac{A}{B} \times 100\%$   (convert to the same units first)
(a) 54 minutes as a percentage of 1.5 hours: $\dfrac{54}{90} \times 100 = \mathbf{60\%}$.
(b) £21 as a percentage of £14: $\dfrac{21}{14} \times 100 = \mathbf{150\%}$.

Comparing two quantities using percentages

Mia scored 39 out of 50 on a science test and 57 out of 75 on a history test. Which was the better score?

Science: $\tfrac{39}{50} = 78\%$. History: $\tfrac{57}{75} = 76\%$. The science score was better.

3.2.2 Percentage Change Using Multipliers (R9)

R9 asks you to interpret percentage changes multiplicatively. Instead of working out the change and then adding or subtracting, multiply by a single decimal multiplier.

Increase by $r\%$: multiplier $= 1 + \dfrac{r}{100}$    e.g. $+15\% \to 1.15$
Decrease by $r\%$: multiplier $= 1 - \dfrac{r}{100}$    e.g. $-35\% \to 0.65$
New amount $=$ original amount $\times$ multiplier
(a) Increase £64 by 15%: $64 \times 1.15 = \mathbf{£73.60}$.
(b) A sofa costing £380 is reduced by 35%: $380 \times 0.65 = \mathbf{£247}$.

Finding a percentage change

$$\text{Percentage change} = \frac{\text{actual change}}{\text{original value}} \times 100\%$$
Bus fares rise from £48 to £60 a month. Change $= 12$. Percentage increase $= \dfrac{12}{48} \times 100 = \mathbf{25\%}$.

A suitcase’s mass falls from 75 kg to 63 kg. Percentage decrease $= \dfrac{12}{75} \times 100 = \mathbf{16\%}$.
Divide by the original value. Using the new value (12 ÷ 63) gives 19.0%, which scores no accuracy marks.

Profit and loss

Percentage profit $= \dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100$. A market stall buys 60 scarves at £5 each (cost £300), sells 45 at £8 and the other 15 at £3 (takings $360 + 45 = £405$). Profit $= £105$, so the percentage profit is $\tfrac{105}{300} \times 100 = \mathbf{35\%}$.

3.2.3 Original Value Problems (R9)

In an original value (reverse percentage) problem you are told the amount after a percentage change. Because new $=$ original $\times$ multiplier, $$\text{original} = \frac{\text{new amount}}{\text{multiplier}}$$
After an 8% rise, a season ticket costs £540. What did it cost before?

$108\% = 540$, so original $= 540 \div 1.08 = \mathbf{£500}$.
In a sale a pair of boots costs £63 after a 30% reduction. Find the original price.

$70\% = 63$, so original $= 63 \div 0.7 = \mathbf{£90}$.
A bill of £78 includes VAT at 20%. The amount before VAT is $78 \div 1.2 = \mathbf{£65}$.
Do not take 20% of £78 and subtract it: $78 - 15.60 = £62.40$ is wrong, because the 20% was calculated on the price before VAT, not on £78.

Repeated percentage changes

Multiply the multipliers together to find a single overall multiplier.

A share price rises by 20% and then falls by 20%.

Overall multiplier $= 1.2 \times 0.8 = 0.96$, so the price ends at 96% of where it started: a 4% decrease.
Prices are cut by 25%, and then a further 10% is taken off the sale price.

$0.75 \times 0.9 = 0.675$: total reduction $= 100\% - 67.5\% = \mathbf{32.5\%}$ (not 35%).

3.2.4 Simple Interest in Financial Mathematics (R9)

With simple interest, interest is calculated on the original sum (the principal) only, so exactly the same interest is added each year.
$$\text{Simple interest} = P \times \frac{r}{100} \times n$$ $P$ = principal, $r$ = annual interest rate (%), $n$ = number of years
£1800 is invested for 4 years at 2.5% per year simple interest.

Interest each year $= 1800 \times 0.025 = £45$. Total interest $= 45 \times 4 = \mathbf{£180}$.
Value after 4 years $= 1800 + 180 = £1980$.

3.2.5 Growth, Decay and Compound Interest (R16)

With compound interest, each year’s interest is added to the balance and earns interest itself. Population growth, depreciation and radioactive decay follow the same pattern: the same percentage change is applied again and again.
$$\text{Value after } n \text{ periods} = P \times \left(1 \pm \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$ $+$ for growth or interest, $-$ for decay or depreciation.
£5000 is invested at 3% per year compound interest for 4 years.

$5000 \times 1.03^4 = 5627.544\ldots \approx \mathbf{£5627.54}$.
A car bought for £22 000 depreciates by 15% each year. Find its value after 3 years.

Multiplier $= 0.85$. Value $= 22\,000 \times 0.85^3 = \mathbf{£13\,510.75}$.
A machine costs £30 000. It loses 30% of its value in the first year and 10% in each later year. Value after 3 years:

$30\,000 \times 0.7 \times 0.9^2 = 30\,000 \times 0.567 = \mathbf{£17\,010}$.

Simple versus compound interest

Account A: £8000 at 5% simple interest for 4 years. Account B: £8000 at 4.8% compound interest for 4 years.

A: $8000 + 4 \times 400 = £9600$.   B: $8000 \times 1.048^4 = £9650.17$.
Account B pays about £50 more despite its lower rate, because the interest compounds.

How many years?

Substitute whole numbers of years into the formula and show the values either side of the target.

A herd of 2500 deer decreases by 6% each year. After how many years will there first be fewer than 2000 deer?

$2500 \times 0.94^3 = 2076.5$ (still 2000 or more); $2500 \times 0.94^4 = 1951.9$ (below 2000).
Answer: after 4 years.

Working backwards

After 3 years of 4% compound interest a balance is £5624.32. Original $= 5624.32 \div 1.04^3 = 5624.32 \div 1.124864 = \mathbf{£5000}$.
£6000 grows to £7146.10 in 3 years at $r\%$ per year compound interest. Find $r$.

$\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)^3 = \dfrac{7146.10}{6000} = 1.19101\ldots$, so $1 + \dfrac{r}{100} = \sqrt[3]{1.19101\ldots} = 1.06$ and $r = \mathbf{6}$.
“Compound”, “each year” and “depreciates by … per year” all signal a power. Check whether the question wants the final value or only the interest, and round money to 2 decimal places at the end.

3.2.6 General Iterative Processes (R16) Higher

The Higher version of R16 adds “work with general iterative processes”. These combine a percentage change with a fixed amount added or taken away each period, written as an iterative formula:

$$u_{n+1} = a\,u_n + b$$ $u_0$ is the starting value, $a$ is the multiplier for the percentage change, and $b$ is the fixed amount added ($b > 0$) or removed ($b < 0$) each period.
FormulaInterpretation
$u_{n+1} = 1.04u_n - 30$4% growth each period, then 30 removed
$u_{n+1} = 1.025u_n + 200$2.5% interest a year, then £200 paid in
$P_{n+1} = 0.85P_n + 120$15% of the population lost, then 120 added
$B_{n+1} = 1.005B_n - 400$loan at 0.5% interest per month with £400 repaid monthly
$u_{n+1} = 1.04u_n - 30$ and $u_0 = 800$. Find $u_2$.

$u_1 = 1.04 \times 800 - 30 = 832 - 30 = 802$
$u_2 = 1.04 \times 802 - 30 = 834.08 - 30 = \mathbf{804.08}$
A town’s population is modelled by $P_{n+1} = 1.1P_n - 60$, with $P_0 = 1000$ in 2025. In which year does the model first give a population above 1500?

$P_1 = 1040,\; P_2 = 1084,\; P_3 = 1132.4,\; P_4 = 1185.64,\; P_5 = 1244.20,\; P_6 = 1308.62,\; P_7 = 1379.49,\; P_8 = 1457.44,\; P_9 = 1543.18$
It first exceeds 1500 when $n = 9$, i.e. in 2034.

Long-term behaviour

If the terms settle towards a limit $L$, then $L$ satisfies $L = aL + b$.

$u_{n+1} = 0.75u_n + 20$. Long-term value: $L = 0.75L + 20 \Rightarrow 0.25L = 20 \Rightarrow L = \mathbf{80}$.
A £6000 loan is charged 0.5% interest per month and only £30 is repaid each month: $B_1 = 1.005 \times 6000 - 30 = 6000$. The monthly interest (£30) equals the repayment, so the balance never goes down.
On your calculator, enter $u_0$ and press =, then type $1.04 \times \text{Ans} - 30$ and keep pressing =. Write down every term you use so the examiner can award method marks, and do not round until the final answer.
