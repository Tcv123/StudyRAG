1.4 Powers, Roots, Surds & Standard Form

Pearson Edexcel GCSE Mathematics 1MA1 — Number — spec refs N6–N9 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

1.4.1 Powers and Roots (N6)

A power (or index) shows repeated multiplication: $a^n = a \times a \times \cdots \times a$ with $n$ factors. In $2^5$ the base is 2 and the index is 5, so $2^5 = 32$.

A root undoes a power: $\sqrt{121} = 11$ because $11^2 = 121$; $\sqrt[3]{125} = 5$ because $5^3 = 125$; $\sqrt[5]{32} = 2$ because $2^5 = 32$.

A positive number has two square roots, e.g. $\pm 9$ for 81, but the symbol $\sqrt{\ }$ always means the positive one. A negative number has a negative cube root: $\sqrt[3]{-27} = -3$.

Facts to Learn

N6 requires you to recognise powers of 2, 3, 4 and 5, as well as square and cube numbers.

Powers and roots come before multiplication and subtraction: $27 \times 4 - 9 = 108 - 9 = \mathbf{99}$

Estimating Powers and Roots Higher

The 1MA1 Higher version of N6 adds "estimate powers and roots of any given positive number". Trap the root between two known squares (or cubes), then test decimals.

Estimate $\sqrt{45}$ to 1 decimal place.

$6^2 = 36$ and $7^2 = 49$, so $6 < \sqrt{45} < 7$.
$6.7^2 = 44.89$ and $6.8^2 = 46.24$. 45 is much closer to 44.89, so $\sqrt{45} \approx \mathbf{6.7}$
Estimate $4.1^3$.

$4.1 \approx 4$, so $4.1^3 \approx 4^3 = 64$. This is an underestimate, because 4.1 > 4 (the true value is 68.921).

1.4.2 Calculating with Indices (N7)

$$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$ $$a^0 = 1 \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \qquad a^1 = a$$ These laws need the same base: $2^3 \times 5^4$ cannot be combined into one power.
Simplify $3^8 \times 3^{-3} \div 3^2$. Give your answer as a power of 3.

$3^{8 + (-3) - 2} = \mathbf{3^3}$ (which is 27)
Simplify $(5x^2)^3$.

Cube the number and the letter part: $5^3 \times x^{2 \times 3} = \mathbf{125x^6}$

Zero and Negative Indices

Foundation N7 covers all integer indices, so zero and negative powers are on both tiers.

The division law gives $7^2 \div 7^2 = 7^0$, but anything (non-zero) divided by itself is 1, so $7^0 = 1$.

Likewise $7^2 \div 7^5 = 7^{-3}$, while $\dfrac{49}{16\,807} = \dfrac{1}{343} = \dfrac{1}{7^3}$. So a negative index means one over (the reciprocal).

Work out (a) $5^{-2}$   (b) $\left(\tfrac{2}{5}\right)^{-2}$

(a) $\dfrac{1}{5^2} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{25}}$    (b) turn the fraction upside down, then square: $\left(\tfrac{5}{2}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\dfrac{25}{4}}$
Find the value of $n$ when $2^n = 32 \times 2^4$.

$32 = 2^5$, so $2^n = 2^5 \times 2^4 = 2^9$, giving $\mathbf{n = 9}$
A negative power gives a small positive answer, not a negative one: $5^{-2} = \frac{1}{25}$, not $-25$. Watch brackets too: $(-3)^2 = 9$ but $-3^2 = -9$.

Fractional Indices Higher

Higher N7 extends this to "integer and fractional indices".

$$a^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad\qquad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m \qquad\qquad a^{-\frac{m}{n}} = \frac{1}{\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m}$$ Bottom of the fraction = root; top = power; minus sign = reciprocal.
Work out $125^{\frac{2}{3}}$.

Root first: $\sqrt[3]{125} = 5$. Then power: $5^2 = \mathbf{25}$
Work out $36^{-\frac{1}{2}}$.

$\dfrac{1}{36^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{36}} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{6}}$
Work out $\left(\dfrac{64}{27}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}$.

Reciprocal: $\left(\dfrac{27}{64}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$. Cube root: $\dfrac{3}{4}$. Square: $\mathbf{\dfrac{9}{16}}$
Solve $4^x = \dfrac{1}{32}$.

Use base 2 on both sides: $4^x = 2^{2x}$ and $\dfrac{1}{32} = 2^{-5}$, so $2x = -5$ and $\mathbf{x = -\tfrac{5}{2}}$
Take the root before the power so the numbers stay manageable without a calculator: $16^{\frac{3}{4}} = 2^3 = 8$, rather than $\sqrt[4]{4096}$.

1.4.3 Calculating Exactly with Fractions and Multiples of $\pi$ (N8)

"Give your answer in terms of $\pi$" means keep $\pi$ as a symbol rather than using 3.14…; the answer is then exact. Exact fractions (such as $\frac{1}{3}$ rather than 0.333) are treated in the same way.

A circle has diameter 18 cm. Work out its exact area and its exact circumference.

$r = 9$: Area $= \pi \times 9^2 = \mathbf{81\pi \text{ cm}^2}$; Circumference $= \pi \times 18 = \mathbf{18\pi \text{ cm}}$
A circle of diameter 8 cm is cut from a square of side 8 cm. Work out the exact area remaining.

Square $= 64$; circle $= \pi \times 4^2 = 16\pi$. Remaining area $= \mathbf{64 - 16\pi \text{ cm}^2}$ (these are not like terms, so this is the final answer).
Handle $\pi$ like a letter: $4\pi + 7\pi = 11\pi$ and $3 \times 5\pi = 15\pi$, but $64 - 16\pi$ cannot be simplified to $48\pi$.

1.4.4 Surds (N8) Higher

Higher N8 adds "calculate exactly with surds; simplify surd expressions involving squares and rationalise denominators".

A surd is a root that is irrational — it cannot be written as a fraction — such as $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{7}$ or $\sqrt{20}$. Leaving answers as surds keeps them exact. ($\sqrt{16} = 4$ is not a surd.)
$$\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} \qquad \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} \qquad \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = a$$ Warning: $\sqrt{a + b} \ne \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$ (for example $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$, not 7).

Simplifying Surds

Split the number into the largest square factor times what is left.

Simplify $\sqrt{45}$ and $\sqrt{200}$.

$\sqrt{45} = \sqrt{9 \times 5} = \sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{5} = \mathbf{3\sqrt{5}}$
$\sqrt{200} = \sqrt{100 \times 2} = \mathbf{10\sqrt{2}}$
Simplify $\sqrt{50} + \sqrt{18} - \sqrt{8}$.

$5\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} - 2\sqrt{2} = \mathbf{6\sqrt{2}}$   (like surds are collected like like terms)

Expanding Brackets

Expand and simplify $(4 + \sqrt{5})^2$.

$(4 + \sqrt{5})(4 + \sqrt{5}) = 16 + 4\sqrt{5} + 4\sqrt{5} + 5 = \mathbf{21 + 8\sqrt{5}}$
Expand and simplify $(6 + \sqrt{7})(6 - \sqrt{7})$.

$36 - 6\sqrt{7} + 6\sqrt{7} - 7 = \mathbf{29}$   (the surd terms cancel: $a^2 - b$)

Rationalising the Denominator

To rationalise the denominator is to rewrite a fraction so that no surd is left on the bottom.

$$\frac{a}{\sqrt{b}} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{b}} \times \frac{\sqrt{b}}{\sqrt{b}} = \frac{a\sqrt{b}}{b}$$ $$\frac{a}{c - \sqrt{b}} \;\text{: multiply top and bottom by}\; c + \sqrt{b}\text{, making the denominator } c^2 - b$$
Rationalise the denominator of $\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{6}}$.

$\dfrac{12}{\sqrt{6}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{\sqrt{6}} = \dfrac{12\sqrt{6}}{6} = \mathbf{2\sqrt{6}}$
Rationalise the denominator of $\dfrac{8}{3 - \sqrt{5}}$.

$\dfrac{8(3 + \sqrt{5})}{(3 - \sqrt{5})(3 + \sqrt{5})} = \dfrac{24 + 8\sqrt{5}}{9 - 5} = \dfrac{24 + 8\sqrt{5}}{4} = \mathbf{6 + 2\sqrt{5}}$
"In the form $a\sqrt{b}$" means fully simplified, with $b$ as small as possible: $\sqrt{48} = 4\sqrt{3}$, not $2\sqrt{12}$.

1.4.5 Standard Form (N9)

A number is in standard form when it is written as $$A \times 10^n \quad \text{where } 1 \le A < 10 \text{ and } n \text{ is an integer}$$ Numbers of 10 or more have a positive power; numbers between 0 and 1 have a negative power.
Ordinary numberStandard form
640 000$6.4 \times 10^5$
7 300 000$7.3 \times 10^6$
0.0009$9 \times 10^{-4}$
0.0000481$4.81 \times 10^{-5}$

Multiplying and Dividing

Work with the number parts and the powers of 10 separately, then adjust so that $1 \le A < 10$.

Work out $(8 \times 10^6) \times (7 \times 10^2)$. Give your answer in standard form.

$8 \times 7 = 56$ and $10^6 \times 10^2 = 10^8$, giving $56 \times 10^8$.
56 is too big for $A$: $56 \times 10^8 = 5.6 \times 10^1 \times 10^8 = \mathbf{5.6 \times 10^9}$
Work out $(6.3 \times 10^4) \div (2.1 \times 10^{-3})$.

$6.3 \div 2.1 = 3$ and $10^4 \div 10^{-3} = 10^{4-(-3)} = 10^7$, so the answer is $\mathbf{3 \times 10^7}$

Adding and Subtracting

You cannot simply add the number parts unless the powers are the same, so write the numbers out in full (or with the same power of 10) first.

Work out $(5.1 \times 10^6) - (2.4 \times 10^5)$.

$5\,100\,000 - 240\,000 = 4\,860\,000 = \mathbf{4.86 \times 10^6}$

Calculator Displays

A calculator may show 2.9E-4 or 2.9×10⁻⁴; both mean $2.9 \times 10^{-4} = 0.00029$. Always write the answer properly — "2.9E-4" will not get the mark.

Jupiter is about $7.8 \times 10^8$ km from the Sun. Light travels at $3 \times 10^5$ km/s. How long does sunlight take to reach Jupiter?

$\text{time} = \dfrac{7.8 \times 10^8}{3 \times 10^5} = 2.6 \times 10^3 = 2600$ seconds. $2600 \div 60 = 43.3\ldots$, so the time is $\mathbf{43 \text{ minutes } 20 \text{ seconds}}$
Check your final $A$: $56 \times 10^8$ and $0.56 \times 10^{10}$ have the right value but are not standard form, and Edexcel mark schemes withhold the accuracy mark for them.
