Pearson Edexcel GCSE Mathematics 1MA1 — Algebra — spec refs A1–A4 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

2.1.1 Algebraic Notation (A1)

In algebra a letter stands for a number that may vary or may be unknown. Edexcel mark schemes expect answers written with the standard shorthand below, and a final answer left as, for example, $3 \times a \times a$ instead of $3a^2$ can lose the accuracy mark.

Long formStandard formComment
$p \times q$$pq$No multiplication sign; letters normally in alphabetical order
$m + m + m + m$ or $4 \times m$$4m$The number (coefficient) is written first
$t \times t$$t^2$“$t$ squared”
$t \times t \times t$$t^3$“$t$ cubed”
$p \times p \times q$$p^2q$The index belongs to $p$ only
$m \div n$$\dfrac{m}{n}$Division is written as a fraction
$0.25y$$\dfrac{1}{4}y$ or $\dfrac{y}{4}$Coefficients are given as fractions rather than decimals
$5 \times (y - 1)$$5(y - 1)$A bracket shows the whole of $y - 1$ is multiplied by 5
Watch where an index sits. $3m^2$ means $3 \times m \times m$, while $(3m)^2 = 3m \times 3m = 9m^2$. Likewise $-x^2$ with $x = 4$ is $-16$, but $(-x)^2$ is $16$.

2.1.2 Substituting into Expressions and Formulae (A2)

To substitute, replace every letter by its value and evaluate using the order of operations (brackets, indices, then division and multiplication, then addition and subtraction). Edexcel often uses formulae from science, finance or everyday life. You do not need to know them in advance, because everything you need is given in the question.

Work out the value of $2a^2 - 5b$ when $a = -3$ and $b = 4$.

$a^2 = (-3)^2 = 9$  (square first, and the result is positive)
$2 \times 9 - 5 \times 4 = 18 - 20$
$= -2$
The power, $P$ watts, used by an electrical component is given by $P = I^2R$. Work out $P$ when $I = 3$ and $R = 12$.

$I^2 = 3^2 = 9$
$P = 9 \times 12 = 108$ watts
Write the substitution out in full, with negative numbers in brackets, before you calculate. On Paper 1 this working earns the M1 even if the arithmetic slips.

2.1.3 Expressions, Equations, Formulae, Identities and Inequalities (A3)

Term: one part of an expression, made from numbers and letters multiplied together, e.g. $-6y$, $4x^2$, $9$.

Coefficient: the number multiplying a letter, e.g. in $-7x^2$ the coefficient of $x^2$ is $-7$.

Factor: a number, letter or bracket that divides exactly, e.g. $5$, $y$ and $5y$ are factors of $10y$; $(x + 3)$ is a factor of $x^2 + 7x + 12$.

Expression: terms combined with no equals sign, e.g. $6y + 1$.

Equation: a statement that two expressions are equal, true only for certain values, e.g. $4y - 3 = 9$.

Formula: a rule connecting different variables, e.g. $F = ma$.

Identity: true for all values of the letters, shown with $\equiv$, e.g. $2(y - 3) \equiv 2y - 6$.

Inequality: a statement using $<$, $>$, $\le$ or $\ge$, e.g. $y + 2 > 5$.
Name each statement: (A) $6y + 1$   (B) $4y - 3 = 9$   (C) $F = ma$   (D) $2(y - 3) \equiv 2y - 6$   (E) $y + 2 > 5$.

A: no equals sign → expression
B: only true when $y = 3$ → equation
C: links force, mass and acceleration → formula
D: true whatever $y$ is → identity
E: uses $>$ → inequality

2.1.4 Collecting Like Terms and the Laws of Indices (A4)

Like terms

Terms are like terms when they contain exactly the same letters with the same powers, for example $5ab$ and $-2ba$. Add or subtract their coefficients; unlike terms such as $3m$ and $3m^2$ stay separate.

Simplify $8m + 3n - 5m - 7n + 2$.

$m$ terms: $8m - 5m = 3m$
$n$ terms: $3n - 7n = -4n$
Answer: $3m - 4n + 2$

Laws of indices

$$x^a \times x^b = x^{a+b} \qquad x^a \div x^b = x^{a-b} \qquad (x^a)^b = x^{ab}$$ $$x^0 = 1 \qquad x^{-a} = \frac{1}{x^a}$$

When terms have coefficients, deal with the numbers and each letter separately: multiply (or divide) the numbers, then add (or subtract) the powers letter by letter.

Simplify $(2x^3y)^4 \div 4x^5y^2$.

$(2x^3y)^4 = 2^4 \times x^{12} \times y^4 = 16x^{12}y^4$  (the 2 is raised to the power 4 too)
$16 \div 4 = 4$,   $x^{12-5} = x^7$,   $y^{4-2} = y^2$
Answer: $4x^7y^2$
Simplify $6x^{-2} \times 3x^7$.

$6 \times 3 = 18$ and $-2 + 7 = 5$
Answer: $18x^5$

2.1.5 Expanding a Single Bracket and Taking Out Common Factors (A4)

Expanding

The term outside multiplies each term inside: $k(a - b) = ka - kb$. A negative term outside changes the sign of everything inside.

Expand and simplify $5(2x + 3) - 3(x - 4)$.

$5(2x + 3) = 10x + 15$
$-3(x - 4) = -3x + 12$  ($-3 \times -4 = +12$)
$10x + 15 - 3x + 12 = 7x + 27$

Factorising with a common factor

Factorising reverses expanding. Find the highest common factor of the numbers and of each letter, put it outside the bracket, and divide every term by it to fill the bracket.

Factorise fully $9a^2b + 15ab^3$.

HCF of 9 and 15 is 3; lowest power of $a$ is $a$; lowest power of $b$ is $b$
HCF $= 3ab$
$9a^2b \div 3ab = 3a$ and $15ab^3 \div 3ab = 5b^2$
Answer: $3ab(3a + 5b^2)$
“Factorise fully” means nothing more can be taken out. An answer such as $3(3a^2b + 5ab^3)$ is correct but incomplete and typically scores M1 A0.

2.1.6 Expanding Products of Two Binomials (A4)

A binomial has two terms, for example $(2x - 5)$. Multiplying two binomials gives four products, which you then simplify by collecting like terms. A grid keeps the four products in order.

Expand and simplify $(x - 4)(x + 9)$.

$x \times x = x^2$,   $x \times 9 = 9x$,   $-4 \times x = -4x$,   $-4 \times 9 = -36$
$x^2 + 9x - 4x - 36 = x^2 + 5x - 36$

Squares and the difference of two squares

$$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 \qquad (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$$ $$(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$$
Jo writes $(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 9$. Show that Jo is wrong and give the correct expansion.

Try $x = 5$: $(5 - 3)^2 = 4$, but $5^2 - 9 = 16$. The two sides are not equal, so Jo is wrong.
$(x - 3)(x - 3) = x^2 - 3x - 3x + 9 = x^2 - 6x + 9$
Always write a squared bracket out twice before expanding. Forgetting the middle term ($2ab$) is the commonest error on these questions.

2.1.7 Factorising $x^2 + bx + c$ and the Difference of Two Squares (A4)

For $x^2 + bx + c$, look for two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$. They become the numbers in the two brackets.

Factorise $x^2 + 10x + 21$.

Factor pairs of 21: $1 \times 21$, $3 \times 7$
$3 + 7 = 10$ ✓
Answer: $(x + 3)(x + 7)$
Factorise $x^2 - 4x - 21$.

Product $-21$, sum $-4$: the numbers are $-7$ and $+3$
Answer: $(x - 7)(x + 3)$  (check: $-7x + 3x = -4x$ ✓)
Signs in $x^2 + bx + c$Signs in the bracketsExample
$b$ and $c$ both positive$(x + \;)(x + \;)$$x^2 + 8x + 15 = (x + 3)(x + 5)$
$b$ negative, $c$ positive$(x - \;)(x - \;)$$x^2 - 8x + 15 = (x - 3)(x - 5)$
$c$ negativeone $+$ and one $-$$x^2 - 2x - 15 = (x - 5)(x + 3)$

Difference of two squares

Two square terms with a minus sign between them and no middle term factorise as

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$$
Factorise $x^2 - 121$.

$x^2 - 11^2 = (x + 11)(x - 11)$
Factorise fully $2x^2 + 4x - 30$.

Take out the common factor 2: $2(x^2 + 2x - 15)$
Product $-15$, sum $+2$: $+5$ and $-3$
Answer: $2(x + 5)(x - 3)$
Look for a common factor before anything else, then expand your brackets to check. A quick check catches almost every sign error.

2.1.8 Expanding Brackets Involving Surds (A4)

The 1MA1 specification includes expressions involving surds in A4 at both tiers. Treat a surd like a letter when expanding, and use $\sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = a$ to tidy up. (Simplifying surds such as $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$ and rationalising denominators are Higher-tier skills from N8.)

Expand and simplify $(\sqrt{7} + 3)(\sqrt{7} - 1)$.

$\sqrt{7} \times \sqrt{7} = 7$,   $\sqrt{7} \times (-1) = -\sqrt{7}$,   $3 \times \sqrt{7} = 3\sqrt{7}$,   $3 \times (-1) = -3$
$7 - \sqrt{7} + 3\sqrt{7} - 3 = 4 + 2\sqrt{7}$
Expand $(1 + \sqrt{3})^2$.

$(1 + \sqrt{3})(1 + \sqrt{3}) = 1 + \sqrt{3} + \sqrt{3} + 3$
$= 4 + 2\sqrt{3}$

Brackets in the form $(a + \sqrt{b})(a - \sqrt{b})$ give a whole number, because the surd terms cancel: $(\sqrt{6} + 2)(\sqrt{6} - 2) = 6 - 4 = 2$.

2.1.9 Factorising $ax^2 + bx + c$ and Expanding Three or More Binomials (A4) Higher

Factorising when the coefficient of $x^2$ is not 1

  1. Multiply $a$ by $c$.
  2. Find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$.
  3. Use them to split the $bx$ term into two terms.
  4. Factorise each pair, then take out the shared bracket.
Factorise $4x^2 + 4x - 15$.

$ac = 4 \times (-15) = -60$; product $-60$ and sum $4$: $+10$ and $-6$
$4x^2 + 10x - 6x - 15$
$2x(2x + 5) - 3(2x + 5)$
Answer: $(2x - 3)(2x + 5)$

The difference of two squares extends to terms with coefficients: $25x^2 - 9 = (5x + 3)(5x - 3)$.

Products of three binomials

Multiply two of the brackets together and simplify, then multiply every term of that result by each term of the third bracket.

Expand and simplify $(x + 1)(x - 3)(x + 5)$.

$(x + 1)(x - 3) = x^2 - 2x - 3$
$(x^2 - 2x - 3)(x + 5) = x^3 + 5x^2 - 2x^2 - 10x - 3x - 15$
$= x^3 + 3x^2 - 13x - 15$
Check a cubic expansion by substituting $x = 1$ into both forms: $(2)(-2)(6) = -24$ and $1 + 3 - 13 - 15 = -24$ ✓.

2.1.10 Algebraic Fractions (A4) Higher

Simplifying

Factorise the top and bottom completely, then cancel common factors (whole brackets). You can never cancel separate terms that are added or subtracted.

Simplify fully $\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{2x^2 + 5x - 3}$.

Numerator: $(x + 3)(x - 3)$
Denominator: $ac = -6$, numbers $6$ and $-1$: $2x^2 + 6x - x - 3 = 2x(x + 3) - 1(x + 3) = (2x - 1)(x + 3)$
Cancel $(x + 3)$: $\dfrac{x - 3}{2x - 1}$

Adding and subtracting

Write both fractions over a common denominator, multiply each numerator accordingly, then simplify the numerator.

Write $\dfrac{3}{x + 2} - \dfrac{1}{x - 4}$ as a single fraction in its simplest form.

Common denominator: $(x + 2)(x - 4)$
$\dfrac{3(x - 4) - (x + 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 4)}$
Numerator: $3x - 12 - x - 2 = 2x - 14 = 2(x - 7)$
Answer: $\dfrac{2(x - 7)}{(x + 2)(x - 4)}$

Multiplying and dividing

Factorise everything first. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction, then cancel.

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 + 5x + 6}{x^2 - 4} \div \dfrac{x + 3}{3x - 6}$.

$\dfrac{(x + 2)(x + 3)}{(x + 2)(x - 2)} \times \dfrac{3(x - 2)}{x + 3}$
Cancel $(x + 2)$, $(x + 3)$ and $(x - 2)$
Answer: $3$
Put the second numerator in brackets when subtracting: $-(x + 2) = -x - 2$. Missing this sign change costs the accuracy mark more often than any other slip.
