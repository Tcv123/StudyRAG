2.3 Linear Graphs & Coordinates

Pearson Edexcel GCSE Mathematics 1MA1 — Algebra — spec refs A8–A10, G11 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

This topic is about locating points anywhere on a coordinate grid (A8), drawing straight-line graphs and finding their equations, including lines that are parallel (A9), reading and interpreting gradients and intercepts both from graphs and from equations (A10), and solving geometry problems set on coordinate axes (G11). For 1MA1 the only Higher-tier extra is using $y = mx + c$ to recognise and construct perpendicular lines (the Higher wording of A9).

2.3.1 Coordinates in All Four Quadrants (A8)

Every point on a grid is described by an ordered pair $(x, y)$. The first number is the horizontal position (positive to the right of the origin, negative to the left); the second is the vertical position (positive above, negative below). The two axes meet at the origin $O(0, 0)$ and split the grid into four quadrants, numbered anticlockwise from the top right.
Read across first, then up or down. Edexcel often puts the axes in the middle of the grid, so check the sign of each coordinate: a point in the 2nd quadrant has a negative $x$ and a positive $y$.

Midpoint of a Line Segment

For the points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$, $$\text{midpoint} = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$ Each coordinate of the midpoint is the average of the two matching coordinates.
Work out the midpoint of $P(-6, 5)$ and $Q(2, -3)$.

$x = \dfrac{-6 + 2}{2} = -2, \quad y = \dfrac{5 + (-3)}{2} = 1$
Midpoint $= \mathbf{(-2, 1)}$
$A$ is $(3, -2)$ and the midpoint of $AB$ is $M(-1, 2)$. Work out the coordinates of $B$.

From $A$ to $M$ is $-4$ across and $+4$ up. Repeat the same step from $M$:
$B = (-1 - 4,\ 2 + 4) = \mathbf{(-5, 6)}$   (check: $2 \times (-1) - 3 = -5$ and $2 \times 2 - (-2) = 6$)

2.3.2 Geometrical Problems on Coordinate Axes (G11)

G11 questions put a shape on a grid and ask you to find a missing vertex, a length, an area or a property. Use what you know about the shape. In a parallelogram $ABCD$, the movement from $A$ to $B$ is identical to the movement from $D$ to $C$; in a rectangle or square, horizontal and vertical sides can be read straight off the grid.

$A(-2, 1)$, $B(3, 2)$ and $C(5, 6)$ are three vertices of parallelogram $ABCD$. Find $D$.

The step from $B$ to $C$ is $(+2, +4)$, so the step from $A$ to $D$ is the same:
$D = (-2 + 2,\ 1 + 4) = \mathbf{(0, 5)}$
The lines $y = 1$, $x = 7$ and $y = x + 1$ enclose a triangle. Work out its area.

$y = x + 1$ meets $y = 1$ at $(0, 1)$ and meets $x = 7$ at $(7, 8)$. The lines $x = 7$ and $y = 1$ meet at $(7, 1)$.
The triangle has a right angle at $(7, 1)$ with base $7$ and height $7$: area $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 7 = \mathbf{24.5}$ square units.
Sketch the points on a quick grid even when no diagram is given. A sketch shows you which sides are horizontal or vertical and catches answers that are obviously in the wrong place.

2.3.3 Plotting Straight-Line Graphs (A9)

An equation in which $x$ and $y$ appear only to the power 1 (no $x^2$, no $\frac{1}{x}$) gives a straight line. To draw it, complete a table of values, plot the points accurately and join them with a single ruled line.

$x$$-1$$0$$1$$2$$3$
$y = 4 - 3x$$7$$4$$1$$-2$$-5$
  • The $y$-values change by the same amount each time ($-3$ here). A value that breaks the pattern is an arithmetic slip.
  • Three points are enough; any more act as checks.
  • Extend the line across the full range of $x$-values given in the question.

Equations in the Form $ax + by = c$

Find where the line meets each axis. For $3x + 4y = 24$: setting $x = 0$ gives $4y = 24$, so $y = 6$; setting $y = 0$ gives $3x = 24$, so $x = 8$. Plot $(0, 6)$ and $(8, 0)$ and join them.

2.3.4 Gradients and Intercepts: $y = mx + c$ (A9, A10)

Any straight line that is not vertical has an equation of the form $$y = mx + c$$ The number $m$ is the gradient: how much $y$ increases when $x$ increases by 1. The number $c$ is the $y$-intercept: the line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, c)$.
Gradient from two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$: $$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$
  • Uphill from left to right: positive gradient. Downhill: negative gradient.
  • A horizontal line has gradient 0.
  • A larger value of $|m|$ means a steeper line.
Work out the gradient of the line through $(-3, 2)$ and $(5, -4)$.

$m = \dfrac{-4 - 2}{5 - (-3)} = \dfrac{-6}{8} = \mathbf{-\tfrac{3}{4}}$

Reading $m$ and $c$ from an Equation

Make $y$ the subject first; then the gradient is the number multiplying $x$ and the intercept is the constant term.

“Write down the gradient of $y = 9 - 4x$” is a one-mark question that many students drop by answering 9. The gradient is the coefficient of $x$, whichever order the terms are in.

Where a Line Crosses the $x$-axis

On the $x$-axis, $y = 0$. For $y = 3x - 12$: $0 = 3x - 12$ gives $x = 4$, so the line crosses at $(4, 0)$.

2.3.5 Finding the Equation of a Line (A9)

One Point and the Gradient

  1. Put the gradient into $y = mx + c$.
  2. Substitute the $x$ and $y$ of the point.
  3. Solve for $c$ and write out the full equation.
Find an equation of the line with gradient $-2$ that passes through $(3, 1)$.

$1 = -2 \times 3 + c \Rightarrow 1 = -6 + c \Rightarrow c = 7$
$\mathbf{y = -2x + 7}$

Two Points

  1. Work out the gradient from the two points.
  2. Substitute either point to find $c$.
  3. Check that the other point fits.
Find an equation of the line through $(-2, -7)$ and $(3, 8)$.

$m = \dfrac{8 - (-7)}{3 - (-2)} = \dfrac{15}{5} = 3$
$-7 = 3 \times (-2) + c \Rightarrow c = -1$,   so $\mathbf{y = 3x - 1}$
Check with $(3, 8)$: $3 \times 3 - 1 = 8$ ✓
Find the line through $(-1, 4)$ and $(5, 0)$. Give your answer in the form $ax + by = c$ where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are integers.

$m = \dfrac{0 - 4}{5 - (-1)} = \dfrac{-4}{6} = -\tfrac{2}{3}$
$4 = -\tfrac{2}{3}(-1) + c = \tfrac{2}{3} + c \Rightarrow c = \tfrac{10}{3}$
$y = -\tfrac{2}{3}x + \tfrac{10}{3} \Rightarrow 3y = -2x + 10 \Rightarrow \mathbf{2x + 3y = 10}$

Parallel Lines

Parallel lines have equal gradients. For example, $y = 5x - 1$ and $2y = 10x + 7$ (which is $y = 5x + 3.5$) are parallel because both have gradient 5.
Find an equation of the line parallel to $y = 5x + 2$ that passes through $(-2, 3)$.

Same gradient, so $m = 5$.   $3 = 5 \times (-2) + c \Rightarrow c = 13$
$\mathbf{y = 5x + 13}$
To decide whether lines are parallel, rearrange every equation into $y = mx + c$ before comparing. $4x + 2y = 7$ has gradient $-2$, not 4.

2.3.6 Interpreting Gradients and Intercepts in Context (A10)

When a straight-line graph models a real situation, the gradient is a rate of change (units of the vertical axis per unit of the horizontal axis) and the intercept is the value when the horizontal quantity is zero, often a fixed charge or starting amount. Reading a gradient as a rate of change also links to R14 in the Ratio strand.

An electrician charges a call-out fee plus an hourly rate. A 2-hour job costs £115 and a 6-hour job costs £235. Work out the hourly rate and the call-out fee.

Gradient $= \dfrac{235 - 115}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{120}{4} = 30$, so the hourly rate is £30 per hour.
$115 = 30 \times 2 + c \Rightarrow c = 55$, so the call-out fee is £55.   Model: $C = 30h + 55$.
“Interpret the gradient” needs a sentence with units and context, for example “the volume of water decreases by 20 litres every minute”. A bare number scores nothing.

2.3.7 Perpendicular Lines (A9) Higher

Two lines are perpendicular (cross at $90°$) when their gradients multiply to give $-1$: $$m_1 \times m_2 = -1 \qquad\text{so}\qquad m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$$ To get the perpendicular gradient, take the reciprocal and change the sign (the negative reciprocal).

$\tfrac{1}{3}$$-3$

$\tfrac{2}{5}$$-\tfrac{5}{2}$
Find an equation of the line perpendicular to $y = 4x + 1$ that passes through $(8, -3)$.

Perpendicular gradient $= -\tfrac{1}{4}$.   $-3 = -\tfrac{1}{4} \times 8 + c \Rightarrow -3 = -2 + c \Rightarrow c = -1$
$\mathbf{y = -\tfrac{1}{4}x - 1}$   (or $x + 4y = -4$)
Show that the lines $4y = 3x - 8$ and $3y + 4x = 6$ are perpendicular.

$4y = 3x - 8 \Rightarrow y = \tfrac{3}{4}x - 2$, gradient $\tfrac{3}{4}$.   $3y + 4x = 6 \Rightarrow y = -\tfrac{4}{3}x + 2$, gradient $-\tfrac{4}{3}$.
$\tfrac{3}{4} \times \left(-\tfrac{4}{3}\right) = -1$, so the lines are perpendicular.

Perpendicular Bisectors

The perpendicular bisector of $AB$ goes through the midpoint of $AB$ at right angles to $AB$. You need both facts: the midpoint gives a point on the line, the negative reciprocal gives its gradient.

Find an equation of the perpendicular bisector of $A(-1, 2)$ and $B(5, 6)$.

Midpoint $= (2, 4)$.   Gradient $AB = \dfrac{6 - 2}{5 - (-1)} = \dfrac{2}{3}$, so the perpendicular gradient is $-\tfrac{3}{2}$.
$4 = -\tfrac{3}{2} \times 2 + c \Rightarrow c = 7$   $\Rightarrow$   $\mathbf{y = -\tfrac{3}{2}x + 7}$   (or $3x + 2y = 14$)

Using Gradients to Prove Properties of Shapes

$A(0, 3)$, $B(4, 5)$, $C(6, 1)$ and $D(2, -1)$. Prove that $ABCD$ is a rectangle.

$m_{AB} = \dfrac{2}{4} = \tfrac{1}{2}$,   $m_{DC} = \dfrac{1 - (-1)}{6 - 2} = \tfrac{1}{2}$,   $m_{BC} = \dfrac{1 - 5}{6 - 4} = -2$,   $m_{AD} = \dfrac{-1 - 3}{2 - 0} = -2$.
Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, and $\tfrac{1}{2} \times (-2) = -1$ so neighbouring sides are perpendicular. Therefore $ABCD$ is a rectangle.
Check a perpendicular gradient by multiplying it by the original: the answer must be exactly $-1$. Changing only the sign ($4 \to -4$) or only flipping ($4 \to \tfrac{1}{4}$) are the two classic errors.
