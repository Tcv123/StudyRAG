# Shared A-level maths content drawn from the OCR B (MEI) deck; board-specific cards excluded.

## 1.1
What is proof by deduction? ~~ Proceeding from given assumptions through a series of logical steps to a conclusion.
What is proof by exhaustion? ~~ Establishing a result by checking every case of a finite set of cases, having first shown the cases cover all possibilities.
What is proof by contradiction? ~~ Assume the statement is false, reason correctly from that assumption, and derive an impossibility. The assumption must therefore be wrong.
How do you disprove a general statement? ~~ Give a single counter-example — one case for which the statement fails. One example never proves a general statement, but one counter-example always disproves it.
What does P ⇒ Q mean, and how does it differ from P ⇔ Q? ~~ P ⇒ Q means P implies Q. P ⇔ Q means each implies the other. Writing ⇔ requires both directions to hold.
What is the contrapositive of 'if P then Q', and why is it useful? ~~ 'If not Q then not P'. It is logically equivalent to the original, so proving it proves the original — often easier than a direct argument.
What is the converse of 'if P then Q'? Is it always true? ~~ 'If Q then P'. It is a different statement and need not be true, confusing the two is a common error.
Give the counter-example that disproves 'every prime is odd'. ~~ 2 is prime and even.
Give the counter-example that disproves 'n² − n + 41 is prime for every positive integer n'. ~~ n = 41, where the expression equals 41², divisible by 41. It does give primes for n = 1 to 40, which is why testing values is not proof.
Outline the proof that √2 is irrational. ~~ Assume √2 = p/q in lowest terms. Then p² = 2q², so p is even, writing p = 2k gives q² = 2k², so q is even. Both even contradicts lowest terms.
Why is 'in its lowest terms' essential to the √2 proof? ~~ It is the assumption the final contradiction violates. Without it, showing p and q are both even contradicts nothing and the proof fails.
Why can the √2 argument not be copied to prove √4 irrational? ~~ It would need 'p² divisible by 4 ⇒ p divisible by 4', which is false (take p = 2). The proof breaks exactly where it must, since √4 = 2 is rational.
Outline Euclid's proof that there are infinitely many primes. ~~ Assume a finite list p₁…pₙ and form N = p₁p₂⋯pₙ + 1. No prime in the list divides N, so N is prime or has a prime factor outside the list — contradicting completeness.
In Euclid's proof, must N = p₁⋯pₙ + 1 be prime? ~~ No. It need only have no factor in the list. With 2,3,5,7,11,13 you get 30031 = 59 × 509 — composite, but both factors lie outside the list.
What is the negation of 'all swans are white'? ~~ 'There exists a swan that is not white.' It is not 'no swans are white', which is a much stronger claim.
What is a conjecture? ~~ A statement believed to be true but not yet proved.
What does "if and only if" require you to prove? ~~ Both directions: P ⇒ Q and Q ⇒ P.
Prove that the sum of two odd numbers is even. ~~ Let the odd numbers be 2m + 1 and 2n + 1. Their sum is 2m + 2n + 2 = 2(m + n + 1), which is even.
How do you represent an even and an odd integer algebraically? ~~ Even: 2n. Odd: 2n + 1 (or 2n − 1), where n is an integer.
How do you represent consecutive integers? ~~ n, n + 1, n + 2, … where n is an integer.
Prove that the square of any odd number is odd. ~~ (2n + 1)² = 4n² + 4n + 1 = 2(2n² + 2n) + 1, which is odd.
Prove that n³ − n is always divisible by 6. ~~ n³ − n = (n − 1)n(n + 1), the product of three consecutive integers. One is a multiple of 3 and at least one is even, so the product is divisible by 6.
What is the first step in a proof by contradiction? ~~ Assume the negation of the statement you want to prove.
How do you prove there is no largest even number? ~~ Assume N is the largest even number. Then N + 2 is even and larger, a contradiction.
When is proof by exhaustion practical? ~~ When there is a small finite number of cases to check, e.g. all integers from 1 to 10, or cases such as n odd and n even.
What does ∴ mean in a proof? ~~ "Therefore".
What does the symbol ∈ mean? ~~ "Is an element of", e.g. n ∈ ℤ means n is an integer.
What do ℕ, ℤ, ℚ and ℝ represent? ~~ Natural numbers, integers, rational numbers and real numbers respectively.
Why is a single example not a proof of a general statement? ~~ It only shows the statement holds in that case; a proof must cover every case.

## 1.2
How do you simplify a surd such as √50? ~~ Extract the largest square factor: √50 = √(25 × 2) = 5√2.
How do you rationalise a denominator of the form a + √b? ~~ Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate a − √b, using the difference of two squares to clear the surd.
Is √(a + b) equal to √a + √b? ~~ No. The product rule holds but the sum rule does not: √(9 + 16) = 5 while √9 + √16 = 7.
State the laws of indices for multiplication, division and powers. ~~ xᵃ × xᵇ = x^(a+b), xᵃ ÷ xᵇ = x^(a−b). (xᵃ)ᵇ = x^(ab).
What are x⁰, x^(−a) and x^(m/n)? ~~ x⁰ = 1 for x ≠ 0, x^(−a) = 1/xᵃ, x^(m/n) = (ⁿ√x)^m — take the root first to keep the numbers small.
What is the discriminant, and what do its three cases tell you? ~~ b² − 4ac. Positive: two distinct real roots. Zero: one repeated root. Negative: no real roots.
What is the difference between 'two distinct real roots' and 'real roots'? ~~ Distinct requires b² − 4ac > 0, 'real roots' usually allows the repeated case, so b² − 4ac ≥ 0. The wording decides < or ≤ in the answer.
What is the condition for a line to be tangent to a curve? ~~ Substitute the line into the curve and set the discriminant of the resulting quadratic to zero.
How do you complete the square for x² + bx + c? ~~ Write (x + b/2)² and subtract (b/2)², then add c. For x² + 6x + 2 this gives (x + 3)² − 7, so the minimum is at (−3, −7).
How do you solve one linear and one quadratic simultaneous equation? ~~ Always substitute the linear equation into the quadratic, then substitute the roots back into the linear equation and pair the values correctly.
How do you solve a quadratic inequality such as x² − 4x − 5 > 0? ~~ Factorise to find the critical values, then read the sign from the parabola's shape. Upward parabola: positive outside the roots, so x < −1 or x > 5.
Why can't you multiply an inequality through by (x − 3)? ~~ Its sign is unknown, so the inequality may or may not reverse. Instead collect over a common denominator, or multiply by the square (x − 3)², which is always positive.
When do two solution intervals need 'or' rather than 'and'? ~~ Always, for two separate intervals. 'x < −1 and x > 5' describes no numbers at all, the union needs 'or'.
Write the four proportion relationships and their equations. ~~ y ∝ x gives y = kx, y ∝ 1/x gives y = k/x, y ∝ xⁿ gives y = kxⁿ, y ∝ 1/x² gives y = k/x².
What is the commonest error with repeated factors in partial fractions? ~~ Omitting the A/(x+a) term and using only B/(x+a)². The system then has no solution.
Simplify √12 + √27. ~~ 2√3 + 3√3 = 5√3.
State the factor theorem. ~~ If f(a) = 0 then (x − a) is a factor of f(x), and conversely.
State the remainder theorem. ~~ The remainder when f(x) is divided by (x − a) is f(a). A remainder of zero means (x − a) is a factor.
To test whether (x + 3) is a factor, which value do you substitute? ~~ x = −3, since (x + 3) = (x − (−3)). Substituting +3 is the standard sign error.
When hunting for a first factor of a cubic, which values should you test? ~~ The divisors of the constant term. For a constant of 6, test ±1, ±2, ±3, ±6.
What is the domain and what is the range of a function? ~~ The domain is the set of permitted inputs, the range is the set of outputs the function actually takes.
What are the two usual causes of a restricted domain? ~~ You cannot divide by zero, and you cannot take the square root of a negative number.
How do you find the range of a quadratic? ~~ Complete the square. For f(x) = x² − 4x + 7 = (x − 2)² + 3, the range is f(x) ≥ 3.
What does fg(x) mean? ~~ f(g(x)) — apply g first, then f. The inner function is the one written next to the x.
Is composition of functions commutative? ~~ No. With f(x) = 2x + 3 and g(x) = x², fg(x) = 2x² + 3 but gf(x) = (2x + 3)².
When does an inverse function exist? ~~ Only when the function is one-to-one. A many-to-one function such as x² needs its domain restricted first.
How do you find an inverse function? ~~ Write y = f(x), rearrange to make x the subject, then swap the letters. Use the given domain to choose the sign of any square root.
How are the graphs of f and f⁻¹ related? ~~ Each is the reflection of the other in the line y = x. Domain and range swap, and for an INCREASING function any intersection lies on y = x.
Define |x| as a piecewise function. ~~ |x| = x for x ≥ 0, and |x| = −x for x < 0. The graph is a V with vertex at the origin.
How do you obtain y = |f(x)| from y = f(x)? ~~ Reflect the parts below the x-axis up into it, leaving the rest unchanged. It acts on the OUTPUT.

## 1.3
Write down the gradient, midpoint and distance formulae. ~~ Gradient (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁), midpoint ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2), distance √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²).
What is the relationship between perpendicular gradients? ~~ Their product is −1, so one is the negative reciprocal of the other. The rule fails for a vertical line, whose gradient is undefined.
How do you show three points are collinear? ~~ Show the gradient between successive pairs is equal, and note they share a common point.
Write down the equation of a circle with centre (a, b) and radius r. ~~ (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r². Note the signs: centre (2, −3) gives (x − 2)² + (y + 3)².
How do you find the centre and radius from x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0? ~~ Complete the square in both x and y. The right-hand side is then r², so remember to take the square root.
What is the commonest error when reading a circle equation? ~~ Quoting r² as the radius. From (x − 3)² + (y + 2)² = 25 the radius is 5, not 25.
How do you find a circle's equation from the endpoints of a diameter? ~~ The centre is the midpoint, the radius is half the distance between the endpoints.
How do you tell whether a point is inside, on, or outside a circle? ~~ Substitute the coordinates and compare with r². Greater than r² means outside, less means inside.
How do you find the equation of a tangent to a circle at a given point? ~~ Find the gradient of the radius to that point, take its negative reciprocal for the tangent, then use the point-gradient form.
How do you find how many times a line meets a circle? ~~ Substitute the line into the circle and look at the discriminant of the resulting quadratic: two roots, one (tangent), or none.
What are parametric equations? ~~ x and y are each given as functions of a third variable, the parameter. Each parameter value gives one point on the curve.
Why are parametric equations useful? ~~ They describe curves that cannot be written as y = f(x), such as a full circle, which fails the one-output-per-input test.
How do you convert parametric equations to Cartesian form? ~~ Eliminate the parameter. Rearrange the SIMPLER equation for the parameter, or use sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for trigonometric parameters.
What is dy/dx for a parametric curve? ~~ dy/dx = (dy/dt) ÷ (dx/dt). Getting the fraction the wrong way up is a standard error.
Write down the parametric equations of a circle. ~~ x = a + r cos θ and y = b + r sin θ, for centre (a, b) and radius r. Eliminating θ with cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 returns the Cartesian form.
Find the equation of the line through (1, 2) with gradient 3. ~~ y − 2 = 3(x − 1), so y = 3x − 1.
What is the gradient of the line 2x + 3y = 6? ~~ Rearrange to y = −2x/3 + 2, so gradient = −2/3.
Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of A(0, 0) and B(4, 2). ~~ Midpoint (2, 1); gradient AB = 1/2, so perpendicular gradient = −2. y − 1 = −2(x − 2), so y = −2x + 5.
What is the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6)? ~~ √((4 − 1)² + (6 − 2)²) = √(9 + 16) = 5.
Find the centre and radius of x² + y² − 4x + 6y − 12 = 0. ~~ (x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 25: centre (2, −3), radius 5.
What does the angle in a semicircle theorem state? ~~ The angle subtended at the circumference by a diameter is a right angle.
What does the perpendicular from the centre to a chord do? ~~ It bisects the chord.
What is the relationship between a tangent and a radius? ~~ A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
How do you find the equation of a circle through three points? ~~ Find the perpendicular bisectors of two chords; they intersect at the centre. Then find the radius.
Convert x = t², y = 2t into Cartesian form. ~~ t = y/2, so x = y²/4, i.e. y² = 4x.
Find dy/dx for x = t², y = t³. ~~ dy/dx = (3t²)/(2t) = 3t/2.
What is the parametric form of the circle (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²? ~~ x = a + r cos θ, y = b + r sin θ.
How do you find where a parametric curve meets the x-axis? ~~ Set y = 0, solve for t, then substitute into x.
What does it mean if a line meets a circle at exactly one point? ~~ The line is a tangent to the circle (the discriminant is zero).

## 1.4
Write down the nth term and sum formulae for an arithmetic sequence. ~~ uₙ = a + (n − 1)d. Sₙ = (n/2)(2a + (n − 1)d), or (n/2)(a + l) using the last term.
Write down the nth term and sum formulae for a geometric sequence. ~~ uₙ = arⁿ⁻¹. Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) for r ≠ 1.
When does a geometric series converge, and what is its sum to infinity? ~~ It converges if and only if |r| < 1, and then S∞ = a/(1 − r). Always state the condition.
What happens if you use the sum-to-infinity formula when |r| ≥ 1? ~~ It produces a plausible-looking number that is meaningless, because the series diverges.
What is a recurrence relation? ~~ A rule defining each term from the previous one, together with a starting value — for example u₍ₙ₊₁₎ = uₙ + 3 with u₁ = 2.
How do you find the limit of a convergent recurrence relation? ~~ Replace both u₍ₙ₊₁₎ and uₙ by L and solve for L, rejecting any root inconsistent with the sequence.
Write the binomial expansion of (1 + x)ⁿ for a positive integer n. ~~ 1 + nx + [n(n−1)/2!]x² + [n(n−1)(n−2)/3!]x³ + … It terminates, giving exactly n + 1 terms.
What is the standard error when expanding (1 + 2x)⁵? ~~ Forgetting to raise the coefficient too: (2x)³ = 8x³, not 2x³.
How do you find a particular coefficient in (a + b)ⁿ? ~~ Use the term ⁿCᵣ a^(n−r) b^r, choosing r to give the required power.
What changes when the binomial index is negative or fractional? ~~ The expansion no longer terminates — it becomes an infinite series — and it is valid only for |x| < 1.
What is the validity condition for expanding (1 + ax)ⁿ with non-integer n? ~~ |ax| < 1, that is |x| < 1/|a|. It is NOT |x| < 1 whenever there is a coefficient on x.
Expand (1 + x)^(1/2) as far as the term in x². ~~ 1 + x/2 − x²/8.
How do you expand √(4 + x) using the binomial series? ~~ Factorise first: √(4 + x) = 2(1 + x/4)^(1/2). The series cannot be applied to a bracket not starting with 1.
How do you find the nth term given the sum of the first n terms? ~~ uₙ = Sₙ − S₍ₙ₋₁₎. If Sₙ = n² then uₙ = n² − (n − 1)² = 2n − 1.
What are Σ from k=1 to n of k, and of (2k − 1)? ~~ n(n + 1)/2 and n² respectively.
What is the commonest confusion in sequences questions? ~~ Mixing up uₙ (a single term) with Sₙ (a running total). Read the question twice.
Find the 10th term of the arithmetic sequence 3, 7, 11, … ~~ a = 3, d = 4: u₁₀ = 3 + 9 × 4 = 39.
Find the sum of the first 20 terms of 3, 7, 11, … ~~ S₂₀ = 20/2 × (2 × 3 + 19 × 4) = 10 × 82 = 820.
Find the sum to infinity of 8 + 4 + 2 + … ~~ a = 8, r = 1/2: S∞ = 8/(1 − 1/2) = 16.
What is a periodic sequence? ~~ A sequence that repeats after a fixed number of terms, e.g. 1, −1, 1, −1, …
What is an increasing sequence? ~~ Each term is larger than the previous one: uₙ₊₁ > uₙ for all n.
What is a divergent sequence? ~~ A sequence that doesn't tend to a finite limit.
What is sigma notation? ~~ A shorthand for sums: Σ from r=1 to n of f(r) means f(1) + f(2) + … + f(n).
What is ⁿCᵣ? ~~ The number of ways of choosing r items from n: n!/(r!(n − r)!).
Find the coefficient of x³ in (2 + x)⁵. ~~ ⁵C₃ × 2² × x³ = 10 × 4 = 40.
Expand (1 − x)⁻¹ up to x³. ~~ 1 + x + x² + x³, valid for |x| < 1.
Expand (1 + 2x)⁻² up to x². ~~ 1 − 4x + 12x², valid for |x| < 1/2.
What is Pascal's triangle used for? ~~ To find binomial coefficients for small positive integer powers.
What is n! ? ~~ n × (n − 1) × … × 2 × 1, with 0! = 1.
How do you find the number of terms in an arithmetic sequence? ~~ Use uₙ = a + (n − 1)d, set it equal to the last term and solve for n.

## 1.5
What is a radian, and how does it relate to degrees? ~~ The angle subtended at the centre by an arc equal in length to the radius. π radians = 180°.
Write down the arc length and sector area formulae. ~~ s = rθ and A = ½r²θ, both requiring θ in RADIANS. Using degrees gives an answer wrong by a factor of about 57.
State the sine rule, the cosine rule and the triangle area formula. ~~ a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A. Area = ½ab sin C.
When do you use the cosine rule rather than the sine rule? ~~ With three sides, or two sides and the angle between them. Otherwise use the sine rule.
What is the ambiguous case of the sine rule? ~~ Given two sides and a non-included angle, sin θ = k has two solutions in 0° to 180°: θ and 180° − θ. Both must be checked.
Give the exact values of sin, cos and tan at 30°, 45° and 60°. ~~ sin: ½, √2/2, √3/2.  cos: √3/2, √2/2, ½.  tan: 1/√3, 1, √3.
How do you find the second solution of sin x = k, cos x = k and tan x = k? ~~ For sin: π − x. For cos: 2π − x. For tan: x + π.
How do you solve an equation such as sin 2x = k over 0° ≤ x < 360°? ~~ Widen the range FIRST: 2x runs over 0° to 720°. Solve there, then halve every answer. Halving first loses half the solutions.
Define sec, cosec and cot. ~~ sec θ = 1/cos θ, cosec θ = 1/sin θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ. Look at the third letter: se-c goes with c-os.
State the two identities derived from sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. ~~ 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ (divide by cos²θ), 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ (divide by sin²θ).
Write down the compound angle formulae for sin(A ± B) and cos(A ± B). ~~ sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B, cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B — note the sign flip.
Write down the three forms of cos 2θ. ~~ cos²θ − sin²θ, 2cos²θ − 1, 1 − 2sin²θ. Choose the form matching the other function in the equation.
What are sin 2θ and tan 2θ? ~~ sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ, tan 2θ = 2tan θ/(1 − tan²θ).
How do you write a sin x + b cos x as a single trigonometric function? ~~ As R sin(x + α) with R = √(a² + b²) and tan α = b/a. The expression then ranges between −R and R.
State the small angle approximations. ~~ For small θ in RADIANS: sin θ ≈ θ, tan θ ≈ θ, cos θ ≈ 1 − θ²/2. Also (sin θ)/θ → 1 as θ → 0.
What goes wrong if you divide a trigonometric equation by cos x? ~~ You lose every solution where cos x = 0. Factorise instead of dividing, and check those cases separately.
Convert 150° to radians. ~~ 150 × π/180 = 5π/6.
Find the arc length of a sector with radius 6 cm and angle π/3. ~~ s = rθ = 6 × π/3 = 2π cm.
Find the area of a sector with radius 6 cm and angle π/3. ~~ A = ½r²θ = ½ × 36 × π/3 = 6π cm².
What is the exact value of sin(π/6)? ~~ 1/2.
What is the exact value of cos(π/4)? ~~ √2/2 (or 1/√2).
What is the exact value of tan(π/3)? ~~ √3.
What is the period of y = sin x and y = tan x? ~~ sin x has period 2π (360°); tan x has period π (180°).
What is the identity linking tan, sin and cos? ~~ tan θ = sin θ/cos θ.
What is 1 + tan²θ equal to? ~~ sec²θ.
What is 1 + cot²θ equal to? ~~ cosec²θ.
Solve sin x = 0.5 for 0 ≤ x < 2π. ~~ x = π/6 or x = 5π/6.
What are the principal value ranges of arcsin, arccos and arctan? ~~ arcsin: −π/2 to π/2; arccos: 0 to π; arctan: −π/2 to π/2 (exclusive).
Write sin 3x cos x + cos 3x sin x as a single term. ~~ sin(3x + x) = sin 4x.
Express 3 sin x + 4 cos x as R sin(x + α). ~~ R = 5, tan α = 4/3, so α ≈ 0.927 rad (53.1°): 5 sin(x + 0.927).

## 1.6
What is the relationship between aˣ and log_a x? ~~ They are inverse functions: x = a^y is equivalent to y = log_a x. Their graphs are reflections in y = x.
State the three laws of logarithms. ~~ log(xy) = log x + log y, log(x/y) = log x − log y, log(xᵏ) = k log x.
Is there a law for log(x + y)? ~~ No. The addition law applies to a PRODUCT inside the logarithm, not a sum. log(x + y) ≠ log x + log y.
What are log_a a and log_a 1? ~~ log_a a = 1 and log_a 1 = 0.
How do you solve an equation such as 3ˣ = 20? ~~ Take logarithms of both sides, then use the power law to bring x down: x = log 20 ÷ log 3 ≈ 2.727.
What must you check after solving a logarithmic equation? ~~ That each solution keeps every logarithm's argument positive. Reject any root that makes an argument zero or negative.
What is special about e? ~~ It is the base for which the gradient of y = eˣ equals eˣ itself. More generally the gradient of e^(kx) is k e^(kx).
Why does the exponential model suit so many situations? ~~ Because the derivative of e^(kt) is proportional to the function itself — exactly the condition 'rate of change proportional to current size'.
What is ln x, and what are ln e and e^(ln x)? ~~ ln x is log to base e, the inverse of eˣ. ln e = 1, and e^(ln x) = x for x > 0.
How do you reduce y = axⁿ to linear form? ~~ Take logs: log y = log a + n log x. Plot log y against log x — gradient n, intercept log a.
How do you reduce y = abˣ to linear form? ~~ Take logs: log y = log a + x log b. Plot log y against PLAIN x — gradient log b, intercept log a.
How do you tell a power law from an exponential law using graphs? ~~ A power law gives a straight line on log–log axes, an exponential law gives one with log y against plain x. The difference is whether the horizontal axis carries a logarithm.
Write down the exponential growth and decay models. ~~ N = N₀e^(kt) for growth and N = N₀e^(−kt) for decay, with k > 0 and N₀ the value at t = 0.
How do you find the decay constant from a half-life T? ~~ Set e^(−kT) = ½, so kT = ln 2 and k = (ln 2)/T.
Give two criticisms of an exponential population growth model. ~~ It predicts unbounded growth, ignoring finite resources, and it assumes k stays constant, though it may drift with conditions. A logistic model adds a carrying capacity.
Solve 2ˣ = 8. ~~ x = 3.
Solve eˣ = 5. ~~ x = ln 5.
Simplify log 8 + log 5 − log 4. ~~ log(8 × 5/4) = log 10.
Write 2 log x − log y as a single log. ~~ log(x²/y).
What is the gradient of y = eᵏˣ? ~~ keᵏˣ.
What is log_a aˣ equal to? ~~ x.
What is e^(ln 7)? ~~ 7.
What is ln(e³)? ~~ 3.
Solve ln x = 2. ~~ x = e².
What is the change of base formula? ~~ log_a b = log_c b / log_c a.
What is the asymptote of y = eˣ? ~~ y = 0 (the x-axis).
What is the asymptote of y = ln x? ~~ x = 0 (the y-axis).
If y = axⁿ, what does a log–log graph give? ~~ log y = log a + n log x: a straight line with gradient n and intercept log a.
If y = abˣ, what does plotting log y against x give? ~~ log y = log a + x log b: a straight line with gradient log b and intercept log a.

## 1.7
Write down the definition of the derivative as a limit. ~~ f′(x) = limit as h → 0 of (f(x + h) − f(x))/h — the gradient of a chord becoming the gradient of the tangent.
Why must you cancel the h before letting h → 0? ~~ Substituting h = 0 first gives 0/0, which is undefined. Cancelling is the whole point of the method.
What are the derivatives of xⁿ, eˣ and ln x? ~~ nxⁿ⁻¹, eˣ, 1/x.
What are the derivatives of sin x, cos x and tan x? ~~ cos x, −sin x, sec²x. These hold only when x is in RADIANS.
Why do the trigonometric derivatives require radians? ~~ They follow from the limit (sin h)/h → 1, which is true only in radians. In degrees an extra constant factor appears.
State the chain rule. ~~ The derivative of f(g(x)) is f′(g(x)) × g′(x) — the outer derivative times the inner derivative.
State the product rule. ~~ The derivative of uv is u′v + uv′.
State the quotient rule. ~~ The derivative of u/v is (u′v − uv′)/v². Note the minus, and that the order matters — reversing it flips every sign.
How do you differentiate implicitly? ~~ Differentiate both sides with respect to x, applying the chain rule to every y term so each contributes a factor dy/dx, then collect and solve.
Why does a dy/dx appear when differentiating a y term? ~~ Because y is itself a function of x, so the chain rule applies: differentiating f(y) with respect to x gives f′(y) × dy/dx.
How do you differentiate an inverse function? ~~ Use dy/dx = 1 ÷ (dx/dy). For y = ln x, write x = e^y, so dx/dy = e^y = x and dy/dx = 1/x.
How do you find the gradient of a tangent and of a normal? ~~ The tangent gradient is dy/dx at that point, the normal gradient is −1 divided by that value.
How do you classify a stationary point using the second derivative? ~~ Positive means minimum, negative means maximum, zero is inconclusive and requires checking the gradient either side.
Does d²y/dx² = 0 prove a point of inflection? ~~ No. For y = x⁴ at the origin the second derivative is zero yet the point is a minimum. A genuine inflection needs the second derivative to CHANGE SIGN.
How do connected rates of change work? ~~ Link them by the chain rule, for example dV/dt = (dV/dr) × (dr/dt).
What are the two marks most often lost in an optimisation question? ~~ Not justifying that the stationary point is the required maximum or minimum, and not answering the quantity actually asked for.
Differentiate y = 3x⁴ − 2x + 7. ~~ dy/dx = 12x³ − 2.
Differentiate y = √x. ~~ y = x^(1/2), so dy/dx = ½x^(−1/2) = 1/(2√x).
Differentiate y = e^(3x). ~~ dy/dx = 3e^(3x).
Differentiate y = ln(5x). ~~ dy/dx = 1/x.
Differentiate y = sin(2x). ~~ dy/dx = 2 cos(2x).
Differentiate y = (2x + 1)⁵. ~~ dy/dx = 5(2x + 1)⁴ × 2 = 10(2x + 1)⁴.
Differentiate y = x² eˣ. ~~ Product rule: dy/dx = 2x eˣ + x² eˣ = x eˣ(2 + x).
Differentiate y = (sin x)/x. ~~ Quotient rule: dy/dx = (x cos x − sin x)/x².
What is the derivative of aˣ? ~~ aˣ ln a.
What does it mean if dy/dx > 0? ~~ The function is increasing.
What does a positive second derivative indicate? ~~ The curve is concave upwards (convex), and a stationary point there is a minimum.
What is a point of inflection? ~~ A point where the curve changes concavity (d²y/dx² changes sign).
Find the gradient of x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4). ~~ 2x + 2y dy/dx = 0, so dy/dx = −x/y = −3/4.
What is the derivative of sec x? ~~ sec x tan x.

## 1.8
What is the integral of xⁿ, and when does it fail? ~~ x^(n+1)/(n + 1) + c, failing for n = −1 where the denominator would be zero. Then the integral of 1/x is ln|x| + c.
Why is the modulus written in ln|x|? ~~ So the result is valid for negative x too, since the logarithm of a negative number is undefined.
Why does an indefinite integral need + c but a definite one does not? ~~ Differentiating a constant gives zero, so it cannot be recovered. In a definite integral the constant appears in both F(b) and F(a) and cancels.
State the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. ~~ The definite integral from a to b of f equals F(b) − F(a), where F′ = f. It links area with the antiderivative and shows the two processes are inverse.
What happens to a region below the x-axis in a definite integral? ~~ It contributes a negative amount. If the curve crosses the axis within the limits, split there and add the magnitudes, or the parts will cancel.
How do you find the area between two curves? ~~ Integrate (upper − lower) between the intersections, so the integrand is non-negative and the area comes out positive.
When is integration by substitution the right method? ~~ When the integrand contains an inner function together with its derivative — for example x e^(x²), where u = x² gives du = 2x dx.
What must you also change when substituting in a DEFINITE integral? ~~ The limits, converting them to values of u — or substitute back to x before applying the original limits.
State the standard result for f′(x)/f(x). ~~ Its integral is ln|f(x)| + c. For example the integral of 2x/(x² + 1) is ln(x² + 1) + c.
State the integration by parts formula. ~~ The integral of u(dv/dx) equals uv minus the integral of v(du/dx) — the reverse of the product rule.
How do you choose u when integrating by parts? ~~ Choose u to be the factor that becomes SIMPLER when differentiated, and dv/dx to be one you can integrate.
How do you integrate ln x? ~~ Write it as 1 × ln x, take u = ln x and dv/dx = 1. This gives x ln x − x + c.
How do you integrate eˣ sin x? ~~ Apply parts twice, the original integral reappears, so treat it as an unknown and solve the resulting equation algebraically.
Why are partial fractions useful in integration? ~~ A fraction with a factorised denominator has no standard integral, but splitting it gives terms A/(x + a), each integrating to a logarithm.
How do you integrate cos²x? ~~ Use the double angle identity cos²x = ½(1 + cos 2x), giving x/2 + (sin 2x)/4 + c.
What are the integrals of sin x, cos x and sec²x? ~~ −cos x + c, sin x + c, tan x + c. The minus on the sine integral is the one most often dropped.
Integrate 6x² + 4x − 3. ~~ 2x³ + 2x² − 3x + c.
Integrate 1/x². ~~ −1/x + c.
Integrate e^(2x). ~~ ½e^(2x) + c.
Integrate 1/x. ~~ ln|x| + c.
Integrate cos(3x). ~~ ⅓ sin(3x) + c.
Integrate (2x + 1)³. ~~ (2x + 1)⁴/8 + c.
What is the difference between a general and a particular solution? ~~ A general solution contains an arbitrary constant and represents a family of curves, a particular solution uses an initial condition to fix that constant.
How do you separate the variables? ~~ If dy/dx = f(x)g(y), gather all the y terms with dy and all the x terms with dx, then integrate both sides.
Translate 'the rate of growth is proportional to the size' into an equation. ~~ dP/dt = kP. For decay it is dN/dt = −kN with k positive.
Translate 'the rate is proportional to the square of the population'. ~~ dP/dt = kP².
Write down Newton's law of cooling. ~~ dθ/dt = −k(θ − θ₀), where θ₀ is the temperature of the surroundings — the rate is proportional to the excess temperature.
What is the solution of dP/dt = kP with P = P₀ at t = 0? ~~ P = P₀e^(kt).
Why is it useful to write the constant as ln A? ~~ It turns an additive constant into a multiplicative one, so ln y = ln x + c becomes y = Ax instead of y = e^c × x.
Solve dy/dx = xy with y = 1 at x = 0. ~~ Separate to (1/y)dy = x dx, integrate to ln y = x²/2 + c, apply the condition to get c = 0, so y = e^(x²/2).

## 1.9
State the change of sign condition for locating a root. ~~ If f is CONTINUOUS on [a, b] and f(a), f(b) have opposite signs, then f(x) = 0 has at least one root in (a, b).
Which part of the change of sign argument is most often omitted? ~~ The statement that f is continuous on the interval. Without it the argument is invalid.
Name two situations in which a change of sign method fails. ~~ A repeated root, where the curve touches without crossing, e.g. (x − 2)², and a vertical asymptote, e.g. 1/(x − 2), where the sign changes but there is no root.
How do you show a root is 1.86 correct to 2 decimal places? ~~ Show f changes sign between the rounding bounds 1.855 and 1.865, so every value in that interval rounds to 1.86.
What is fixed point iteration? ~~ Rearrange f(x) = 0 into x = g(x), choose a starting value, then apply x₍ₙ₊₁₎ = g(xₙ) repeatedly.
When does a fixed point iteration converge? ~~ When |g′(x)| < 1 near the root. If |g′(x)| > 1 it diverges, so different rearrangements of the same equation can behave differently.
What is the difference between a staircase and a cobweb diagram? ~~ Both are drawn between y = g(x) and y = x. A staircase occurs when g′(x) > 0, a cobweb, spiralling and alternating sides, when g′(x) < 0.
State the Newton-Raphson formula and its geometric meaning. ~~ x₍ₙ₊₁₎ = xₙ − f(xₙ)/f′(xₙ). Follow the tangent at the current point down to where it meets the x-axis.
Why does Newton-Raphson converge faster than a simple iteration? ~~ Near a simple root the error is roughly SQUARED at each step, so the number of correct digits approximately doubles.
When does Newton-Raphson fail? ~~ When f′(xₙ) = 0, since the tangent is horizontal and never meets the axis, and near a stationary point, where a nearly horizontal tangent throws the next estimate far away.
State the trapezium rule. ~~ With h = (b − a)/n, the integral is approximately (h/2)[y₀ + yₙ + 2(y₁ + … + y₍ₙ₋₁₎)] — first and last counted once, interior ordinates doubled.
How many ordinates are needed for n strips? ~~ n + 1, since adjacent strips share an ordinate.
When does the trapezium rule give an over- or under-estimate? ~~ Concave upwards: chords lie above the curve, so it is an OVERestimate. Concave downwards: chords lie below, so it is an UNDERestimate.
How should you justify the direction of a trapezium error? ~~ Refer to the concavity: 'the curve is concave upwards on this interval, so each chord lies above it and the estimate is too large'.
What happens to the trapezium rule error if you halve the strip width? ~~ It reduces by a factor of roughly 4, since the error is approximately proportional to the square of the width.
How can rectangles give bounds on an integral? ~~ For a monotonic function, left-hand and right-hand rectangle sums lie on either side of the true value, bracketing it.
What does the change of sign method rely on? ~~ If f is continuous on [a, b] and f(a) and f(b) have opposite signs, there is a root between a and b.
Why can a change of sign occur without a root? ~~ At a vertical asymptote (discontinuity), e.g. f(x) = 1/x between −1 and 1.
Why might there be a root with no change of sign? ~~ A repeated root where the curve touches the x-axis, or two roots close together.
What is decimal search? ~~ Checking values at intervals of 0.1, then 0.01, etc., to locate a root by change of sign.
What is interval bisection? ~~ Repeatedly halving the interval containing the root and keeping the half with a sign change.
How do you rearrange f(x) = 0 into x = g(x)? ~~ Make x the subject of part of the equation, e.g. x³ − x − 1 = 0 → x = ∛(x + 1).
What is the convergence condition for xₙ₊₁ = g(xₙ)? ~~ |g′(x)| < 1 near the root.
What is a staircase diagram? ~~ A diagram showing iteration converging monotonically, when 0 < g′(x) < 1.
What is a cobweb diagram? ~~ A diagram showing iteration oscillating around the root, when −1 < g′(x) < 0.
Apply one step of Newton–Raphson to f(x) = x² − 2 from x₀ = 1. ~~ x₁ = 1 − (1 − 2)/2 = 1.5.
When does the trapezium rule give an overestimate? ~~ When the curve is concave up (convex) over the interval.
When does the trapezium rule give an underestimate? ~~ When the curve is concave down over the interval.
How do you improve the accuracy of the trapezium rule? ~~ Increase the number of strips (decrease the strip width).
What is the width h in the trapezium rule? ~~ h = (b − a)/n, where n is the number of strips.

## 1.10
What is the difference between a scalar and a vector? ~~ A scalar has magnitude only (mass, time, speed), a vector has magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, force).
How are vectors written in print and by hand? ~~ Bold in print, underlined by hand. The magnitude is written |a| or a in italic.
How do you find the magnitude of a vector in 2D and 3D? ~~ √(a₁² + a₂²) in two dimensions, √(a₁² + a₂² + a₃²) in three.
How do you find a unit vector in the direction of a? ~~ Divide a by its magnitude: â = a/|a|. For 6i − 8j, the magnitude is 10 so the unit vector is 0.6i − 0.8j.
When are two non-zero vectors parallel? ~~ When one is a scalar multiple of the other. A negative scalar reverses the direction but they remain parallel.
Write down the vector AB in terms of position vectors. ~~ AB = b − a — destination minus start. Reversing it gives the same magnitude in the opposite direction.
How do you find the distance between two points from their position vectors? ~~ It is |b − a|, the magnitude of the vector joining them.
What is the position vector of the midpoint of AB? ~~ (a + b)/2. More generally the point dividing AB in the ratio m : n is (na + mb)/(m + n).
How do you convert between component and magnitude-direction form? ~~ a₁ = r cos θ and a₂ = r sin θ, conversely r = √(a₁² + a₂²) and tan θ = a₂/a₁.
Why is arctan alone not enough to give a direction? ~~ It returns angles only in the first and fourth quadrants. Use the signs of the components to identify the quadrant and adjust — (−3, −4) gives 233.1°, not 53.1°.
How do you prove three points collinear using vectors? ~~ Show one joining vector is a scalar multiple of another, AND state that they share a common point. Parallel alone is not enough.
What does the sum of force vectors represent? ~~ The resultant force. The specification explicitly includes using vectors for problems involving forces.
What is the condition for a set of forces to be in equilibrium? ~~ Their vector sum is the zero vector, so drawn head to tail they form a closed figure.
Is |a + b| equal to |a| + |b|? ~~ Not in general. It lies between ||a| − |b|| and |a| + |b|, reaching the upper value only when the vectors point the same way.
How does 3D vector work extend the 2D case? ~~ A third component along k is added. Magnitudes involve the sum of three squares, and all the 2D techniques carry over.
What is the zero vector? ~~ A vector with zero magnitude and no specific direction.
Find the magnitude of 3i − 4j. ~~ √(9 + 16) = 5.
Find the unit vector in the direction of 3i − 4j. ~~ (3i − 4j)/5 = 0.6i − 0.8j.
If a = 2i + j and b = i − 3j, find 2a − b. ~~ 4i + 2j − i + 3j = 3i + 5j.
What is a position vector? ~~ A vector giving the position of a point relative to the origin.
Find the direction of 3i + 3j. ~~ 45° above the positive x-axis.
What is the magnitude of 2i − j + 2k? ~~ √(4 + 1 + 4) = 3.
How do you find a vector of magnitude 10 in the direction of 3i + 4j? ~~ Unit vector (3i + 4j)/5, times 10: 6i + 8j.
If AB = b − a, what is BA? ~~ a − b.
What is the resultant of forces (3i + 2j) N and (−i + 4j) N? ~~ 2i + 6j N.
Express 10 N at 30° above the x-axis in component form. ~~ 10 cos 30° i + 10 sin 30° j = 5√3 i + 5j N.
How do you show two vectors are parallel? ~~ Show one is a scalar multiple of the other.
What is a column vector? ~~ A vector written vertically with components, e.g. (3, 4) written as a column.
What does it mean for vectors to be equal? ~~ They have the same magnitude and direction (equal components).

## 2.1
What is the difference between a population and a sample? ~~ A population is the entire group being studied, a sample is a subset used to draw conclusions about it.
What is the difference between a parameter and a statistic? ~~ A parameter describes the POPULATION, a statistic is calculated from a SAMPLE and used to estimate it.
Define simple random sampling. ~~ Every possible sample of the required size has the same probability of being selected. It requires a complete sampling frame.
What is a sampling frame? ~~ A list of all members of the population, from which the sample is drawn. An incomplete frame means some members can never be selected.
Describe systematic sampling and its main risk. ~~ Select every kth member of an ordered list. It fails if the list has a repeating pattern matching the sampling interval.
Describe stratified sampling. ~~ Divide the population into strata and sample each in proportion to its size, selecting AT RANDOM within each stratum.
Name the four types of data. ~~ Categorical (labels), discrete (separate numerical values), continuous (any value in a range) and ranked (ordered positions).
In a histogram, what represents frequency? ~~ The AREA of each bar. The vertical axis is frequency density, which is frequency divided by class width.
How does a frequency chart differ from a histogram? ~~ A frequency chart has equal-width bars with FREQUENCY on the vertical axis, a histogram may have unequal widths and uses frequency density.
What does a box-and-whisker diagram show? ~~ The median, the lower and upper quartiles, and the extreme values. The box spans the interquartile range.
When is the median preferred to the mean? ~~ For skewed data or when outliers are present, since the median depends only on position in the order and resists extremes.
When is a weighted mean appropriate? ~~ When combining groups of different sizes, weighting each group mean by the size of that group.
How is probability calculated for equally likely outcomes? ~~ Favourable outcomes divided by total outcomes. Every probability lies between 0 and 1 inclusive.
What is a complementary event? ~~ A′ is the event that A does not occur, and P(A′) = 1 − P(A). It is the fastest route for 'at least one' questions.
What is the expected frequency of an event in n trials? ~~ nP(A). In 200 trials with probability 0.15, the expected frequency is 30.
What do A ∩ B and A ∪ B mean? ~~ A ∩ B means both occur. A ∪ B means A or B or both occur.
Define mutually exclusive events and give the addition rule. ~~ They cannot both occur, so P(A ∩ B) = 0 and P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B).
Define independent events and give the multiplication rule. ~~ The occurrence of one does not affect the probability of the other, so P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B).
What is a discrete random variable? ~~ A variable taking separate values each with a given probability. Capital X denotes the variable, lower-case x or r a particular value.
What condition must a discrete probability distribution satisfy? ~~ All probabilities non-negative and summing to 1.
What is a discrete uniform distribution? ~~ One in which all outcomes are equally likely, such as the score on a fair die.
State the four conditions for a binomial model. ~~ A fixed number of trials, exactly two outcomes per trial, the same probability of success each time, and independent trials.
What does X ~ B(n, p) mean? ~~ X has the binomial distribution with n trials and probability p of success. The symbol ~ is read 'has the distribution'.
Write down the binomial probability formula. ~~ P(X = r) = ⁿCᵣ pʳ q^(n−r), where q = 1 − p.
What are the null and alternative hypotheses? ~~ H₀ is the default assumption being tested, always an equality. H₁ is what is concluded if H₀ is rejected.
Must hypotheses refer to the sample or the population? ~~ The POPULATION parameter — p or μ. Writing H₀: x̄ = 50 is wrong, it must be H₀: μ = 50.
What is the significance level? ~~ The probability of incorrectly rejecting a TRUE null hypothesis. It sets the size of the critical region.
Define critical region, acceptance region and critical value. ~~ The critical (rejection) region contains test statistic values leading to rejection, the acceptance region those that do not, the critical value is the boundary.
What is a p-value and how is it used? ~~ The probability of a result at least as extreme as the one observed, assuming H₀. Reject H₀ if it is below the significance level.
When is a test one-tailed and when two-tailed? ~~ One-tailed when H₁ specifies a DIRECTION ('biased towards heads'), two-tailed when it does not ('biased', 'has changed').

## 2.2
What is the difference between distance travelled and displacement? ~~ Distance travelled is the total path length, a scalar, displacement is the net change in position, a vector.
What is the difference between speed and velocity? ~~ Speed is the magnitude of velocity, so it is a scalar and never negative, velocity carries direction.
Give the formulae for average speed and average velocity. ~~ Average speed = distance travelled ÷ time, average velocity = overall displacement ÷ time. A there-and-back journey has zero average velocity.
What do the gradient and area of a velocity-time graph give? ~~ Gradient gives acceleration, area gives displacement.
What does the gradient of a displacement-time graph give? ~~ Velocity.
How are velocity and acceleration obtained from a position vector? ~~ By differentiating with respect to t, component by component. Integrating reverses the process.
Why can vector calculus be done component by component? ~~ The i and j directions are fixed and independent of time, so each component is an independent function of t.
What is the speed of a particle with velocity v? ~~ |v|, the magnitude — a scalar. Quoting a speed as a vector loses the mark.
If r = 3t i + t² j, what are v and a? ~~ v = 3i + 2t j and a = 2j. At t = 2 the speed is |3i + 4j| = 5.
State the four standard projectile modelling assumptions. ~~ No air resistance, the projectile is a particle, gravity always acts in the same direction, gravity is constant in magnitude.
What is the central idea that makes projectile problems work? ~~ Horizontal and vertical motion are INDEPENDENT. Horizontally there is no acceleration, vertically the acceleration is g downwards. Time links them.
Give the initial velocity components for launch at speed u and angle θ. ~~ Horizontal u cos θ, vertical u sin θ.
Give the displacement components at time t. ~~ Horizontal u cos θ × t, vertical u sin θ × t − ½gt².
Write down the time of flight, maximum height and range on level ground. ~~ T = 2u sin θ/g. H = u²sin²θ/(2g). R = u² sin 2θ/g.
Name four common forces and their directions. ~~ Weight acts vertically downwards, normal reaction perpendicular to the surface, tension along a string pulling inwards, friction along the surface opposing motion.
What is the difference between tension and thrust? ~~ Tension pulls the ends together, thrust pushes them apart. A string can only pull, a rod can do either.
Is the normal reaction always equal to the weight? ~~ No. It depends on the other forces: pressing down increases it, pulling upwards at an angle reduces it, and on a slope it is mg cos θ.
Give the components of weight on a plane inclined at θ. ~~ mg sin θ along the plane and mg cos θ perpendicular to it. Check with θ = 0: sin 0 = 0, so the along-plane component vanishes as it should.
Why resolve parallel and perpendicular to an inclined plane? ~~ It puts the normal reaction on one axis and the friction on the other, so each equation contains fewer unknowns.
State Newton's first law. ~~ An object continues at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless acted on by a resultant force.
State Newton's second law. ~~ A resultant force F on an object of fixed mass m gives acceleration a with F = ma.
State Newton's third law. ~~ When one object exerts a force on another, there is a reaction equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
What must the F in F = ma be? ~~ The RESULTANT of all forces acting. Using a single force and ignoring friction or weight is the standard structural error.
How do you write down an equation of motion? ~~ Draw a force diagram, choose a positive direction (usually that of the acceleration), resolve in that direction and set the total equal to ma.
How is equilibrium a special case of the second law? ~~ Setting a = 0 gives a resultant force of zero — which also recovers the first law.
Why does the point of application matter for a rigid body but not a particle? ~~ A particle has no size, so forces only translate it. A rigid body has extent, so forces can also produce a turning effect.
Define the moment of a force and give its units. ~~ Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the point. Units are newton metres, N m.
What is the moment of a force acting through the pivot? ~~ Zero, since the perpendicular distance is zero. This is why choosing the pivot cleverly removes unknowns.
State the two conditions for a rigid body to be in equilibrium. ~~ The resultant force is zero, AND the sum of the moments about any one point is zero.
Why is the force condition alone insufficient? ~~ Two equal and opposite forces applied at different points give no net force but do produce a turning effect.

Prove that the product of two consecutive integers is even. ~~ Consider n(n + 1). One of n and n + 1 is even, so the product has a factor of 2 and is even.
