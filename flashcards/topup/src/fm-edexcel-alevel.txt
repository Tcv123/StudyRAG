## CP1.1
What is i? ~~ The imaginary unit, defined by i² = −1.
What is a complex number? ~~ A number of the form z = a + bi, where a and b are real.
What are Re(z) and Im(z) for z = 3 − 4i? ~~ Re(z) = 3 and Im(z) = −4.
What is the complex conjugate of z = a + bi? ~~ z* = a − bi.
Simplify i³ and i⁴. ~~ i³ = −i and i⁴ = 1.
Add (2 + 3i) + (4 − i). ~~ 6 + 2i.
Multiply (2 + 3i)(1 − i). ~~ 2 − 2i + 3i − 3i² = 5 + i.
What is z z* for z = a + bi? ~~ a² + b², which is always real and non-negative.
How do you divide complex numbers? ~~ Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.
Calculate (3 + i)/(1 − i). ~~ (3 + i)(1 + i)/2 = (3 + 4i + i²)/2 = (2 + 4i)/2 = 1 + 2i.
Solve z² + 4 = 0. ~~ z = ±2i.
Solve z² − 2z + 5 = 0. ~~ z = (2 ± √(4 − 20))/2 = 1 ± 2i.
What is special about the complex roots of a real polynomial? ~~ They occur in conjugate pairs.
If 2 + i is a root of a real quadratic, what is the quadratic? ~~ Roots 2 ± i: z² − 4z + 5 = 0.
When are two complex numbers equal? ~~ When their real parts are equal and their imaginary parts are equal.
Find the square roots of 3 + 4i. ~~ (a + bi)² = 3 + 4i gives a² − b² = 3 and ab = 2, so ±(2 + i).
What is the modulus of z = a + bi? ~~ |z| = √(a² + b²).
What is the argument of z? ~~ The angle θ between the positive real axis and the line to z, usually −π < θ ≤ π.
Find the modulus and argument of 1 + i. ~~ |z| = √2 and arg z = π/4.
Find the argument of −1 − √3 i. ~~ −2π/3.
What is the modulus–argument form of z? ~~ z = r(cos θ + i sin θ).
How do moduli behave under multiplication? ~~ |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|.
How do arguments behave under multiplication? ~~ arg(z₁z₂) = arg z₁ + arg z₂ (adjusted to the principal range).
How do moduli and arguments behave under division? ~~ |z₁/z₂| = |z₁|/|z₂| and arg(z₁/z₂) = arg z₁ − arg z₂.
Solve a cubic with a known real root: z³ − 1 = 0. ~~ (z − 1)(z² + z + 1) = 0, so z = 1 or z = −½ ± (√3/2)i.
What is z + z* for z = a + bi? ~~ 2a, which is real.
What is z − z*? ~~ 2bi, which is purely imaginary.
What is (z*)*? ~~ z.
Express 2(cos π/3 + i sin π/3) in the form a + bi. ~~ 1 + √3 i.
Why can't complex numbers be ordered like real numbers? ~~ There is no consistent way to say which of two complex numbers is larger.

## CP1.2
What is an Argand diagram? ~~ A diagram representing complex numbers as points, with the real part on the horizontal axis and the imaginary part on the vertical axis.
How is z* shown on an Argand diagram? ~~ As the reflection of z in the real axis.
What does |z − a| represent? ~~ The distance from z to the point a on the Argand diagram.
Describe the locus |z − 2 − i| = 3. ~~ A circle with centre (2, 1) and radius 3.
Describe the locus |z − 1| = |z + i|. ~~ The perpendicular bisector of the points 1 and −i.
Describe the locus arg(z − 1) = π/4. ~~ A half-line from (1, 0) at angle π/4 to the positive real axis, not including the point 1.
What does |z| < 2 represent? ~~ The interior of a circle centred at the origin with radius 2.
Describe the region |z − 3| ≤ |z + 1|. ~~ The points closer to 3 than to −1: Re(z) ≥ 1.
What does arg z = θ represent? ~~ A half-line from the origin at angle θ.
How is addition shown on an Argand diagram? ~~ As vector addition (the parallelogram rule).
How is multiplication by i shown on an Argand diagram? ~~ A rotation by π/2 anticlockwise about the origin.
What is the effect of multiplying by r(cos θ + i sin θ)? ~~ An enlargement by scale factor r and a rotation by θ about the origin.
Find the maximum value of |z| if |z − 3 − 4i| = 2. ~~ The distance to the centre is 5, so the maximum is 5 + 2 = 7.
Find the minimum value of |z| if |z − 3 − 4i| = 2. ~~ 5 − 2 = 3.
How do you convert a locus like |z − 1| = 2|z + 1| into Cartesian form? ~~ Let z = x + iy, square both sides, and simplify, giving a circle (an Apollonius circle).
What does arg((z − a)/(z − b)) = θ represent? ~~ An arc of a circle through a and b, where the angle subtended by ab is θ.
What does arg((z − a)/(z − b)) = π/2 represent? ~~ A semicircle with diameter ab.
How do you shade a region like π/6 ≤ arg z ≤ π/3? ~~ Shade between the two half-lines from the origin at those angles.
How is the modulus shown on an Argand diagram? ~~ As the distance from the origin to the point.
What locus is |z − a| = k|z − b| for k ≠ 1? ~~ A circle.
Where are the roots of z² + 2z + 5 = 0 on an Argand diagram? ~~ At −1 + 2i and −1 − 2i, symmetric about the real axis.
Describe |z + 2i| = 1. ~~ A circle with centre −2i (0, −2) and radius 1.
How do you find where two loci intersect? ~~ Convert both to Cartesian equations and solve simultaneously, or use geometry.
What does Re(z) > 2 represent? ~~ The region to the right of the line x = 2.
What does Im(z) = −1 represent? ~~ A horizontal line through −i.
What is the geometric meaning of |z₁ − z₂|? ~~ The distance between z₁ and z₂.
How can the triangle inequality be written for complex numbers? ~~ |z₁ + z₂| ≤ |z₁| + |z₂|.
Describe the locus |z| = |z − 4|. ~~ The line Re(z) = 2.
What shape do the n-th roots of unity form on an Argand diagram? ~~ A regular n-sided polygon inscribed in the unit circle.
How do you find the greatest arg z for z on a circle not containing the origin? ~~ Draw the tangent from the origin to the circle and use right-angled triangle trigonometry.

## CP1.3
What is Σ r from r = 1 to n? ~~ n(n + 1)/2.
What is Σ r² from r = 1 to n? ~~ n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6.
What is Σ r³ from r = 1 to n? ~~ n²(n + 1)²/4.
What is Σ 1 from r = 1 to n? ~~ n.
Evaluate Σ r² from r = 1 to 10. ~~ 10 × 11 × 21/6 = 385.
Find Σ (2r − 1) from r = 1 to n. ~~ n(n + 1) − n = n².
Find Σ r(r + 1) from r = 1 to n. ~~ Σr² + Σr = n(n + 1)(n + 2)/3.
How do you evaluate Σ from r = 11 to 20? ~~ Σ from 1 to 20 minus Σ from 1 to 10.
What is the method of differences? ~~ Writing each term as f(r) − f(r + 1) so that most terms cancel when summed.
Use the method of differences to sum Σ 1/(r(r + 1)). ~~ 1/(r(r + 1)) = 1/r − 1/(r + 1), so the sum from 1 to n is 1 − 1/(n + 1).
Find Σ 1/(r(r + 2)) from r = 1 to n. ~~ ½ Σ (1/r − 1/(r + 2)) = ½(3/2 − 1/(n + 1) − 1/(n + 2)).
What is a telescoping series? ~~ A series in which consecutive terms cancel, leaving only a few terms.
What is the sum to infinity of Σ 1/(r(r + 1))? ~~ 1.
How do you prove a summation formula? ~~ Using the standard results, the method of differences, or proof by induction.
What is Σ (ar + b)? ~~ aΣr + bn.
Express Σ r(r − 1) in terms of n. ~~ Σr² − Σr = (n − 1)n(n + 1)/3.
Evaluate Σ r³ from r = 1 to 5. ~~ 25 × 36/4 = 225.
What is the relationship between Σ r³ and Σ r? ~~ Σ r³ = (Σ r)².
Why must care be taken with the lower limit when using the method of differences? ~~ The surviving terms depend on the limits; write out the first and last few terms.
How are partial fractions useful for series? ~~ They split a term into differences that telescope.
What is sigma notation? ~~ Σ f(r) from r = a to b means f(a) + f(a + 1) + … + f(b).
Can a divergent series have a finite partial sum? ~~ Yes, every partial sum is finite, but the sum to infinity doesn't exist.
Find Σ (r + 1)² from r = 1 to n. ~~ Σr² + 2Σr + n = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6 + n(n + 1) + n.
What is Σ from r = 1 to n of r(r + 1)(r + 2)? ~~ n(n + 1)(n + 2)(n + 3)/4.
Show that Σ from 1 to n of (r³ − r) = n(n + 1)(n − 1)(n + 2)/4. ~~ n²(n + 1)²/4 − n(n + 1)/2 = n(n + 1)/4 × (n(n + 1) − 2) = n(n + 1)(n − 1)(n + 2)/4.
What is the sum of the first n odd cubes? ~~ n²(2n² − 1).
What does convergence mean for a series? ~~ The partial sums approach a finite limit as n → ∞.
What is the harmonic series and does it converge? ~~ Σ 1/r; it diverges.
How do you sum Σ f(r) − f(r − 1) from 1 to n? ~~ It telescopes to f(n) − f(0).
What is the general strategy for summing a polynomial in r? ~~ Expand, then use the standard results for Σr, Σr², Σr³ and Σ1.

## CP1.4
For ax² + bx + c = 0 with roots α, β, what are α + β and αβ? ~~ α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a.
For ax³ + bx² + cx + d = 0, what are Σα, Σαβ and αβγ? ~~ Σα = −b/a, Σαβ = c/a, αβγ = −d/a.
For a quartic ax⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e = 0, what is the product of the roots? ~~ αβγδ = e/a.
Express α² + β² in terms of α + β and αβ. ~~ (α + β)² − 2αβ.
Express α³ + β³ in terms of α + β and αβ. ~~ (α + β)³ − 3αβ(α + β).
Express 1/α + 1/β in terms of α + β and αβ. ~~ (α + β)/αβ.
Express α² + β² + γ² for a cubic. ~~ (Σα)² − 2Σαβ.
How do you find an equation whose roots are 2α and 2β? ~~ Substitute x = w/2 into the original equation.
How do you find an equation whose roots are α + 1, β + 1? ~~ Substitute x = w − 1 into the original equation.
How do you find an equation with roots α², β² using substitution? ~~ Substitute x = √w, rearrange so √w terms are on one side, and square.
The roots of x² − 5x + 3 = 0 are α, β. Find α² + β². ~~ 25 − 6 = 19.
The roots of 2x³ − 3x² + x − 4 = 0 are α, β, γ. Find αβγ. ~~ −(−4)/2 = 2.
Find the quadratic with roots α + β and αβ given α + β = 3, αβ = 2. ~~ Roots 3 and 2: x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
If one root of x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 = 0 is 1, find the others. ~~ (x − 1)(x² − 5x + 6), so 2 and 3.
What does it mean if a cubic's roots form an arithmetic sequence? ~~ They can be written a − d, a, a + d, and Σα = 3a.
Why do real polynomials have complex roots in conjugate pairs? ~~ Taking the conjugate of p(z) = 0 gives p(z*) = 0, because the coefficients are real.
A real cubic has roots 2 and 1 + i. Find the cubic. ~~ Roots 2, 1 ± i: (x − 2)(x² − 2x + 2) = x³ − 4x² + 6x − 4.
How many real roots can a real cubic have? ~~ One or three (counting repeated roots).
How many complex (non-real) roots can a real quartic have? ~~ 0, 2 or 4.
What is Σα for x⁴ − 2x³ + x − 7 = 0? ~~ 2.
What is Σαβγ for a quartic ax⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e? ~~ −d/a.
Find α³ + β³ if α + β = 2 and αβ = −1. ~~ 8 − 3(−1)(2) = 14.
How do you find 1/α + 1/β + 1/γ for a cubic? ~~ Σαβ/αβγ.
What does a repeated root mean for the graph? ~~ The graph touches the x-axis at that point.
If x = 2 + 3i is a root of a real polynomial, what else must be? ~~ 2 − 3i.
How can the sum and product of roots check a solution? ~~ Compare the computed roots' sum and product with −b/a and c/a.
What substitution gives roots 1/α, 1/β, 1/γ? ~~ Substitute x = 1/w (this reverses the coefficients).
Find the equation with roots 1/α, 1/β if α, β are roots of 2x² + 3x − 1 = 0. ~~ Reverse the coefficients: −w² + 3w + 2 = 0, i.e. w² − 3w − 2 = 0.
How many roots does a polynomial of degree n have? ~~ Exactly n roots in the complex numbers, counting multiplicity.
What is the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra? ~~ Every non-constant polynomial has at least one complex root.

## CP1.5
What is the volume of revolution about the x-axis? ~~ V = π ∫ y² dx between the limits.
What is the volume of revolution about the y-axis? ~~ V = π ∫ x² dy between the y-limits.
Find the volume when y = x from 0 to 3 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀³ x² dx = 9π (a cone).
Find the volume when y = √x from 0 to 4 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀⁴ x dx = 8π.
Find the volume when y = x² from 0 to 1 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀¹ x⁴ dx = π/5.
How do you find the volume between two curves rotated about the x-axis? ~~ V = π ∫ (y₁² − y₂²) dx, where y₁ is the outer curve.
Why is it not π ∫ (y₁ − y₂)² dx? ~~ The solid is a washer: the volume is the outer solid minus the inner solid.
How do you rotate y = x² about the y-axis between y = 0 and y = 4? ~~ x² = y, so V = π ∫₀⁴ y dy = 8π.
What is the volume of a sphere derived by rotation? ~~ Rotate x² + y² = r² about the x-axis: π ∫₋ᵣʳ (r² − x²) dx = (4/3)πr³.
What is the volume of a cone derived by rotation? ~~ Rotate y = (r/h)x from 0 to h: π ∫ (r²/h²)x² dx = (1/3)πr²h.
How can volumes of revolution model real objects? ~~ E.g. vases, bottles and bowls can be modelled by rotating curves.
What assumption is made when modelling an object with a volume of revolution? ~~ The object is perfectly symmetrical about its axis and the curve accurately describes its profile.
Find the volume when y = eˣ from 0 to 1 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀¹ e^(2x) dx = (π/2)(e² − 1).
Find the volume when y = 1/x from 1 to 2 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₁² x⁻² dx = π/2.
What must you do before rotating about the y-axis? ~~ Rearrange to make x² the subject in terms of y, and use y-limits.
Find the volume when y = sin x from 0 to π is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀^π sin²x dx = π²/2.
How do you find the volume of a solid with a hole? ~~ Subtract the volume of the hole (inner solid) from the outer solid.
What are the units of a volume of revolution? ~~ Cubic units.
What is the volume of a cylinder by rotation? ~~ Rotate y = r from 0 to h about the x-axis: π ∫₀ʰ r² dx = πr²h.
How do you find the limits for the region between y = x and y = x²? ~~ Solve x = x²: x = 0 and x = 1.
Find the volume when the region between y = x and y = x² is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀¹ (x² − x⁴) dx = π(1/3 − 1/5) = 2π/15.
What if the curve crosses the axis of rotation? ~~ y² is always positive, so the volume formula still works, but check the region carefully.
How can a volume of revolution be scaled to model a real object? ~~ Multiply by the cube of the scale factor, or use scaled units.
Why might a model underestimate a real vase's volume? ~~ The curve may not match the real shape exactly, or the wall thickness may be ignored.
What volume is generated by rotating y = 2 between x = 1 and x = 3? ~~ A cylinder: π × 4 × 2 = 8π.
Find the volume when y = x³ from 0 to 1 is rotated about the y-axis. ~~ x = y^(1/3), x² = y^(2/3): π ∫₀¹ y^(2/3) dy = 3π/5.
What is the washer method? ~~ Finding the volume of a solid with a hole by subtracting inner from outer volumes.
How do you find the volume of a region bounded by a curve and a line not on an axis? ~~ Find the intersection points, then subtract the volume under the line from the volume under the curve.
Which integral gives volume about the x-axis for y = f(x)? ~~ π ∫ [f(x)]² dx.
Why is volume of revolution an application of integration? ~~ The solid is split into thin discs of volume πy² δx, which are summed as δx → 0.

## CP1.6
What is the order of a matrix? ~~ The number of rows × the number of columns.
When can two matrices be multiplied? ~~ When the number of columns in the first equals the number of rows in the second.
Is matrix multiplication commutative? ~~ No; in general AB ≠ BA.
Is matrix multiplication associative? ~~ Yes: (AB)C = A(BC).
What is the identity matrix I? ~~ A square matrix with 1s on the leading diagonal and 0s elsewhere; AI = IA = A.
What is the determinant of a 2×2 matrix (a b; c d)? ~~ ad − bc.
What is the inverse of a 2×2 matrix (a b; c d)? ~~ (1/(ad − bc)) × (d −b; −c a).
What is a singular matrix? ~~ A matrix with determinant zero, which has no inverse.
What is (AB)⁻¹? ~~ B⁻¹A⁻¹.
How do you find the determinant of a 3×3 matrix? ~~ Expand along a row or column using cofactors: a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg).
What does the determinant of a transformation matrix represent? ~~ The area (2D) or volume (3D) scale factor; a negative value means orientation is reversed.
How can matrices solve simultaneous equations? ~~ Write as AX = B, then X = A⁻¹B if A is non-singular.
What does det A = 0 mean for a system of 3 equations? ~~ There is no unique solution: either no solutions (inconsistent) or infinitely many.
How do three planes with no unique solution appear geometrically? ~~ As a sheaf (common line), a prism (no common point), or with parallel planes.
What is det(AB)? ~~ det A × det B.
What is the transpose of a matrix? ~~ The matrix formed by swapping rows and columns, Aᵀ.
How do you find the inverse of a 3×3 matrix? ~~ Find the matrix of cofactors, transpose it (adjugate), then divide by the determinant.
What is the zero matrix? ~~ A matrix in which every element is 0.
Multiply (1 2; 3 4)(0 1; 1 0). ~~ (2 1; 4 3).
Find the determinant of (3 1; 5 2). ~~ 6 − 5 = 1.
Find the inverse of (3 1; 5 2). ~~ (2 −1; −5 3).
For what value of k is (k 2; 3 6) singular? ~~ 6k − 6 = 0, so k = 1.
What is A² for a matrix A? ~~ A × A (only defined for square matrices).
What does it mean if AB = I? ~~ B is the inverse of A (and A is the inverse of B).
How is a system of linear equations consistent? ~~ When it has at least one solution.
What is a minor in a 3×3 determinant? ~~ The determinant of the 2×2 matrix left after deleting a row and column.
What is a cofactor? ~~ A minor multiplied by (−1)^(i+j).
If det A = 3, what is det(2A) for a 3×3 matrix? ~~ 2³ × 3 = 24.
If det A = 5, what is det(A⁻¹)? ~~ 1/5.
How do you check a matrix inverse is correct? ~~ Multiply by the original matrix and check the result is I.

## CP1.7
What transformation does (1 0; 0 −1) represent? ~~ A reflection in the x-axis.
What transformation does (−1 0; 0 1) represent? ~~ A reflection in the y-axis.
What transformation does (0 1; 1 0) represent? ~~ A reflection in the line y = x.
What matrix represents an anticlockwise rotation by θ about the origin? ~~ (cos θ −sin θ; sin θ cos θ).
What matrix represents an enlargement by scale factor k about the origin? ~~ (k 0; 0 k).
What matrix represents a stretch parallel to the x-axis, scale factor k? ~~ (k 0; 0 1).
What does the matrix (1 k; 0 1) represent? ~~ A shear with the x-axis invariant: each point moves parallel to the x-axis by k times its y-coordinate.
How do you find the image of a point under a transformation? ~~ Multiply the transformation matrix by the position vector of the point.
How do you find the matrix of a transformation? ~~ Find the images of the unit vectors i and j (and k); these form the columns.
What is the matrix for a combined transformation "A then B"? ~~ BA (the first transformation is on the right).
What is an invariant point? ~~ A point that maps to itself under a transformation.
What is an invariant line? ~~ A line where every point on it maps to a point on the same line.
What is a line of invariant points? ~~ A line where every point maps to itself.
How do you find invariant points of a matrix M? ~~ Solve M(x, y) = (x, y).
How do you find invariant lines y = mx + c? ~~ Map a general point (x, mx + c) and require the image to satisfy the same equation, for all x.
What is the area of an image under transformation M? ~~ |det M| × original area.
What does a negative determinant tell you about a transformation? ~~ It reverses orientation (includes a reflection).
What matrix represents a reflection in y = −x? ~~ (0 −1; −1 0).
What matrix represents a rotation of 90° anticlockwise about the origin? ~~ (0 −1; 1 0).
What matrix represents a rotation of 180° about the origin? ~~ (−1 0; 0 −1).
What matrix represents a rotation about the z-axis in 3D? ~~ (cos θ −sin θ 0; sin θ cos θ 0; 0 0 1).
What matrix represents a reflection in the plane z = 0? ~~ (1 0 0; 0 1 0; 0 0 −1).
How do you reverse a transformation? ~~ Apply the inverse matrix.
Why is a singular matrix transformation not reversible? ~~ It collapses the plane onto a line or point, so information is lost.
What does a rotation matrix's determinant equal? ~~ 1.
What does a reflection matrix's determinant equal? ~~ −1.
Find the image of (2, 3) under (1 2; 0 1). ~~ (2 + 6, 3) = (8, 3).
What does (2 0; 0 3) do to a unit square's area? ~~ It multiplies it by 6.
What is the matrix for a rotation of θ clockwise? ~~ (cos θ sin θ; −sin θ cos θ).
What is a shear's effect on area? ~~ None: a shear has determinant 1.

## CP1.8
What are the steps of proof by induction? ~~ Basis (show true for n = 1), assumption (assume true for n = k), inductive step (show true for n = k + 1), conclusion.
What should the conclusion of an induction proof state? ~~ If true for n = k, it is true for n = k + 1; since it is true for n = 1, it is true for all positive integers n.
Prove Σ r from 1 to n = n(n + 1)/2 by induction: what is the inductive step? ~~ Assume Σ to k = k(k + 1)/2. Then Σ to k + 1 = k(k + 1)/2 + (k + 1) = (k + 1)(k + 2)/2.
How is induction used for divisibility? ~~ Show f(1) is divisible by m, then write f(k + 1) in terms of f(k) plus a multiple of m.
Prove 6ⁿ − 1 is divisible by 5 for n ≥ 1. ~~ n = 1: 5. Assume 6ᵏ − 1 = 5m. Then 6^(k+1) − 1 = 6(6ᵏ − 1) + 5 = 30m + 5, divisible by 5.
Prove 3^(2n) − 1 is divisible by 8. ~~ n = 1: 8. Assume 9ᵏ − 1 = 8m. Then 9^(k+1) − 1 = 9(9ᵏ − 1) + 8 = 72m + 8.
How is induction used for matrices? ~~ Show the formula for Mⁿ holds for n = 1, then multiply Mᵏ by M to get the formula for M^(k+1).
Prove by induction that (1 1; 0 1)ⁿ = (1 n; 0 1). ~~ n = 1 is true. Assume true for k; then (1 k; 0 1)(1 1; 0 1) = (1 k + 1; 0 1).
How is induction used for recurrence relations? ~~ Show the closed form matches u₁, then use the recurrence with the assumption to show it holds for u_(k+1).
Prove uₙ = 2ⁿ − 1 if u₁ = 1, u_(n+1) = 2uₙ + 1. ~~ n = 1: 1. Assume u_k = 2ᵏ − 1. Then u_(k+1) = 2(2ᵏ − 1) + 1 = 2^(k+1) − 1.
How is induction used for derivatives? ~~ Show the formula for the n-th derivative holds for n = 1, then differentiate the k-th to get the (k + 1)-th.
Prove that d^n/dx^n (x eˣ) = (x + n)eˣ. ~~ n = 1: (x + 1)eˣ. Assume true for k; differentiating (x + k)eˣ gives (x + k + 1)eˣ.
Why is the basis step necessary? ~~ Without it, the chain of implications has no starting point.
Why is the inductive step alone not enough? ~~ It only shows "if true for k then true for k + 1", not that it is ever true.
Can induction start from a value other than 1? ~~ Yes; the basis can be any integer, and the result holds for all integers from there.
What is a common error in induction proofs? ~~ Assuming the result for k + 1 instead of deriving it, or omitting the conclusion.
Prove Σ r² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6: what must be shown in the inductive step? ~~ k(k + 1)(2k + 1)/6 + (k + 1)² = (k + 1)(k + 2)(2k + 3)/6.
Prove that 2ⁿ > n for all n ≥ 1. ~~ n = 1: 2 > 1. Assume 2ᵏ > k. Then 2^(k+1) = 2 × 2ᵏ > 2k ≥ k + 1 for k ≥ 1.
How is induction used for inequalities? ~~ Use the assumption and then show the next case satisfies the inequality, often using extra algebraic steps.
Prove n³ − n is divisible by 6 using induction. ~~ n = 1: 0. (k + 1)³ − (k + 1) = (k³ − k) + 3k(k + 1); k(k + 1) is even, so 3k(k + 1) is a multiple of 6.
What is the key idea of induction? ~~ Like dominoes: if the first falls and each one knocks over the next, all fall.
What is proof by exhaustion compared with induction? ~~ Exhaustion checks a finite number of cases; induction proves infinitely many.
Can induction prove statements about all real numbers? ~~ No, only about integers (or well-ordered sets).
Prove 7ⁿ − 3ⁿ is divisible by 4. ~~ n = 1: 4. 7^(k+1) − 3^(k+1) = 7(7ᵏ − 3ᵏ) + 4 × 3ᵏ, divisible by 4.
What should you write for the assumption step? ~~ "Assume the statement is true for n = k", then write out the statement with k.
How do you prove a sum formula for Σ r(r + 1)? ~~ Basis with n = 1, assume for k, add (k + 1)(k + 2), and factorise.
Why must the final result be clearly shown to match the formula with k + 1? ~~ To demonstrate that the statement holds for n = k + 1.
What does "for all n ∈ ℤ⁺" mean? ~~ For all positive integers n.

## CP2.8
What is simple harmonic motion as a differential equation? ~~ d²x/dt² = −ω²x.
What is the general solution of d²x/dt² = −ω²x? ~~ x = A cos ωt + B sin ωt.
What is damped harmonic motion? ~~ d²x/dt² + k dx/dt + ω²x = 0, where the damping term reduces the amplitude.
What is heavy damping? ~~ The auxiliary equation has two distinct real (negative) roots; the system returns to equilibrium slowly without oscillating.
What is critical damping? ~~ The auxiliary equation has a repeated root; the system returns to equilibrium fastest without oscillating.
What is light damping? ~~ The auxiliary equation has complex roots; the system oscillates with decreasing amplitude.
What is forced oscillation? ~~ An oscillating system with an external driving force: x'' + kx' + ω²x = f(t).
What is resonance in DEs? ~~ When the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, amplitude grows (the particular integral includes t).
How is population growth with limits modelled? ~~ dP/dt = kP(1 − P/K), the logistic equation.
How is predator–prey interaction modelled? ~~ Using coupled first-order differential equations for each population.
What is the general strategy for coupled DEs? ~~ Eliminate one variable by differentiating to give a second-order DE in the other.
What does the steady state of a model mean? ~~ The long-term behaviour, e.g. when derivatives are zero.
What is the model for Newton's law of cooling? ~~ dT/dt = −k(T − T_s).
How is a mixing problem modelled? ~~ Rate of change of amount = rate in − rate out, where rate out depends on the concentration.
Set up a DE: a tank contains 100 L of water with 10 kg salt; brine at 0.5 kg/L enters at 2 L/min and mixture leaves at 2 L/min. ~~ dS/dt = 1 − 2S/100 = 1 − S/50.
What is the solution of dS/dt = 1 − S/50 with S(0) = 10? ~~ S = 50 − 40e^(−t/50).
How is air resistance included in a motion model? ~~ Add a resistive term such as −kv or −kv² to the force equation.
Solve m dv/dt = mg − kv for v with v(0) = 0. ~~ v = (mg/k)(1 − e^(−kt/m)).
What is the terminal velocity in m dv/dt = mg − kv? ~~ mg/k.
How can you evaluate a DE model? ~~ Compare predictions with data, check long-term behaviour, and assess assumptions.
What is the period of SHM x'' = −ω²x? ~~ 2π/ω.
How do you find the amplitude of x = 3 cos 2t + 4 sin 2t? ~~ √(9 + 16) = 5.
What does a negative coefficient in the auxiliary roots imply for long-term behaviour? ~~ Solutions decay to zero (the system is stable).
What does a positive real root imply for long-term behaviour? ~~ Solutions grow without limit (the system is unstable).
How is an electrical circuit modelled by a DE? ~~ E.g. L d²q/dt² + R dq/dt + q/C = E(t), analogous to damped oscillation.
What is a limitation of the logistic model? ~~ It assumes a constant carrying capacity and growth rate.
In a predator–prey model, what does x' = ax − bxy represent? ~~ Prey grow at rate a and are eaten at a rate proportional to encounters xy.
How do you interpret the constants in a model? ~~ Relate each to a physical quantity or rate, e.g. k as a damping coefficient.
What does it mean if a model gives negative populations? ~~ The model has broken down; it is only valid over a limited range.
How can initial conditions be used in modelling DEs? ~~ They determine the constants and give the specific solution for the situation.
