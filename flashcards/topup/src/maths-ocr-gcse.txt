What is the area of a parallelogram? ~~ Base × perpendicular height.
What is the area of a trapezium? ~~ ½(a + b)h.
What is the area of a circle? ~~ πr².
What is the circumference of a circle? ~~ 2πr (or πd).
Find the area of a circle with radius 5 cm. ~~ 25π ≈ 78.5 cm².
Find the circumference of a circle with diameter 10 cm. ~~ 10π ≈ 31.4 cm.
What is the arc length of a sector? ~~ (θ/360) × 2πr.
What is the area of a sector? ~~ (θ/360) × πr².
Find the area of a sector with radius 6 cm and angle 60°. ~~ (1/6) × 36π = 6π ≈ 18.8 cm².
What is the volume of a cuboid? ~~ Length × width × height.
What is the volume of a prism? ~~ Area of cross-section × length.
What is the volume of a cylinder? ~~ πr²h.
What is the volume of a cone? ~~ (1/3)πr²h.
What is the volume of a sphere? ~~ (4/3)πr³.
What is the volume of a pyramid? ~~ (1/3) × base area × height.
What is the surface area of a sphere? ~~ 4πr².
What is the curved surface area of a cone? ~~ πrl, where l is the slant height.
What is the curved surface area of a cylinder? ~~ 2πrh.
Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. ~~ 90π ≈ 283 cm³.
Find the volume of a sphere with radius 3 cm. ~~ 36π ≈ 113 cm³.
What is perimeter? ~~ The total distance around the outside of a shape.
Find the perimeter of a semicircle with diameter 8 cm. ~~ 4π + 8 ≈ 20.6 cm.
What is the surface area of a cuboid? ~~ 2(lw + lh + wh).
What is a frustum? ~~ A cone with the top cut off parallel to the base.
How do you find the volume of a frustum? ~~ Volume of the large cone − volume of the small cone.
How do you find the area of a compound shape? ~~ Split it into simpler shapes and add (or subtract) their areas.
Convert 3.5 litres to cm³. ~~ 3500 cm³.
What is a net? ~~ A 2D shape that folds up to make a 3D shape.

## 4.3
What is a translation? ~~ Moving a shape without rotating or reflecting it, described by a vector.
Describe the translation by the vector (3, −2). ~~ 3 units right and 2 units down.
What is a reflection? ~~ Flipping a shape in a mirror line.
What do you need to describe a reflection? ~~ The equation of the mirror line.
What is a rotation? ~~ Turning a shape about a fixed point.
What do you need to describe a rotation? ~~ The angle, the direction and the centre of rotation.
What is an enlargement? ~~ Changing the size of a shape by a scale factor from a centre of enlargement.
What do you need to describe an enlargement? ~~ The scale factor and the centre of enlargement.
What happens with a scale factor of 1/2? ~~ The shape becomes half the size.
What happens with a negative scale factor? ~~ The image is on the opposite side of the centre and is inverted.
What is an invariant point? ~~ A point that doesn't move under a transformation.
Which transformations produce congruent images? ~~ Translations, reflections and rotations.
Which transformation produces a similar image? ~~ Enlargement.
Reflect (2, 5) in the x-axis. ~~ (2, −5).
Reflect (2, 5) in the y-axis. ~~ (−2, 5).
Reflect (2, 5) in the line y = x. ~~ (5, 2).
Rotate (3, 1) 90° clockwise about the origin. ~~ (1, −3).
Rotate (3, 1) 180° about the origin. ~~ (−3, −1).
Enlarge (2, 3) by scale factor 2 from the origin. ~~ (4, 6).
What is a combined transformation? ~~ Two or more transformations applied one after the other.
How do you find the centre of rotation? ~~ Draw perpendicular bisectors of lines joining corresponding points; they meet at the centre.
How do you find the centre of enlargement? ~~ Draw lines through corresponding points; they meet at the centre.
How do you find a scale factor? ~~ Divide an image length by the corresponding object length.
What happens to area under an enlargement of scale factor 3? ~~ It is multiplied by 9.
What is the equation of the line x = 2? ~~ A vertical line through x = 2.
What is the equation of the line y = −1? ~~ A horizontal line through y = −1.
What does a reflection in y = x do to coordinates? ~~ Swaps x and y.
What is the order of rotational symmetry of a square? ~~ 4.
How many lines of symmetry does a regular pentagon have? ~~ 5.
Does a rotation change the size of a shape? ~~ No.

## 4.4
What is Pythagoras' theorem? ~~ In a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c², where c is the hypotenuse.
Find the hypotenuse of a right triangle with sides 6 and 8. ~~ √100 = 10.
Find the missing side of a right triangle with hypotenuse 13 and one side 5. ~~ √(169 − 25) = 12.
What are SOH CAH TOA? ~~ sin = opposite/hypotenuse, cos = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan = opposite/adjacent.
Find x if sin x = 0.5. ~~ x = 30°.
Find the opposite side if hypotenuse = 10 and angle = 30°. ~~ 10 sin 30° = 5.
Find the angle if opposite = 7 and adjacent = 7. ~~ tan⁻¹(1) = 45°.
What is the sine rule? ~~ a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C.
What is the cosine rule? ~~ a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A.
What is the area of a triangle using sine? ~~ ½ab sin C.
What are the exact values of sin 30°, cos 30° and tan 30°? ~~ 1/2, √3/2 and 1/√3.
What are the exact values of sin 45°, cos 45° and tan 45°? ~~ √2/2, √2/2 and 1.
What are the exact values of sin 60°, cos 60° and tan 60°? ~~ √3/2, 1/2 and √3.
What is sin 0° and cos 0°? ~~ 0 and 1.
What is sin 90° and cos 90°? ~~ 1 and 0.
What is the angle of elevation? ~~ The angle measured upwards from the horizontal.
What is the angle of depression? ~~ The angle measured downwards from the horizontal.
How do you use Pythagoras in 3D? ~~ Find the diagonal of a cuboid: d² = a² + b² + c².
Find the diagonal of a 3 × 4 × 12 cuboid. ~~ √(9 + 16 + 144) = 13.
How do you find the angle between a line and a plane? ~~ Find the projection of the line onto the plane, then use trigonometry in the right-angled triangle.
When do you use the sine rule? ~~ When you have a side and its opposite angle, plus one more side or angle.
When do you use the cosine rule? ~~ When you have two sides and the included angle, or all three sides.
Find c if a = 5, b = 7 and C = 60°. ~~ c² = 25 + 49 − 35 = 39, so c ≈ 6.24.
Find the area of a triangle with sides 8 cm and 5 cm and included angle 30°. ~~ ½ × 8 × 5 × 0.5 = 10 cm².
What is a Pythagorean triple? ~~ Three whole numbers satisfying a² + b² = c², e.g. 3, 4, 5.
How do you check if a triangle is right-angled? ~~ Check whether a² + b² = c² for the longest side c.
Find the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6). ~~ √(9 + 16) = 5.
What is the hypotenuse? ~~ The longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle.
A ladder 5 m long reaches 4 m up a wall. How far is its foot from the wall? ~~ 3 m.
What is the ambiguous case of the sine rule? ~~ When finding an angle, there may be two possible answers (acute and obtuse).

## 4.5
What is the radius of a circle? ~~ The distance from the centre to the edge.
What is the diameter? ~~ A straight line through the centre from edge to edge, twice the radius.
What is a chord? ~~ A straight line joining two points on the circle.
What is a tangent? ~~ A line that touches the circle at one point.
What is an arc? ~~ Part of the circumference.
What is a sector? ~~ The region between two radii and an arc.
What is a segment? ~~ The region between a chord and an arc.
What is the angle at the centre theorem? ~~ The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference from the same arc.
What is the angle in a semicircle theorem? ~~ The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
What is the angles in the same segment theorem? ~~ Angles subtended by the same arc at the circumference are equal.
What is the cyclic quadrilateral theorem? ~~ Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
What is the tangent–radius theorem? ~~ A tangent meets a radius at 90°.
What is the two tangents theorem? ~~ Tangents from an external point to a circle are equal in length.
What is the alternate segment theorem? ~~ The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
What is the perpendicular bisector of a chord theorem? ~~ The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
How do you construct a perpendicular bisector? ~~ With compasses set more than half the line's length, draw arcs from each end above and below, then join the intersections.
How do you construct an angle bisector? ~~ Draw an arc from the vertex crossing both arms, then arcs from those points, and join the vertex to the intersection.
How do you construct a perpendicular from a point to a line? ~~ Draw an arc from the point cutting the line twice, then bisect between those points.
How do you construct a 60° angle? ~~ Draw an equilateral triangle using compasses.
How do you construct a 90° angle? ~~ Construct a perpendicular bisector or a perpendicular at a point.
What is a locus? ~~ A set of points satisfying a rule.
What is the locus of points a fixed distance from a point? ~~ A circle.
What is the locus of points equidistant from two points? ~~ The perpendicular bisector of the line joining them.
What is the locus of points equidistant from two lines? ~~ The angle bisector of the lines.
What is the locus of points a fixed distance from a line? ~~ Two parallel lines (with semicircles at the ends for a line segment).
How do you construct a triangle given three sides? ~~ Draw one side, then use compasses to draw arcs from each end with the other side lengths; they meet at the third vertex.
What is the equation of a circle centred at the origin? ~~ x² + y² = r².
What is a cyclic quadrilateral? ~~ A quadrilateral with all four vertices on a circle.
Why must you give reasons in circle theorem questions? ~~ To show which theorems justify each step.
What is the angle between a tangent and a diameter at the point of contact? ~~ 90°.

## 4.6
What is a vector? ~~ A quantity with magnitude and direction.
How is a vector written? ~~ As a column vector, a bold letter (a), or with an arrow (AB).
What is the column vector from (1, 2) to (4, 7)? ~~ (3, 5).
Add the vectors (2, 3) and (4, −1). ~~ (6, 2).
Subtract (1, 5) from (4, 2). ~~ (3, −3).
Multiply (3, −2) by 4. ~~ (12, −8).
What is the magnitude of the vector (3, 4)? ~~ 5.
What does −a mean? ~~ A vector with the same magnitude as a but in the opposite direction.
When are two vectors parallel? ~~ When one is a multiple of the other.
If OA = a and OB = b, what is AB? ~~ b − a.
If M is the midpoint of AB, what is OM? ~~ ½(a + b).
How do you show three points are collinear using vectors? ~~ Show two vectors between them are parallel and share a point.
What is a resultant vector? ~~ The sum of two or more vectors.
If AB = 2a + b and CD = 4a + 2b, are AB and CD parallel? ~~ Yes, CD = 2AB.
What does 3a represent? ~~ A vector in the same direction as a, three times as long.
How is a translation described using a vector? ~~ By a column vector showing movement in x and y.
What is the zero vector? ~~ A vector with magnitude 0.
If a = (2, 1) and b = (−1, 3), find 2a + b. ~~ (3, 5).
Find the magnitude of (−5, 12). ~~ 13.
How do vectors help with geometric proofs? ~~ By expressing sides as combinations of vectors and showing relationships like parallel lines.
What is a position vector? ~~ A vector from the origin to a point.
What is the position vector of (3, −2)? ~~ (3, −2).
If P divides AB in the ratio 1 : 2, what is AP? ~~ (1/3)AB.
What does it mean if AB = CD? ~~ The vectors are equal: the same magnitude and direction.
How do you find the vector from B to A? ~~ a − b.
What is a scalar? ~~ A quantity with magnitude only, e.g. 3.
Can you add a scalar and a vector? ~~ No.
What does a vector (0, −4) represent? ~~ A movement 4 units down.
How do you write a route through a shape in vectors? ~~ Add the vectors along each step of the route.
What is the difference between a vector and a coordinate? ~~ A coordinate is a position; a vector is a movement or direction.

## 5.1
What is probability? ~~ A measure of how likely an event is, from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).
What is the probability of an event not happening? ~~ 1 − P(event).
What is the probability of rolling a 6 on a fair die? ~~ 1/6.
What is the probability of picking a red card from a standard deck? ~~ 26/52 = 1/2.
What is relative frequency? ~~ The number of times an event happens ÷ the number of trials.
What is expected frequency? ~~ Probability × number of trials.
A coin is flipped 200 times. How many heads are expected? ~~ 100.
What is a sample space? ~~ A list of all possible outcomes.
What are mutually exclusive events? ~~ Events that can't happen at the same time.
What is P(A or B) for mutually exclusive events? ~~ P(A) + P(B).
What are independent events? ~~ Events where one doesn't affect the probability of the other.
What is P(A and B) for independent events? ~~ P(A) × P(B).
What is the probability of two heads when flipping two coins? ~~ 1/4.
What is a tree diagram? ~~ A diagram showing all outcomes of combined events, with probabilities on branches.
How do you use a tree diagram? ~~ Multiply along branches for "and"; add the results of different paths for "or".
What is conditional probability? ~~ The probability of an event given that another has happened.
A bag has 3 red and 2 blue balls. Two are taken without replacement. Find P(both red). ~~ 3/5 × 2/4 = 3/10.
What is a Venn diagram? ~~ A diagram of overlapping circles showing sets and their intersections.
What is A ∩ B? ~~ The intersection: elements in both A and B.
What is A ∪ B? ~~ The union: elements in A or B or both.
What is A'? ~~ The complement: elements not in A.
What is a two-way table? ~~ A table showing frequencies for two categories.
What does a larger number of trials do to relative frequency? ~~ It becomes a better estimate of the true probability.
What does "fair" mean in probability? ~~ All outcomes are equally likely.
What is a frequency tree? ~~ A tree diagram showing frequencies rather than probabilities.
How many outcomes are there when rolling two dice? ~~ 36.
What is P(sum of two dice = 7)? ~~ 6/36 = 1/6.
What is P(at least one six) when rolling two dice? ~~ 1 − (5/6)² = 11/36.
What is the probability of an impossible event? ~~ 0.
If P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.5 and they are mutually exclusive, find P(neither). ~~ 1 − 0.8 = 0.2.

## 6.1
What is the mean? ~~ The sum of the values divided by the number of values.
What is the median? ~~ The middle value when the data are in order.
What is the mode? ~~ The most common value.
What is the range? ~~ The largest value minus the smallest value.
Find the mean of 3, 5, 7, 9, 11. ~~ 35 ÷ 5 = 7.
Find the median of 4, 8, 1, 9, 6, 3. ~~ Ordered: 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9; median = 5.
How do you find the mean from a frequency table? ~~ Σ(value × frequency) ÷ Σfrequency.
How do you estimate the mean from grouped data? ~~ Use the midpoint of each class × frequency, then divide by the total frequency.
Why is the mean from grouped data an estimate? ~~ The exact values aren't known; midpoints are used.
What is the modal class? ~~ The class with the highest frequency.
When is the median better than the mean? ~~ When there are outliers or skewed data.
What is an outlier? ~~ A value much higher or lower than the rest of the data.
What is a bar chart? ~~ A chart with bars of equal width showing frequencies of categories.
What is a pie chart? ~~ A circle divided into sectors showing proportions.
How do you find the angle for a pie chart sector? ~~ (Frequency ÷ total) × 360°.
What is a pictogram? ~~ A chart using symbols to represent frequencies.
What is a stem-and-leaf diagram? ~~ A diagram that splits each value into a stem and a leaf, keeping the original data.
What is a frequency polygon? ~~ A line graph joining the midpoints of classes plotted against frequency.
What is a scatter graph? ~~ A graph plotting pairs of values to show a relationship.
What is positive correlation? ~~ As one variable increases, the other increases.
What is negative correlation? ~~ As one variable increases, the other decreases.
What is a line of best fit? ~~ A straight line drawn through the middle of the points on a scatter graph.
What is interpolation? ~~ Estimating within the range of the data.
What is extrapolation? ~~ Estimating outside the range of the data, which is less reliable.
Does correlation mean causation? ~~ No; a third factor may cause both.
What is a time series graph? ~~ A line graph showing how data change over time.
What is primary data? ~~ Data you collect yourself.
What is secondary data? ~~ Data collected by someone else.
What is a sample? ~~ A smaller group chosen to represent a population.
What is a biased sample? ~~ A sample that doesn't represent the population fairly.

## 6.2
What is cumulative frequency? ~~ A running total of frequencies.
How do you plot a cumulative frequency graph? ~~ Plot cumulative frequency against the upper class boundary, and join with a smooth curve.
How do you find the median from a cumulative frequency graph? ~~ Read across from half the total frequency.
How do you find the lower quartile? ~~ Read across from a quarter of the total frequency.
How do you find the upper quartile? ~~ Read across from three quarters of the total frequency.
What is the interquartile range? ~~ Upper quartile − lower quartile.
Why is the IQR useful? ~~ It measures the spread of the middle 50%, unaffected by outliers.
What is a box plot? ~~ A diagram showing the minimum, lower quartile, median, upper quartile and maximum.
How do you compare two box plots? ~~ Compare the medians (average) and the IQRs or ranges (spread), in context.
What is a histogram? ~~ A chart for continuous data where the area of each bar represents frequency.
What is frequency density? ~~ Frequency ÷ class width.
Why are histograms used for unequal class widths? ~~ Frequency density adjusts for the different widths so the areas represent frequencies.
Find the frequency density for a class 10 < x ≤ 30 with frequency 40. ~~ 40 ÷ 20 = 2.
How do you find frequency from a histogram? ~~ Frequency = frequency density × class width.
How do you estimate the median from a histogram? ~~ Find the value that splits the total area in half.
What does a steep part of a cumulative frequency curve show? ~~ Many values in that range.
What does a flat part of a cumulative frequency curve show? ~~ Few values in that range.
How do you estimate the number above a value from a cumulative frequency graph? ~~ Read the cumulative frequency at that value and subtract from the total.
What does a longer whisker in a box plot show? ~~ More spread in that part of the data.
What is a symmetrical distribution on a box plot? ~~ The median is in the middle of the box, and the whiskers are equal.
What does positive skew look like on a box plot? ~~ The median is closer to the lower quartile, and the upper whisker is longer.
How do you find the class width of 20 ≤ x < 35? ~~ 15.
What is the total frequency if a histogram has areas 10, 20 and 30? ~~ 60.
Why can't you draw a histogram with frequency on the y-axis for unequal widths? ~~ It would misrepresent the data, as wider classes would look larger.
How do you compare distributions from cumulative frequency graphs? ~~ Compare medians and IQRs.
What is the median position for n values? ~~ The (n + 1)/2 th value.
How do you find quartiles for a small data set? ~~ Find the medians of the lower and upper halves.
What does the IQR tell you about consistency? ~~ A smaller IQR means more consistent data.
What is an outlier on a box plot? ~~ A value more than 1.5 × IQR beyond the quartiles.
What is the difference between a bar chart and a histogram? ~~ Bar charts have gaps and show categories; histograms show continuous data with no gaps and use area for frequency.
