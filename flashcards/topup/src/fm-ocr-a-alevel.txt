## 1.1
What is i? ~~ The imaginary unit, defined by i² = −1.
What is a complex number? ~~ A number of the form z = a + bi, where a and b are real.
What are Re(z) and Im(z) for z = 3 − 4i? ~~ Re(z) = 3 and Im(z) = −4.
What is the complex conjugate of z = a + bi? ~~ z* = a − bi.
Simplify i³ and i⁴. ~~ i³ = −i and i⁴ = 1.
Add (2 + 3i) + (4 − i). ~~ 6 + 2i.
Multiply (2 + 3i)(1 − i). ~~ 2 − 2i + 3i − 3i² = 5 + i.
What is z z* for z = a + bi? ~~ a² + b², which is always real and non-negative.
How do you divide complex numbers? ~~ Multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.
Calculate (3 + i)/(1 − i). ~~ (3 + i)(1 + i)/2 = (3 + 4i + i²)/2 = (2 + 4i)/2 = 1 + 2i.
Solve z² + 4 = 0. ~~ z = ±2i.
Solve z² − 2z + 5 = 0. ~~ z = (2 ± √(4 − 20))/2 = 1 ± 2i.
What is an Argand diagram? ~~ A diagram representing complex numbers as points, with the real part on the horizontal axis and the imaginary part on the vertical axis.
How is z* shown on an Argand diagram? ~~ As the reflection of z in the real axis.
What does |z − a| represent? ~~ The distance from z to the point a on the Argand diagram.
Describe the locus |z − 2 − i| = 3. ~~ A circle with centre (2, 1) and radius 3.
Describe the locus |z − 1| = |z + i|. ~~ The perpendicular bisector of the points 1 and −i.
Describe the locus arg(z − 1) = π/4. ~~ A half-line from (1, 0) at angle π/4 to the positive real axis, not including the point 1.
What does |z| < 2 represent? ~~ The interior of a circle centred at the origin with radius 2.
Describe the region |z − 3| ≤ |z + 1|. ~~ The points closer to 3 than to −1: Re(z) ≥ 1.
What does arg z = θ represent? ~~ A half-line from the origin at angle θ.
What is Euler's formula? ~~ e^(iθ) = cos θ + i sin θ.
What is the exponential form of a complex number? ~~ z = re^(iθ).
What is e^(iπ)? ~~ −1.
State de Moivre's theorem. ~~ [r(cos θ + i sin θ)]ⁿ = rⁿ(cos nθ + i sin nθ).
Calculate (1 + i)⁸ using de Moivre. ~~ (√2)⁸(cos 2π + i sin 2π) = 16.
How can de Moivre express cos 3θ in terms of cos θ? ~~ Expand (cos θ + i sin θ)³ and take the real part: cos 3θ = 4cos³θ − 3cos θ.
Express sin 3θ in terms of sin θ. ~~ sin 3θ = 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ.
What is z + 1/z if z = e^(iθ)? ~~ 2 cos θ.
What is z − 1/z if z = e^(iθ)? ~~ 2i sin θ.

## 1.2
What is the order of a matrix? ~~ The number of rows × the number of columns.
When can two matrices be multiplied? ~~ When the number of columns in the first equals the number of rows in the second.
Is matrix multiplication commutative? ~~ No; in general AB ≠ BA.
Is matrix multiplication associative? ~~ Yes: (AB)C = A(BC).
What is the identity matrix I? ~~ A square matrix with 1s on the leading diagonal and 0s elsewhere; AI = IA = A.
What is the determinant of a 2×2 matrix (a b; c d)? ~~ ad − bc.
What is the inverse of a 2×2 matrix (a b; c d)? ~~ (1/(ad − bc)) × (d −b; −c a).
What is a singular matrix? ~~ A matrix with determinant zero, which has no inverse.
What is (AB)⁻¹? ~~ B⁻¹A⁻¹.
How do you find the determinant of a 3×3 matrix? ~~ Expand along a row or column using cofactors: a(ei − fh) − b(di − fg) + c(dh − eg).
What does the determinant of a transformation matrix represent? ~~ The area (2D) or volume (3D) scale factor; a negative value means orientation is reversed.
How can matrices solve simultaneous equations? ~~ Write as AX = B, then X = A⁻¹B if A is non-singular.
What does det A = 0 mean for a system of 3 equations? ~~ There is no unique solution: either no solutions (inconsistent) or infinitely many.
How do three planes with no unique solution appear geometrically? ~~ As a sheaf (common line), a prism (no common point), or with parallel planes.
What is det(AB)? ~~ det A × det B.
What is the transpose of a matrix? ~~ The matrix formed by swapping rows and columns, Aᵀ.
How do you find the inverse of a 3×3 matrix? ~~ Find the matrix of cofactors, transpose it (adjugate), then divide by the determinant.
What is the zero matrix? ~~ A matrix in which every element is 0.
What transformation does (1 0; 0 −1) represent? ~~ A reflection in the x-axis.
What transformation does (−1 0; 0 1) represent? ~~ A reflection in the y-axis.
What transformation does (0 1; 1 0) represent? ~~ A reflection in the line y = x.
What matrix represents an anticlockwise rotation by θ about the origin? ~~ (cos θ −sin θ; sin θ cos θ).
What matrix represents an enlargement by scale factor k about the origin? ~~ (k 0; 0 k).
What matrix represents a stretch parallel to the x-axis, scale factor k? ~~ (k 0; 0 1).
What does the matrix (1 k; 0 1) represent? ~~ A shear with the x-axis invariant: each point moves parallel to the x-axis by k times its y-coordinate.
How do you find the image of a point under a transformation? ~~ Multiply the transformation matrix by the position vector of the point.
How do you find the matrix of a transformation? ~~ Find the images of the unit vectors i and j (and k); these form the columns.
What is the matrix for a combined transformation "A then B"? ~~ BA (the first transformation is on the right).
What is an invariant point? ~~ A point that maps to itself under a transformation.
What is an invariant line? ~~ A line where every point on it maps to a point on the same line.

## 1.3
What is a rational function? ~~ A function of the form f(x) = p(x)/q(x) where p and q are polynomials.
How do you find vertical asymptotes of y = p(x)/q(x)? ~~ Solve q(x) = 0 (where p(x) ≠ 0).
How do you find the horizontal asymptote of y = (ax + b)/(cx + d)? ~~ y = a/c.
What is the oblique asymptote of y = (x² + 1)/x? ~~ y = x, since y = x + 1/x.
How can you find the range of y = (x² + 1)/(x − 1)? ~~ Rearrange into a quadratic in x and use the discriminant ≥ 0 for real x.
What is y = 1/f(x) used for? ~~ Sketching the reciprocal graph: roots of f become vertical asymptotes, and maxima become minima.
How is y = |f(x)| sketched? ~~ Reflect the parts of y = f(x) below the x-axis in the x-axis.
How is y² = f(x) sketched? ~~ Plot y = ±√f(x) where f(x) ≥ 0; the graph is symmetric about the x-axis.
Solve (x + 1)/(x − 2) > 0. ~~ Critical values −1 and 2: x < −1 or x > 2.
Solve x/(x − 1) ≤ 2. ~~ (x − 2(x − 1))/(x − 1) ≤ 0 → (2 − x)/(x − 1) ≤ 0, so x < 1 or x ≥ 2.
Why can't you multiply both sides of an inequality by (x − 1)? ~~ Its sign is unknown; multiply by (x − 1)² instead, or use a sign diagram.
What is the equation of a parabola in standard form? ~~ y² = 4ax, with focus (a, 0) and directrix x = −a.
What is the equation of an ellipse centred at the origin? ~~ x²/a² + y²/b² = 1.
What is the equation of a hyperbola centred at the origin? ~~ x²/a² − y²/b² = 1, with asymptotes y = ±(b/a)x.
What is a rectangular hyperbola? ~~ xy = c², with the axes as asymptotes.
For ax² + bx + c = 0 with roots α, β, what are α + β and αβ? ~~ α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a.
For ax³ + bx² + cx + d = 0, what are Σα, Σαβ and αβγ? ~~ Σα = −b/a, Σαβ = c/a, αβγ = −d/a.
For a quartic ax⁴ + bx³ + cx² + dx + e = 0, what is the product of the roots? ~~ αβγδ = e/a.
Express α² + β² in terms of α + β and αβ. ~~ (α + β)² − 2αβ.
Express α³ + β³ in terms of α + β and αβ. ~~ (α + β)³ − 3αβ(α + β).
Express 1/α + 1/β in terms of α + β and αβ. ~~ (α + β)/αβ.
Express α² + β² + γ² for a cubic. ~~ (Σα)² − 2Σαβ.
How do you find an equation whose roots are 2α and 2β? ~~ Substitute x = w/2 into the original equation.
How do you find an equation whose roots are α + 1, β + 1? ~~ Substitute x = w − 1 into the original equation.
How do you find an equation with roots α², β² using substitution? ~~ Substitute x = √w, rearrange so √w terms are on one side, and square.
The roots of x² − 5x + 3 = 0 are α, β. Find α² + β². ~~ 25 − 6 = 19.
The roots of 2x³ − 3x² + x − 4 = 0 are α, β, γ. Find αβγ. ~~ −(−4)/2 = 2.
Find the quadratic with roots α + β and αβ given α + β = 3, αβ = 2. ~~ Roots 3 and 2: x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
If one root of x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 = 0 is 1, find the others. ~~ (x − 1)(x² − 5x + 6), so 2 and 3.
What does it mean if a cubic's roots form an arithmetic sequence? ~~ They can be written a − d, a, a + d, and Σα = 3a.

## 1.4
What is an improper integral? ~~ An integral where a limit is infinite or the integrand is undefined at a point in the range.
How do you evaluate ∫₁^∞ 1/x² dx? ~~ lim (t→∞) [−1/x]₁ᵗ = 1.
Does ∫₁^∞ 1/x dx converge? ~~ No; ln t → ∞, so it diverges.
Evaluate ∫₀¹ 1/√x dx. ~~ [2√x]₀¹ = 2 (the integral converges even though 1/√x is undefined at 0).
What is the mean value of f(x) over [a, b]? ~~ (1/(b − a)) ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx.
Find the mean value of x² over [0, 3]. ~~ (1/3) × 9 = 3.
What is ∫ 1/√(1 − x²) dx? ~~ arcsin x + c.
What is ∫ 1/(1 + x²) dx? ~~ arctan x + c.
What is ∫ 1/√(a² − x²) dx? ~~ arcsin(x/a) + c.
What is ∫ 1/(a² + x²) dx? ~~ (1/a) arctan(x/a) + c.
What is the derivative of arcsin x? ~~ 1/√(1 − x²).
What is the derivative of arccos x? ~~ −1/√(1 − x²).
What is the derivative of arctan x? ~~ 1/(1 + x²).
Integrate 1/(x² + 4). ~~ (1/2) arctan(x/2) + c.
Integrate 1/√(9 − x²). ~~ arcsin(x/3) + c.
What is the volume of revolution about the x-axis? ~~ V = π ∫ y² dx between the limits.
What is the volume of revolution about the y-axis? ~~ V = π ∫ x² dy between the y-limits.
Find the volume when y = x from 0 to 3 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀³ x² dx = 9π (a cone).
Find the volume when y = √x from 0 to 4 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀⁴ x dx = 8π.
Find the volume when y = x² from 0 to 1 is rotated about the x-axis. ~~ π ∫₀¹ x⁴ dx = π/5.
How do you find the volume between two curves rotated about the x-axis? ~~ V = π ∫ (y₁² − y₂²) dx, where y₁ is the outer curve.
Why is it not π ∫ (y₁ − y₂)² dx? ~~ The solid is a washer: the volume is the outer solid minus the inner solid.
How do you rotate y = x² about the y-axis between y = 0 and y = 4? ~~ x² = y, so V = π ∫₀⁴ y dy = 8π.
What is a Maclaurin series? ~~ f(x) = f(0) + f'(0)x + f''(0)x²/2! + f'''(0)x³/3! + …
What is the Maclaurin series for eˣ? ~~ 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + …, valid for all x.
What is the Maclaurin series for sin x? ~~ x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …, valid for all x.
What is the Maclaurin series for cos x? ~~ 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − …, valid for all x.
What is the Maclaurin series for ln(1 + x)? ~~ x − x²/2 + x³/3 − …, valid for −1 < x ≤ 1.
What is the Maclaurin series for (1 + x)ⁿ? ~~ 1 + nx + n(n − 1)x²/2! + …, valid for |x| < 1 (non-integer n).
Find the Maclaurin series for e^(2x) up to x³. ~~ 1 + 2x + 2x² + (4/3)x³.

## 1.5
What is the vector equation of a line? ~~ r = a + λb, where a is a point on the line and b is the direction vector.
What is the Cartesian equation of a line? ~~ (x − a₁)/b₁ = (y − a₂)/b₂ = (z − a₃)/b₃.
What is the scalar (dot) product? ~~ a·b = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃ = |a||b| cos θ.
What does a·b = 0 mean? ~~ The vectors are perpendicular.
How do you find the angle between two lines? ~~ Use the dot product of their direction vectors: cos θ = (b₁·b₂)/(|b₁||b₂|).
What is the vector equation of a plane? ~~ r = a + λb + μc, or r·n = a·n.
What is the Cartesian equation of a plane? ~~ n₁x + n₂y + n₃z = d, where n is the normal.
What is a normal vector to a plane? ~~ A vector perpendicular to the plane.
How do you find the angle between a line and a plane? ~~ Find the angle between the direction and the normal, then subtract from 90°.
How do you find the angle between two planes? ~~ Find the angle between their normals.
How do you find where a line meets a plane? ~~ Substitute the line's parametric form into the plane equation and solve for λ.
How do you tell if two lines are skew? ~~ They are not parallel and don't intersect.
How do you find the intersection of two lines? ~~ Set the vector equations equal and solve for the parameters, checking all three components.
What is the vector (cross) product? ~~ a × b, a vector perpendicular to both a and b with magnitude |a||b| sin θ.
How do you calculate a × b? ~~ (a₂b₃ − a₃b₂, a₃b₁ − a₁b₃, a₁b₂ − a₂b₁).
How do you find the normal to a plane containing vectors b and c? ~~ Calculate b × c.
What is the perpendicular distance from a point to a plane? ~~ |ax₀ + by₀ + cz₀ − d|/√(a² + b² + c²).
How do you find the shortest distance between skew lines? ~~ |(a₂ − a₁)·n|/|n|, where n = b₁ × b₂.
How do you find the shortest distance from a point to a line? ~~ Find the foot of the perpendicular (where (p − r)·b = 0), then find the distance.
What is the area of a triangle using vectors? ~~ ½|AB × AC|.
What is the volume of a parallelepiped? ~~ |a·(b × c)| (the scalar triple product).
What is the line of intersection of two planes? ~~ It has direction n₁ × n₂; find a point by solving the equations with one variable fixed.
How do you find the reflection of a point in a plane? ~~ Find the foot of the perpendicular, then go the same distance beyond it.
When are two planes parallel? ~~ When their normals are parallel.
What does r·n = d mean geometrically? ~~ All points whose component along n is d/|n| from the origin.
Find the angle between (1, 2, 2) and (2, −1, 2). ~~ Dot product = 2 − 2 + 4 = 4; |a| = |b| = 3; cos θ = 4/9, θ ≈ 63.6°.
How do you check a point lies on a line? ~~ Find a λ that satisfies all three components.
What is a unit normal? ~~ A normal vector divided by its magnitude.
Find the Cartesian equation of the plane with normal (2, −1, 3) through (1, 1, 1). ~~ 2x − y + 3z = 4.
What is the scalar product of a vector with itself? ~~ a·a = |a|².

## 1.6
What are polar coordinates? ~~ Coordinates (r, θ), where r is the distance from the pole and θ is the angle from the initial line.
How do you convert polar to Cartesian? ~~ x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ.
How do you convert Cartesian to polar? ~~ r² = x² + y², tan θ = y/x (taking care with the quadrant).
What is the polar equation r = a? ~~ A circle with centre the pole and radius a.
What is the polar equation θ = α? ~~ A half-line from the pole at angle α.
What is the curve r = aθ? ~~ A spiral (Archimedean spiral).
What is the curve r = a(1 + cos θ)? ~~ A cardioid.
What is the curve r = a cos 2θ? ~~ A four-petalled rose curve.
What is the curve r = 2a cos θ? ~~ A circle through the pole, with centre (a, 0) and radius a.
What is the area enclosed by a polar curve? ~~ A = ½ ∫ r² dθ between the limits.
Find the area enclosed by r = 2 for 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π. ~~ ½ ∫ 4 dθ = 4π.
Find the area of the cardioid r = 1 + cos θ. ~~ ½ ∫₀^(2π) (1 + cos θ)² dθ = 3π/2.
How do you find where two polar curves intersect? ~~ Set the r-values equal and solve for θ, also checking the pole.
How do you find a tangent parallel to the initial line? ~~ Set dy/dθ = 0, where y = r sin θ.
How do you find a tangent perpendicular to the initial line? ~~ Set dx/dθ = 0, where x = r cos θ.
Convert r = 4 sin θ to Cartesian form. ~~ r² = 4r sin θ, so x² + y² = 4y (a circle centred at (0, 2)).
Convert x² + y² = 9 to polar form. ~~ r = 3.
Convert y = x to polar form. ~~ θ = π/4 (and θ = −3π/4 for negative r).
Why is r usually taken as non-negative? ~~ By convention r ≥ 0; values of θ that give negative r are excluded from the sketch.
What does a cardioid look like? ~~ A heart shape with a cusp at the pole.
How many petals does r = cos 3θ have? ~~ 3.
How many petals does r = cos 2θ have? ~~ 4.
What is the area of one petal of r = cos 2θ? ~~ ½ ∫ from −π/4 to π/4 of cos²2θ dθ = π/8.
What identity helps integrate r² = cos²θ? ~~ cos²θ = (1 + cos 2θ)/2.
How do you sketch a polar curve? ~~ Tabulate r for key θ values, note symmetry, and find where r = 0 and r is maximum.
What does symmetry about the initial line mean for r = f(θ)? ~~ f(−θ) = f(θ), e.g. curves involving only cos θ.
How do you find the area between two polar curves? ~~ Subtract the areas: ½ ∫ (r₁² − r₂²) dθ over the appropriate range.
What is the maximum r of r = 3 + 2 cos θ? ~~ 5, when θ = 0.
Does r = 3 + 2 cos θ pass through the pole? ~~ No, since r ≥ 1 for all θ (a dimpled limaçon).
What is the pole? ~~ The origin in polar coordinates.

## 1.7
What is the definition of sinh x? ~~ sinh x = (eˣ − e^(−x))/2.
What is the definition of cosh x? ~~ cosh x = (eˣ + e^(−x))/2.
What is the definition of tanh x? ~~ tanh x = sinh x/cosh x = (eˣ − e^(−x))/(eˣ + e^(−x)).
What is the key hyperbolic identity? ~~ cosh²x − sinh²x = 1.
What is the range of cosh x? ~~ cosh x ≥ 1.
What is the range of tanh x? ~~ −1 < tanh x < 1.
What does the graph of y = cosh x look like? ~~ A U-shape (catenary) with minimum (0, 1), symmetric about the y-axis.
What does the graph of y = sinh x look like? ~~ An S-shape through the origin, increasing, and odd.
What is the derivative of sinh x? ~~ cosh x.
What is the derivative of cosh x? ~~ sinh x.
What is the derivative of tanh x? ~~ sech²x.
What is Osborn's rule? ~~ To convert a trig identity to a hyperbolic one, replace cos with cosh and sin with sinh, but change the sign of any product of two sines.
What is sinh 2x? ~~ 2 sinh x cosh x.
What is cosh 2x? ~~ cosh²x + sinh²x = 2cosh²x − 1 = 1 + 2sinh²x.
What is arsinh x in logarithmic form? ~~ ln(x + √(x² + 1)).
What is arcosh x in logarithmic form? ~~ ln(x + √(x² − 1)), for x ≥ 1.
What is artanh x in logarithmic form? ~~ ½ ln((1 + x)/(1 − x)), for |x| < 1.
What is the derivative of arsinh x? ~~ 1/√(x² + 1).
What is the derivative of arcosh x? ~~ 1/√(x² − 1).
What is the derivative of artanh x? ~~ 1/(1 − x²).
Solve cosh x = 2. ~~ x = ±ln(2 + √3).
Solve sinh x = 3/4. ~~ x = ln(3/4 + 5/4) = ln 2.
Solve 3 sinh x + cosh x = 3. ~~ In exponentials: 3(eˣ − e^(−x))/2 + (eˣ + e^(−x))/2 = 3 → 2eˣ − e^(−x) = 3 → 2e^(2x) − 3eˣ − 1 = 0, eˣ = (3 + √17)/4, x = ln((3 + √17)/4).
What is ∫ sinh x dx? ~~ cosh x + c.
What is ∫ cosh x dx? ~~ sinh x + c.
What is ∫ tanh x dx? ~~ ln cosh x + c.
What is a catenary? ~~ The shape of a hanging chain, described by y = a cosh(x/a).
Is cosh x even or odd? ~~ Even.
Is sinh x even or odd? ~~ Odd.
What is sech x? ~~ 1/cosh x.

## 1.8
What is an integrating factor? ~~ For dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x), the integrating factor is e^(∫P dx).
How do you solve dy/dx + P(x)y = Q(x)? ~~ Multiply by the integrating factor I: d(Iy)/dx = IQ, then integrate.
Solve dy/dx + 2y = e^(−x). ~~ IF = e^(2x): (ye^(2x))' = eˣ, so y = e^(−x) + Ce^(−2x).
Solve x dy/dx + y = x². ~~ (xy)' = x², so xy = x³/3 + C, y = x²/3 + C/x.
What is a second-order linear differential equation with constant coefficients? ~~ a d²y/dx² + b dy/dx + cy = f(x).
What is the auxiliary equation? ~~ am² + bm + c = 0.
What is the complementary function if the auxiliary equation has distinct real roots α, β? ~~ y = Ae^(αx) + Be^(βx).
What is the complementary function for a repeated root α? ~~ y = (A + Bx)e^(αx).
What is the complementary function for complex roots p ± qi? ~~ y = e^(px)(A cos qx + B sin qx).
What is the general solution of a non-homogeneous second-order ODE? ~~ Complementary function + particular integral.
What is a particular integral? ~~ Any solution of the full equation, found by trying a function similar to f(x).
What trial function is used for f(x) = ke^(λx)? ~~ Ae^(λx) (or Axe^(λx) if λ is a root of the auxiliary equation).
What trial function is used for f(x) = polynomial of degree n? ~~ A general polynomial of degree n.
What trial function is used for f(x) = k cos ωx? ~~ A cos ωx + B sin ωx.
What happens if the trial function is part of the complementary function? ~~ Multiply the trial by x (or x² for a repeated root).
Solve y'' − 5y' + 6y = 0. ~~ m² − 5m + 6 = 0, m = 2, 3: y = Ae^(2x) + Be^(3x).
Solve y'' + 4y = 0. ~~ m = ±2i: y = A cos 2x + B sin 2x.
Solve y'' + 2y' + y = 0. ~~ m = −1 (repeated): y = (A + Bx)e^(−x).
What is simple harmonic motion as a differential equation? ~~ d²x/dt² = −ω²x.
What is the general solution of d²x/dt² = −ω²x? ~~ x = A cos ωt + B sin ωt.
What is damped harmonic motion? ~~ d²x/dt² + k dx/dt + ω²x = 0, where the damping term reduces the amplitude.
What is heavy damping? ~~ The auxiliary equation has two distinct real (negative) roots; the system returns to equilibrium slowly without oscillating.
What is critical damping? ~~ The auxiliary equation has a repeated root; the system returns to equilibrium fastest without oscillating.
What is light damping? ~~ The auxiliary equation has complex roots; the system oscillates with decreasing amplitude.
What is forced oscillation? ~~ An oscillating system with an external driving force: x'' + kx' + ω²x = f(t).
What is resonance in DEs? ~~ When the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, amplitude grows (the particular integral includes t).
How is population growth with limits modelled? ~~ dP/dt = kP(1 − P/K), the logistic equation.
How is predator–prey interaction modelled? ~~ Using coupled first-order differential equations for each population.
What is the general strategy for coupled DEs? ~~ Eliminate one variable by differentiating to give a second-order DE in the other.
What does the steady state of a model mean? ~~ The long-term behaviour, e.g. when derivatives are zero.

## 1.9
What is Euler's method? ~~ A step-by-step method: y_(n+1) = yₙ + h f(xₙ, yₙ), for dy/dx = f(x, y).
What is the step length h? ~~ The increase in x at each step.
How does reducing h affect Euler's method? ~~ It usually improves accuracy but requires more steps.
Why does Euler's method have errors? ~~ It assumes the gradient is constant over each step.
What is the improved Euler method? ~~ Uses the average of the gradients at the start and at a predicted end point of each step.
What is the midpoint rule for integration? ~~ Approximate the area using rectangles whose heights are the function values at the midpoints of the strips.
What is Simpson's rule? ~~ ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx ≈ (h/3)[y₀ + 4(y₁ + y₃ + …) + 2(y₂ + y₄ + …) + yₙ], with n even.
Why must n be even in Simpson's rule? ~~ It fits parabolas to pairs of strips.
Why is Simpson's rule usually more accurate than the trapezium rule? ~~ It fits curves (parabolas) rather than straight lines.
What is the relative error? ~~ |approximation − exact value| ÷ |exact value|.
What is the absolute error? ~~ |approximation − exact value|.
How can you estimate the error in a numerical method? ~~ Compare results with successively smaller step sizes.
Apply one step of Euler's method to dy/dx = x + y, y(0) = 1, h = 0.1. ~~ y₁ = 1 + 0.1 × (0 + 1) = 1.1.
What is the Newton–Raphson method? ~~ xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − f(xₙ)/f'(xₙ).
What is the order of convergence of Newton–Raphson? ~~ Quadratic (the number of correct digits roughly doubles each step), near a simple root.
What is the bisection method? ~~ Repeatedly halving an interval containing a root, keeping the half with a sign change.
What is the secant method? ~~ Like Newton–Raphson, but using the gradient of the line through the two previous estimates instead of the derivative.
What is linear interpolation (false position)? ~~ Estimating a root using a straight line between two points with opposite signs.

## 1.10
What are the steps of proof by induction? ~~ Basis (show true for n = 1), assumption (assume true for n = k), inductive step (show true for n = k + 1), conclusion.
What should the conclusion of an induction proof state? ~~ If true for n = k, it is true for n = k + 1; since it is true for n = 1, it is true for all positive integers n.
Prove Σ r from 1 to n = n(n + 1)/2 by induction: what is the inductive step? ~~ Assume Σ to k = k(k + 1)/2. Then Σ to k + 1 = k(k + 1)/2 + (k + 1) = (k + 1)(k + 2)/2.
How is induction used for divisibility? ~~ Show f(1) is divisible by m, then write f(k + 1) in terms of f(k) plus a multiple of m.
Prove 6ⁿ − 1 is divisible by 5 for n ≥ 1. ~~ n = 1: 5. Assume 6ᵏ − 1 = 5m. Then 6^(k+1) − 1 = 6(6ᵏ − 1) + 5 = 30m + 5, divisible by 5.
Prove 3^(2n) − 1 is divisible by 8. ~~ n = 1: 8. Assume 9ᵏ − 1 = 8m. Then 9^(k+1) − 1 = 9(9ᵏ − 1) + 8 = 72m + 8.
How is induction used for matrices? ~~ Show the formula for Mⁿ holds for n = 1, then multiply Mᵏ by M to get the formula for M^(k+1).
Prove by induction that (1 1; 0 1)ⁿ = (1 n; 0 1). ~~ n = 1 is true. Assume true for k; then (1 k; 0 1)(1 1; 0 1) = (1 k + 1; 0 1).
How is induction used for recurrence relations? ~~ Show the closed form matches u₁, then use the recurrence with the assumption to show it holds for u_(k+1).
Prove uₙ = 2ⁿ − 1 if u₁ = 1, u_(n+1) = 2uₙ + 1. ~~ n = 1: 1. Assume u_k = 2ᵏ − 1. Then u_(k+1) = 2(2ᵏ − 1) + 1 = 2^(k+1) − 1.
How is induction used for derivatives? ~~ Show the formula for the n-th derivative holds for n = 1, then differentiate the k-th to get the (k + 1)-th.
Prove that d^n/dx^n (x eˣ) = (x + n)eˣ. ~~ n = 1: (x + 1)eˣ. Assume true for k; differentiating (x + k)eˣ gives (x + k + 1)eˣ.
Why is the basis step necessary? ~~ Without it, the chain of implications has no starting point.
Why is the inductive step alone not enough? ~~ It only shows "if true for k then true for k + 1", not that it is ever true.
Can induction start from a value other than 1? ~~ Yes; the basis can be any integer, and the result holds for all integers from there.
What is a common error in induction proofs? ~~ Assuming the result for k + 1 instead of deriving it, or omitting the conclusion.
Prove Σ r² = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6: what must be shown in the inductive step? ~~ k(k + 1)(2k + 1)/6 + (k + 1)² = (k + 1)(k + 2)(2k + 3)/6.
Prove that 2ⁿ > n for all n ≥ 1. ~~ n = 1: 2 > 1. Assume 2ᵏ > k. Then 2^(k+1) = 2 × 2ᵏ > 2k ≥ k + 1 for k ≥ 1.
How is induction used for inequalities? ~~ Use the assumption and then show the next case satisfies the inequality, often using extra algebraic steps.
Prove n³ − n is divisible by 6 using induction. ~~ n = 1: 0. (k + 1)³ − (k + 1) = (k³ − k) + 3k(k + 1); k(k + 1) is even, so 3k(k + 1) is a multiple of 6.
What is the key idea of induction? ~~ Like dominoes: if the first falls and each one knocks over the next, all fall.
What is proof by exhaustion compared with induction? ~~ Exhaustion checks a finite number of cases; induction proves infinitely many.
Can induction prove statements about all real numbers? ~~ No, only about integers (or well-ordered sets).
Prove 7ⁿ − 3ⁿ is divisible by 4. ~~ n = 1: 4. 7^(k+1) − 3^(k+1) = 7(7ᵏ − 3ᵏ) + 4 × 3ᵏ, divisible by 4.
What should you write for the assumption step? ~~ "Assume the statement is true for n = k", then write out the statement with k.
How do you prove a sum formula for Σ r(r + 1)? ~~ Basis with n = 1, assume for k, add (k + 1)(k + 2), and factorise.
Why must the final result be clearly shown to match the formula with k + 1? ~~ To demonstrate that the statement holds for n = k + 1.
What does "for all n ∈ ℤ⁺" mean? ~~ For all positive integers n.
Prove that 4ⁿ + 6n − 1 is divisible by 9. ~~ n = 1: 9. f(k + 1) − 4f(k) = 6(k + 1) − 1 − 24k + 4 = −18k + 9, a multiple of 9.
Why might f(k + 1) − f(k) be useful in divisibility proofs? ~~ If it is divisible by m and f(k) is, then so is f(k + 1).

## D1.2
What is a minimum spanning tree? ~~ A spanning tree with the smallest total edge weight.
Describe Kruskal's algorithm. ~~ Sort edges by weight; add the shortest edge that doesn't form a cycle, until all vertices are connected.
Describe Prim's algorithm. ~~ Start at any vertex; repeatedly add the shortest edge joining a vertex in the tree to one not in the tree.
Why is Prim's algorithm suited to distance matrices? ~~ It works column by column, choosing the smallest entry in the rows of vertices already in the tree.
What is the key difference between Prim's and Kruskal's? ~~ Kruskal's builds a forest that joins up; Prim's grows a single tree.
Describe Dijkstra's algorithm. ~~ Label the start 0; repeatedly give a permanent label to the vertex with the smallest temporary label, updating neighbours' temporary labels.
What does Dijkstra's algorithm find? ~~ The shortest path from a start vertex to all other vertices.
How do you trace back the shortest route in Dijkstra's? ~~ Work backwards from the end, choosing edges where the weight equals the difference in permanent labels.
What is Floyd's algorithm? ~~ An algorithm finding the shortest paths between all pairs of vertices using distance and route matrices.
What is the route inspection (Chinese postman) problem? ~~ Finding the shortest route that travels every edge at least once and returns to the start.
How do you solve the route inspection problem? ~~ Find the odd vertices, pair them to minimise the extra distance, add those paths, then total = sum of edges + extra.
How many pairings are there for 4 odd vertices? ~~ 3.
How many pairings are there for 6 odd vertices? ~~ 15.
What if the route can start and end at different vertices? ~~ Leave the pair of odd vertices with the longest connecting path unrepeated, starting and ending there.
What is the travelling salesperson problem (TSP)? ~~ Finding the shortest route visiting every vertex and returning to the start.
What is the classical TSP versus the practical TSP? ~~ Classical: each vertex visited exactly once. Practical: vertices can be revisited.
How do you find an upper bound for the TSP? ~~ Use the nearest neighbour algorithm, or double the minimum spanning tree and use shortcuts.
What is the nearest neighbour algorithm? ~~ From the current vertex, go to the nearest unvisited vertex; return to the start at the end.
How do you find a lower bound for the TSP? ~~ Delete a vertex, find the MST of the rest, then add the two shortest edges from the deleted vertex.
What is the best upper bound? ~~ The smallest upper bound found.
What is the best lower bound? ~~ The largest lower bound found.
What does it mean if the upper and lower bounds are equal? ~~ The optimal solution has been found.
What is a network flow? ~~ The movement of a quantity through a network with capacities on the arcs.
What is a cut in a network? ~~ A set of arcs whose removal separates the source from the sink.
What is the max-flow min-cut theorem? ~~ The maximum flow through a network equals the capacity of the minimum cut.
What is the feasibility condition for flows? ~~ Flow in each arc doesn't exceed capacity, and flow into each vertex equals flow out (except source and sink).
What is a supersource? ~~ An extra vertex added to connect multiple sources into one.
What is the complexity of Kruskal's algorithm? ~~ Dominated by sorting the edges: O(E log E).
What is the complexity of Dijkstra's algorithm (simple version)? ~~ O(n²) for n vertices.
What is an algorithm's order? ~~ How the run time grows with problem size, e.g. quadratic O(n²).

## D1.3
What is linear programming? ~~ Optimising a linear objective function subject to linear constraints.
What are decision variables? ~~ The variables whose values are chosen, e.g. x = number of chairs, y = number of tables.
What is the objective function? ~~ The expression to be maximised or minimised, e.g. P = 3x + 5y.
What are constraints? ~~ Linear inequalities that limit the values of the decision variables.
What are non-negativity constraints? ~~ x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0.
What is the feasible region? ~~ The region of the graph satisfying all the constraints.
How is the optimal solution found graphically? ~~ Use an objective line (ruler method) or test each vertex of the feasible region.
What is the ruler (objective line) method? ~~ Slide a line of the objective function across the feasible region; the last point it touches is optimal.
Why does the optimum occur at a vertex? ~~ The objective function is linear, so its maximum or minimum over a convex polygon is at a vertex.
What if integer solutions are required? ~~ Test integer points near the optimal vertex within the feasible region.
What is the simplex algorithm? ~~ An algebraic method that moves between vertices of the feasible region, improving the objective each step.
What is a slack variable? ~~ A variable added to a ≤ constraint to make it an equation, e.g. x + y + s = 10.
What is a surplus variable? ~~ A variable subtracted from a ≥ constraint to make it an equation.
How do you choose the pivot column in simplex? ~~ The column with the most negative value in the objective row (for maximisation).
How do you choose the pivot row? ~~ The row with the smallest non-negative ratio of the value to the pivot column entry.
When is the simplex tableau optimal? ~~ When there are no negative values in the objective row (for maximisation).
What is the two-stage simplex method? ~~ A method for problems with ≥ constraints, first minimising artificial variables to find a feasible solution.
What is the Big-M method? ~~ Adding artificial variables with a large penalty M in the objective to handle ≥ constraints.
What is an artificial variable? ~~ A variable added to ≥ or = constraints to give an initial basic feasible solution.
How do you convert a minimisation problem for simplex? ~~ Maximise the negative of the objective function.
What is a basic variable? ~~ A variable that is non-zero in the current solution (in the basis).
What is a non-basic variable? ~~ A variable set to zero in the current solution.
What does a slack variable's final value represent? ~~ The unused amount of that resource.
Formulate: each chair uses 2 h and each table 3 h, with 60 h available. ~~ 2x + 3y ≤ 60.
What is an unbounded LP problem? ~~ One where the objective can increase without limit because the feasible region is unbounded.
What does an infeasible LP problem mean? ~~ No point satisfies all the constraints.
How do you find the vertices of a feasible region? ~~ Solve pairs of boundary equations simultaneously.
What is sensitivity analysis? ~~ Examining how changes in coefficients or constraints affect the optimal solution.
What is a degenerate solution in simplex? ~~ When a basic variable has value zero, which can cause cycling.
How do you interpret the final simplex tableau? ~~ Read the basic variables' values from the value column; the objective row gives the optimal value.

## D1.4
What is a two-person zero-sum game? ~~ A game where one player's gain is exactly the other's loss.
What is a pay-off matrix? ~~ A table showing the gains for one player (usually the row player) for each combination of strategies.
What is a play-safe strategy? ~~ The strategy that maximises the worst possible outcome.
What is the row maximin? ~~ The maximum of the row minimums: the row player's play-safe value.
What is the column minimax? ~~ The minimum of the column maximums: the column player's play-safe value.
What is a stable solution (saddle point)? ~~ When the row maximin equals the column minimax; neither player can improve by changing strategy.
What is the value of the game? ~~ The expected pay-off to the row player when both play optimally.
What is a dominated strategy? ~~ A strategy that is always worse than (or no better than) another, so it can be removed.
How do you remove dominance for the row player? ~~ Remove a row if every entry is less than or equal to the corresponding entry in another row.
How do you remove dominance for the column player? ~~ Remove a column if every entry is greater than or equal to the corresponding entry in another column (from row's perspective).
What is a mixed strategy? ~~ Playing each strategy with a certain probability.
How do you solve a 2×2 game with no saddle point? ~~ Let the row player play strategy 1 with probability p, equate expected pay-offs against each column strategy, and solve for p.
How is a 2×n game solved graphically? ~~ Plot the expected pay-off lines against p and find the highest point of the lower boundary.
How is a 3×3 game solved? ~~ Convert to a linear programming problem and solve using the simplex method.
Why add a constant to all entries before using LP for games? ~~ To make all entries positive, so the value of the game is positive.
What is the column player's pay-off matrix? ~~ The negative of the row player's matrix (for a zero-sum game).
What is a Nash equilibrium? ~~ A set of strategies where no player can improve by changing their strategy alone.
Find the saddle point of (3 5; 2 1). ~~ Row minima: 3, 1 (maximin 3). Column maxima: 3, 5 (minimax 3). Saddle point at 3.
What is the expected pay-off if the row player plays p against column 1? ~~ The weighted average of pay-offs in column 1 using p and 1 − p.
Why are mixed strategies used? ~~ To prevent the opponent from predicting and exploiting a fixed strategy.
What does it mean if the value of the game is 0? ~~ The game is fair.
What is the optimal strategy for the column player in a 2×2 game? ~~ Found similarly, by equating expected pay-offs for the column player's probabilities.
In LP formulation of a game, what is maximised? ~~ The value of the game V (or equivalently a transformed variable).
What constraints does the LP for a game have? ~~ The expected pay-off against each opponent strategy is at least V, and the probabilities sum to 1.
What is a pure strategy? ~~ Choosing one strategy with certainty.
Can dominance reduce a 3×3 game to a 2×2? ~~ Yes, if a row or column is dominated.
What is the relationship between the players' values in a zero-sum game? ~~ The row player's value is the negative of the column player's value.
What is a non-zero-sum game? ~~ A game where the players' gains and losses don't sum to zero, e.g. the prisoner's dilemma.
How do you check a stable solution? ~~ Check that the saddle point is the smallest in its row and the largest in its column.
What is the maximin criterion? ~~ Choosing the strategy that maximises the minimum pay-off.

What is a recurrence relation? ~~ An equation defining each term of a sequence in terms of previous terms.

What is the general solution of uₙ₊₁ = auₙ (a ≠ 1)? ~~ uₙ = Aaⁿ.
What is the particular solution of uₙ₊₁ = auₙ + b (a ≠ 1)? ~~ A constant c with c = ac + b, so c = b/(1 − a).
Solve uₙ₊₁ = 2uₙ + 3 with u₀ = 1. ~~ c = −3, so uₙ = A2ⁿ − 3; u₀ = 1 gives A = 4: uₙ = 4 × 2ⁿ − 3.
What is a second-order linear recurrence? ~~ uₙ₊₂ = auₙ₊₁ + buₙ (+ f(n)).
What is the auxiliary equation for uₙ₊₂ = auₙ₊₁ + buₙ? ~~ λ² = aλ + b.
What is the general solution if the auxiliary equation has distinct roots λ₁, λ₂? ~~ uₙ = Aλ₁ⁿ + Bλ₂ⁿ.
What is the general solution for a repeated root λ? ~~ uₙ = (A + Bn)λⁿ.
Solve the Fibonacci recurrence uₙ₊₂ = uₙ₊₁ + uₙ. ~~ λ² = λ + 1, λ = (1 ± √5)/2: uₙ = A((1 + √5)/2)ⁿ + B((1 − √5)/2)ⁿ.
Solve uₙ₊₂ = 5uₙ₊₁ − 6uₙ. ~~ λ = 2, 3: uₙ = A2ⁿ + B3ⁿ.
Solve uₙ₊₂ = 4uₙ₊₁ − 4uₙ. ~~ λ = 2 (repeated): uₙ = (A + Bn)2ⁿ.
What trial is used for a particular solution with f(n) = constant? ~~ A constant (or Cn if 1 is a root of the auxiliary equation).
What trial is used for f(n) = kⁿ? ~~ Ckⁿ (or Cnkⁿ if k is a root).
What trial is used for f(n) = linear in n? ~~ Cn + D.
How are recurrence relations used in modelling? ~~ E.g. population changes, loan repayments and investment growth.
Model a loan of £1000 at 5% interest per month with £100 repayments. ~~ uₙ₊₁ = 1.05uₙ − 100, u₀ = 1000.
What is the long-term behaviour of uₙ₊₁ = auₙ + b if |a| < 1? ~~ It converges to b/(1 − a).
What is the long-term behaviour if |a| > 1? ~~ It diverges (unless it starts exactly at the fixed point).
What does a negative a produce in uₙ₊₁ = auₙ + b? ~~ Oscillating terms.
What are complex roots in a second-order recurrence? ~~ They lead to solutions of the form rⁿ(A cos nθ + B sin nθ).
How do initial conditions determine a unique solution? ~~ They give simultaneous equations to find A and B.
What is a closed form of a recurrence? ~~ A formula for uₙ directly in terms of n.
How can a closed form be proved? ~~ By induction.
What is a homogeneous recurrence relation? ~~ One with no f(n) term.

What is the fixed point of uₙ₊₁ = 0.5uₙ + 4? ~~ c = 0.5c + 4, so c = 8.
What is the general solution of uₙ₊₁ = uₙ + 3? ~~ uₙ = u₀ + 3n (arithmetic sequence).
What is the relationship between recurrence relations and differential equations? ~~ Recurrence relations are discrete analogues; their solution methods (auxiliary equations) are similar.
What is the order of a recurrence relation? ~~ The difference between the highest and lowest subscripts.

What is the impulse of a variable force? ~~ I = ∫ F dt.
What is the principle of conservation of momentum? ~~ The total momentum of a system is constant if no external forces act.
What is Newton's experimental law (law of restitution)? ~~ Speed of separation = e × speed of approach.
What is the coefficient of restitution e? ~~ A number between 0 and 1: e = 1 for perfectly elastic collisions, e = 0 for perfectly inelastic.
What happens in a perfectly inelastic collision? ~~ The objects coalesce (stick together).
Is kinetic energy conserved in a collision with e < 1? ~~ No, kinetic energy is lost.
A 2 kg ball at 3 m/s hits a 1 kg ball at rest and they coalesce. Find the final speed. ~~ 6 = 3v, so v = 2 m/s.
How do you find the loss in KE in a collision? ~~ KE before − KE after.
What is the rebound speed of a ball hitting a wall at u with coefficient e? ~~ eu.
A ball is dropped from height h and rebounds with coefficient e. What height does it reach? ~~ e²h.
What is an oblique collision with a smooth wall? ~~ The velocity component parallel to the wall is unchanged; the perpendicular component is multiplied by e and reversed.
In an oblique collision between smooth spheres, what is conserved? ~~ Momentum along the line of centres, and each sphere's velocity perpendicular to the line of centres is unchanged.
What is the impulse on a ball of 0.2 kg that hits a wall at 10 m/s and rebounds at 8 m/s? ~~ 0.2 × (8 − (−10)) = 3.6 N s.
What are successive collisions? ~~ A series of collisions, such as a ball hitting another which then hits a third, analysed one at a time.
How do you know if there will be a further collision? ~~ Compare the velocities after the collisions to see whether one object catches up with another.
What is impulse in 2D? ~~ A vector: I = m(v − u).
What is the effect of an explosion on momentum? ~~ Momentum is conserved; if initially at rest, the fragments have equal and opposite momentum.
What is the recoil of a gun? ~~ The gun moves backwards so that total momentum remains zero.
What does a large impulse over a short time mean? ~~ A large force.
Why do airbags reduce injury? ~~ They increase the collision time, reducing the force for the same impulse.
What is the momentum of a 3 kg object at (2i − j) m/s? ~~ (6i − 3j) kg m/s.
What is the relationship between impulse and area under a force–time graph? ~~ The impulse equals the area under the graph.
What is the direct impact of spheres? ~~ A collision where velocities are along the line of centres.
For e = 1, what happens when equal masses collide directly, one at rest? ~~ They exchange velocities.
What is the maximum KE loss in a collision? ~~ When e = 0 (the objects coalesce).
What does e depend on? ~~ The materials of the colliding objects.
How is the impulse between two colliding bodies related? ~~ Equal and opposite by Newton's third law.

## M1.4
What is angular velocity? ~~ The rate of change of angle: ω = dθ/dt, measured in rad/s.
What is the relationship between linear speed and angular speed? ~~ v = rω.
What is the acceleration of an object in uniform circular motion? ~~ v²/r = rω², directed towards the centre.
What is the centripetal force? ~~ F = mv²/r = mrω², the resultant force towards the centre.
What provides the centripetal force for a car on a flat bend? ~~ Friction between the tyres and the road.
What is the maximum speed of a car on a flat bend with friction coefficient μ? ~~ μmg = mv²/r, so v = √(μgr).
Why are roads banked? ~~ So the normal reaction has a horizontal component providing centripetal force, reducing reliance on friction.
What is the speed on a banked track with no friction? ~~ tan θ = v²/(rg), so v = √(rg tan θ).
What is a conical pendulum? ~~ A particle on a string moving in a horizontal circle: T cos θ = mg and T sin θ = mrω².
How do you find the angle of a conical pendulum? ~~ tan θ = rω²/g.
What is motion in a vertical circle? ~~ Circular motion where speed varies due to gravity; use energy conservation and F = mv²/r.
What is the condition for a particle on a string to complete a vertical circle? ~~ Tension ≥ 0 at the top: v² ≥ gr at the top.
What is the condition for a bead on a smooth wire to complete a vertical circle? ~~ Speed > 0 at the top (it can't fall off).
What is the tension at the bottom of a vertical circle? ~~ T = mg + mv²/r.
What is the tension at the top of a vertical circle? ~~ T = mv²/r − mg.
How do you find the speed at any point of a vertical circle? ~~ Use conservation of energy from a known point.
What happens when the tension in the string becomes zero? ~~ The particle leaves the circular path and moves as a projectile.
What happens when a particle on the outside of a sphere loses contact? ~~ The normal reaction becomes zero: mg cos θ = mv²/r.
What is the period of circular motion? ~~ T = 2π/ω.
Convert 30 rpm to rad/s. ~~ 30 × 2π/60 = π rad/s.
A 0.5 kg mass moves in a circle of radius 2 m at 4 m/s. Find the centripetal force. ~~ 0.5 × 16/2 = 4 N.
What is centrifugal force? ~~ A fictitious outward force felt in a rotating frame; in an inertial frame only centripetal force is real.
What is the minimum speed at the bottom for a string to complete a vertical circle of radius r? ~~ v² = 5gr at the bottom, so v = √(5gr).
What is the tension at the bottom when the particle just completes the circle? ~~ T = mg + m(5gr)/r = 6mg.
What forces act on a car going over a hump-backed bridge? ~~ Weight downwards and the normal reaction upwards; mg − R = mv²/r.
What is the maximum speed at the top of a hump without leaving the road? ~~ R = 0: v = √(gr).
Is velocity constant in uniform circular motion? ~~ No; the speed is constant but the direction changes.
What is the angular speed of the Earth's rotation? ~~ 2π/(24 × 3600) ≈ 7.27 × 10⁻⁵ rad/s.
How is circular motion analysed with vectors? ~~ Position r = r(cos ωt i + sin ωt j), and differentiate to find velocity and acceleration.
What is the direction of velocity in circular motion? ~~ Tangential to the circle.

## M1.5
What is simple harmonic motion? ~~ Motion where the acceleration is proportional to the displacement from a fixed point and directed towards it: ẍ = −ω²x.
What is the general solution of ẍ = −ω²x? ~~ x = a sin(ωt + φ) (or A cos ωt + B sin ωt).
What is the period of SHM? ~~ T = 2π/ω.
What is the amplitude? ~~ The maximum displacement from the centre.
What is the speed formula in SHM? ~~ v² = ω²(a² − x²).
What is the maximum speed in SHM? ~~ aω, at the centre.
What is the maximum acceleration in SHM? ~~ aω², at the extremes.
What is the period of a simple pendulum (small oscillations)? ~~ 2π√(l/g).
What is the period of a mass on a spring? ~~ 2π√(m/k), or 2π√(ml/λ).
How do you show a motion is SHM? ~~ Derive an equation of the form ẍ = −ω²x from Newton's second law.
What is the equilibrium position for a mass hanging on a spring? ~~ Where the tension equals the weight: λe/l = mg.
Why is a simple pendulum only approximately SHM? ~~ The restoring force is proportional to sin θ, which is only approximately θ for small angles.
What is the time to go from the centre to half the amplitude? ~~ Solve x = a sin ωt with x = a/2: t = π/(6ω).
Is the period of SHM independent of amplitude? ~~ Yes (for true SHM).
What is damped SHM? ~~ SHM with a resistive force proportional to velocity, reducing the amplitude.
What is the equation of damped oscillation? ~~ ẍ + 2kẋ + ω²x = 0.
What are the three types of damping? ~~ Light (oscillates with decreasing amplitude), critical (fastest return without oscillating), heavy (slow return without oscillating).
A particle performs SHM with amplitude 5 m and period 4 s. Find its maximum speed. ~~ ω = π/2, so v_max = 5π/2 ≈ 7.85 m/s.
What happens to SHM if a string becomes slack? ~~ The particle moves freely under gravity for part of the motion, so the motion is only partly SHM.
What is the phase in SHM? ~~ The constant φ in x = a sin(ωt + φ), set by initial conditions.
What is the relationship between SHM and circular motion? ~~ SHM is the projection of uniform circular motion onto a diameter.
What is the energy in SHM? ~~ Total energy = ½mω²a², shared between KE and PE.
What is forced oscillation? ~~ Oscillation driven by a periodic external force.
What is resonance? ~~ Large amplitude oscillations when the driving frequency matches the natural frequency.
What is the equation for a mass on a vertical spring? ~~ mẍ = mg − λ(e + x)/l = −λx/l, giving SHM about the equilibrium.
What is the frequency of SHM? ~~ f = ω/(2π) = 1/T.
Find the acceleration at x = 3 m for SHM with ω = 2 rad/s. ~~ ẍ = −ω²x = −12 m/s².
What is the speed at x = 3 m if a = 5 m and ω = 2 rad/s? ~~ v² = 4(25 − 9) = 64, so v = 8 m/s.
How do you find the time spent above a certain height in SHM? ~~ Use x = a cos ωt to find the times at which x equals that value.
What is the centre of oscillation? ~~ The point about which the particle oscillates (x = 0).

## S1.1
What is a discrete random variable? ~~ A variable that takes a countable set of values, each with a probability.
What is E(X) for a discrete random variable? ~~ Σ x P(X = x).
What is E(X²)? ~~ Σ x² P(X = x).
What is Var(X)? ~~ E(X²) − [E(X)]².
What is E(aX + b)? ~~ aE(X) + b.
What is Var(aX + b)? ~~ a²Var(X).
What is E(X + Y)? ~~ E(X) + E(Y), always.
What is Var(X + Y) for independent X and Y? ~~ Var(X) + Var(Y).
What is Var(X − Y) for independent X and Y? ~~ Var(X) + Var(Y).
What is E(g(X))? ~~ Σ g(x) P(X = x).
What is the discrete uniform distribution on 1 to n? ~~ P(X = r) = 1/n, with mean (n + 1)/2 and variance (n² − 1)/12.
What is the geometric distribution? ~~ The number of trials up to and including the first success: P(X = r) = (1 − p)^(r−1) p.
What is the mean of a geometric distribution? ~~ 1/p.
What is the variance of a geometric distribution? ~~ (1 − p)/p².
What is P(X > r) for a geometric distribution? ~~ (1 − p)ʳ.
What is the negative binomial distribution? ~~ The number of trials needed to get r successes.
What is the mean of a binomial B(n, p)? ~~ np.
What is the variance of B(n, p)? ~~ np(1 − p).
What is the mode of a discrete distribution? ~~ The value with the highest probability.
How do you find an unknown probability k in a distribution table? ~~ Use the fact that all probabilities sum to 1.
What is a cumulative distribution function for a discrete variable? ~~ F(x) = P(X ≤ x).
What is the standard deviation? ~~ The square root of the variance.
If E(X) = 3 and Var(X) = 2, find E(2X − 1) and Var(2X − 1). ~~ 5 and 8.
If X ~ B(10, 0.4), find E(X) and Var(X). ~~ 4 and 2.4.
What is E(XY) for independent X and Y? ~~ E(X)E(Y).
What does independence mean for random variables? ~~ The value of one doesn't affect the probabilities of the other.
Find E(X) if P(X = 1) = 0.2, P(X = 2) = 0.5, P(X = 3) = 0.3. ~~ 0.2 + 1 + 0.9 = 2.1.
Find Var(X) for the distribution above. ~~ E(X²) = 0.2 + 2 + 2.7 = 4.9; Var = 4.9 − 4.41 = 0.49.
What is the sum of n independent identical variables' variance? ~~ nVar(X).
What is the difference between Var(2X) and Var(X₁ + X₂)? ~~ Var(2X) = 4Var(X); Var(X₁ + X₂) = 2Var(X) for independent copies.

## S1.2
What is a continuous random variable? ~~ A variable that can take any value in an interval, described by a probability density function.
What is a probability density function (pdf)? ~~ A function f(x) ≥ 0 with ∫ f(x) dx = 1 over all x.
How is P(a < X < b) found? ~~ ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx.
What is P(X = a) for a continuous random variable? ~~ 0.
What is the cumulative distribution function F(x)? ~~ F(x) = P(X ≤ x) = ∫ from −∞ to x of f(t) dt.
How is the pdf found from the cdf? ~~ f(x) = F'(x).
What is E(X) for a continuous random variable? ~~ ∫ x f(x) dx.
What is E(X²)? ~~ ∫ x² f(x) dx.
What is Var(X)? ~~ E(X²) − [E(X)]².
How is the median found? ~~ Solve F(m) = 0.5.
How are quartiles found? ~~ Solve F(q) = 0.25 and F(q) = 0.75.
How is the mode of a continuous distribution found? ~~ The value of x where f(x) is maximum (differentiate or look at the graph).
What is the continuous uniform (rectangular) distribution? ~~ f(x) = 1/(b − a) for a ≤ x ≤ b.
What is the mean of U(a, b)? ~~ (a + b)/2.
What is the variance of U(a, b)? ~~ (b − a)²/12.
What is the exponential distribution? ~~ f(x) = λe^(−λx) for x ≥ 0.
What is the mean of the exponential distribution? ~~ 1/λ.
What is the variance of the exponential distribution? ~~ 1/λ².
What does the exponential distribution model? ~~ The time between events in a Poisson process.
What is the memoryless property? ~~ P(X > s + t | X > s) = P(X > t) for the exponential distribution.
Find k if f(x) = kx for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2. ~~ ∫₀² kx dx = 2k = 1, so k = 1/2.
Find E(X) for f(x) = x/2, 0 ≤ x ≤ 2. ~~ ∫₀² x²/2 dx = 4/3.
Find the median of f(x) = x/2, 0 ≤ x ≤ 2. ~~ F(x) = x²/4 = 0.5, so m = √2.
What is E(g(X)) for a continuous variable? ~~ ∫ g(x) f(x) dx.
What does the shape of a pdf tell you about skew? ~~ A longer tail to the right means positive skew (mean > median).
What is a piecewise pdf? ~~ A pdf defined by different formulas on different intervals.
How do you find F(x) for a piecewise pdf? ~~ Integrate each piece, adding the accumulated probability from earlier pieces.
What are the properties of F(x)? ~~ Non-decreasing, F(−∞) = 0, F(∞) = 1.
What is P(X > a) in terms of F? ~~ 1 − F(a).
What is the pdf of Y = g(X)? ~~ Find the cdf of Y using F_X, then differentiate.

## S1.3
What is a point estimate? ~~ A single value estimating a population parameter, e.g. x̄ for μ.
What is an unbiased estimator? ~~ An estimator whose expected value equals the parameter it estimates.
What is the unbiased estimate of the population variance? ~~ s² = Σ(x − x̄)²/(n − 1).
What is the standard error of the mean? ~~ σ/√n.
What is the Central Limit Theorem? ~~ For large n, the sample mean is approximately normally distributed: X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n), regardless of the population's distribution.
What is a confidence interval? ~~ An interval that, with a stated probability, is expected to contain the population parameter.
What is a 95% confidence interval for μ with known σ? ~~ x̄ ± 1.96σ/√n.
What does a 95% confidence interval mean? ~~ If many such intervals were calculated, 95% would contain the true mean.
When is the t-distribution used? ~~ When σ is unknown and the sample is small from a normal population.
What is the t-statistic? ~~ t = (x̄ − μ)/(s/√n), with n − 1 degrees of freedom.
What is a 95% confidence interval using t? ~~ x̄ ± t(n−1) × s/√n.
What happens to the t-distribution as n increases? ~~ It approaches the standard normal distribution.
What is a Type I error? ~~ Rejecting H₀ when it is true.
What is a Type II error? ~~ Not rejecting H₀ when it is false.
What is the power of a test? ~~ The probability of rejecting H₀ when it is false: 1 − P(Type II error).
How is the probability of a Type I error related to the significance level? ~~ For continuous tests it equals the significance level.
How do you increase the power of a test? ~~ Increase the sample size, or increase the significance level.
What is a paired t-test? ~~ A test on the mean difference between paired observations.
What is a two-sample t-test? ~~ A test comparing the means of two independent samples.
What assumption does the two-sample t-test make? ~~ Both populations are normal with equal variances (for the pooled test).
What is the pooled variance estimate? ~~ s_p² = ((n₁ − 1)s₁² + (n₂ − 1)s₂²)/(n₁ + n₂ − 2).
How does a larger sample affect a confidence interval? ~~ It makes it narrower.
How does a higher confidence level affect the interval? ~~ It makes it wider.
What is the critical value for a 99% two-tailed normal interval? ~~ 2.576.
Find a 95% CI for μ given x̄ = 50, σ = 10, n = 25. ~~ 50 ± 1.96 × 2 = (46.08, 53.92).
What is the sampling distribution of the difference in means? ~~ X̄ − Ȳ ~ N(μ₁ − μ₂, σ₁²/n₁ + σ₂²/n₂).
What is a test for a population mean using a large sample? ~~ Use z = (x̄ − μ)/(s/√n) by the CLT.
What does it mean if a hypothesised value lies outside a confidence interval? ~~ H₀ would be rejected at the corresponding significance level.
What is a consistent estimator? ~~ One that gets closer to the true value as the sample size increases.
Why divide by n − 1 for the sample variance? ~~ To give an unbiased estimate of σ².

## S1.4
What is a chi-squared goodness of fit test? ~~ A test of whether observed frequencies fit a proposed distribution.
What is the chi-squared test statistic? ~~ X² = Σ (O − E)²/E.
How are expected frequencies found in a goodness of fit test? ~~ Total frequency × probability from the proposed distribution.
What is the condition on expected frequencies? ~~ Each should be at least 5; combine classes if necessary.
How are degrees of freedom found for goodness of fit? ~~ Number of classes (after combining) − 1 − number of parameters estimated from the data.
What is a contingency table test? ~~ A chi-squared test for independence between two categorical variables.
How are expected frequencies calculated in a contingency table? ~~ (Row total × column total) ÷ grand total.
How many degrees of freedom does an r × c contingency table have? ~~ (r − 1)(c − 1).
What is Yates' continuity correction? ~~ For 2×2 tables: Σ (|O − E| − 0.5)²/E.
What are the hypotheses for a test of independence? ~~ H₀: the variables are independent; H₁: they are not independent (associated).
When do you reject H₀ in a chi-squared test? ~~ When the test statistic exceeds the critical value.
Is a chi-squared test one-tailed or two-tailed? ~~ One-tailed (upper tail), since large values show poor fit.
What does a very small X² value suggest? ~~ A very good fit, possibly suspiciously good.
How do you test whether data fit a binomial distribution with p estimated? ~~ Estimate p from the data mean, calculate expected frequencies, and subtract an extra degree of freedom.
How do you test whether data fit a Poisson distribution? ~~ Estimate λ from the sample mean (if unknown), find expected frequencies, and test with one fewer degree of freedom.
What is the critical value for χ² with 1 degree of freedom at 5%? ~~ 3.841.
What is the critical value for χ² with 2 degrees of freedom at 5%? ~~ 5.991.
How do you interpret a significant contingency table result? ~~ Look at which cells have the largest contributions (O − E)²/E to describe the association.
What is the effect of combining classes? ~~ It reduces the degrees of freedom.
Can chi-squared tests be used with percentages? ~~ No, only with observed frequencies.
What is the distribution of the test statistic under H₀? ~~ Approximately χ² with the appropriate degrees of freedom.
What is a uniform distribution test? ~~ Testing whether all outcomes are equally likely, e.g. a fair die.
Find the expected frequency for a fair die rolled 120 times. ~~ 20 for each face.
What is the shape of the χ² distribution? ~~ Positively skewed, becoming more symmetrical as the degrees of freedom increase.
What is the mean of a χ² distribution with ν degrees of freedom? ~~ ν.
What should the conclusion of a chi-squared test include? ~~ Whether to reject H₀, stated in context.
Why must observations be independent for a chi-squared test? ~~ The test assumes each observation falls into a category independently.
What does a large contribution from one cell indicate? ~~ That cell differs most from what would be expected under H₀.
How do you test a normal distribution fit? ~~ Group the data, calculate expected frequencies using normal probabilities, and subtract degrees of freedom for estimated μ and σ.
What does "goodness of fit" mean? ~~ How well a theoretical distribution matches observed data.

## S1.5
What is a probability generating function (PGF)? ~~ G(t) = E(tˣ) = Σ P(X = x)tˣ for a discrete random variable.
What is G(1) for any PGF? ~~ 1.
How is E(X) found from a PGF? ~~ E(X) = G'(1).
How is Var(X) found from a PGF? ~~ Var(X) = G''(1) + G'(1) − [G'(1)]².
What is the PGF of a binomial B(n, p)? ~~ (q + pt)ⁿ, where q = 1 − p.
What is the PGF of a Poisson Po(λ)? ~~ e^(λ(t − 1)).
What is the PGF of a geometric distribution? ~~ pt/(1 − qt).
What is the PGF of the sum of independent random variables? ~~ The product of their PGFs: G_(X+Y)(t) = G_X(t)G_Y(t).
What is the PGF of aX + b? ~~ t^b G_X(tᵃ).
How can P(X = r) be found from a PGF? ~~ The coefficient of tʳ in G(t).
What is the PGF of a Bernoulli variable? ~~ q + pt.
Use PGFs to show the sum of two independent Poisson variables is Poisson. ~~ e^(λ(t − 1)) × e^(μ(t − 1)) = e^((λ + μ)(t − 1)), which is Po(λ + μ).
Use the PGF to find the mean of Po(λ). ~~ G'(t) = λe^(λ(t − 1)), so G'(1) = λ.
Find the variance of Po(λ) using its PGF. ~~ G''(1) = λ²; Var = λ² + λ − λ² = λ.
What is the PGF of a fair six-sided die? ~~ (t + t² + … + t⁶)/6.
What is the PGF of the total score of two dice? ~~ [(t + t² + … + t⁶)/6]².
How can P(X = 0) be found from G(t)? ~~ G(0).
What is the PGF of the negative binomial distribution? ~~ [pt/(1 − qt)]ʳ.
Why are PGFs useful? ~~ They make it easy to find means, variances and the distributions of sums.
Find E(X) if G(t) = (1 + t)²/4. ~~ G'(t) = (1 + t)/2, so E(X) = 1.
What does it mean if two variables have the same PGF? ~~ They have the same distribution.
What is G'(t)? ~~ Σ x P(X = x) t^(x−1).
What is G''(1)? ~~ E(X(X − 1)).
Find Var(X) for B(n, p) using its PGF. ~~ G'(1) = np, G''(1) = n(n − 1)p²; Var = n(n − 1)p² + np − n²p² = np(1 − p).
What is the PGF of X + Y if X ~ B(n, p) and Y ~ B(m, p) are independent? ~~ (q + pt)^(n+m), so X + Y ~ B(n + m, p).
What range of t makes a PGF valid? ~~ At least |t| ≤ 1, where the series converges.
How can a PGF identify a distribution? ~~ By matching its form to a standard PGF.
What is the PGF of Y = 2X for X ~ Po(λ)? ~~ G_X(t²) = e^(λ(t² − 1)).
What is the probability of an even outcome using G? ~~ (G(1) + G(−1))/2.
How do you expand a PGF to find probabilities? ~~ Expand as a power series in t and read the coefficients.
