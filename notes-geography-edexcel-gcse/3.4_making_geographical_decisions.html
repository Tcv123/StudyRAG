3.4 Making Geographical Decisions

1. The Decision-Making Exercise

Component 3 (People and Environment Issues — Making Geographical Decisions) is a 1 hour 30 minute exam worth 64 marks (including 4 marks for SPaG). It is based on a resource booklet given out in the exam — there is no pre-release. The booklet focuses on a particular place and issue linked to Topics 7–9.

SectionContent
Section APeople and the Biosphere — questions using the resources and your knowledge of Topic 7
Section BForests under Threat — Topic 8
Section CConsuming Energy Resources — Topic 9
Section DThe decision — choose one of three options and justify it (12 marks plus 4 SPaG)

2. How to Approach the Resource Booklet

  1. Spend a few minutes skimming the whole booklet before answering — understand the place, the issue and the options.
  2. Annotate figures as you go: what does each show, and does it support or oppose any option?
  3. Identify the stakeholders and their likely views.
  4. Use both the resources and your own knowledge of the biosphere, forests and energy.

3. Making and Justifying the Decision

  • Choose one option and state it clearly at the start.
  • Justify it using evidence from several figures — quote data and refer to figure numbers.
  • Consider economic, social and environmental impacts, in the short and long term, and for different stakeholders.
  • Explain why you rejected the other options.
  • Acknowledge the weaknesses of your chosen option and suggest how they could be reduced.
  • Link to sustainability — does the option meet present needs without harming future generations?

4. Useful Frameworks

FrameworkUse

Exam Tip: The highest marks go to answers that are balanced but decisive: they recognise that every option has drawbacks, but still reach a clear, well-supported judgement. Leave time to check spelling, punctuation and grammar — 4 SPaG marks are available on the final question.

5. Geographical Skills for Component 3

  • Interpreting maps, graphs, tables, photographs and newspaper extracts.
  • Calculating percentages, percentage change, means and ranges.
  • Recognising bias in sources — who wrote it, and why?
  • Comparing data from different places and times.
