3.2 Percentages, Growth & Decay

AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Ratio, Proportion & Rates of Change — spec refs R9, R16 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

3.2.1 What a Percentage Means

Per cent means “number of parts per hundred”. $35\% = \dfrac{35}{100} = 0.35$.
A percentage can be greater than 100%: $120\%$ of an amount is $1.2$ times the amount.

Percentages of an amount without a calculator

Build up from 10% (divide by 10), 5% (half of 10%), 1% (divide by 100) and so on.

Work out $17.5\%$ of £360.

$10\% = 36$, $5\% = 18$, $2.5\% = 9$. So $17.5\% = 36 + 18 + 9 = \mathbf{£63}$.

One quantity as a percentage of another

$A$ as a percentage of $B$ $= \dfrac{A}{B} \times 100\%$   (same units first)
(a) 45 minutes as a percentage of 2 hours: $\dfrac{45}{120} \times 100 = \mathbf{37.5\%}$.
(b) £18 as a percentage of £15: $\dfrac{18}{15} \times 100 = \mathbf{120\%}$.

Comparing using percentages

Sam scored 42 out of 60 in test A and 54 out of 75 in test B. Which is the better result?

Test A: $\tfrac{42}{60} = 70\%$. Test B: $\tfrac{54}{75} = 72\%$. Test B is better.

3.2.2 Percentage Increase and Decrease with Multipliers

Interpret a percentage change multiplicatively: the new amount is a percentage of the original, written as a decimal multiplier.

Increase by $r\%$: multiplier $= 1 + \dfrac{r}{100}$    e.g. $+12\% \to 1.12$
Decrease by $r\%$: multiplier $= 1 - \dfrac{r}{100}$    e.g. $-18\% \to 0.82$
New amount $=$ original $\times$ multiplier
(a) Increase £85 by 12%: $85 \times 1.12 = \mathbf{£95.20}$.
(b) A TV costing £450 is reduced by 18%: $450 \times 0.82 = \mathbf{£369}$.

Percentage change

$$\text{Percentage change} = \frac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%$$
A ticket price rises from £36 to £45. Change $= 9$. Percentage increase $= \dfrac{9}{36} \times 100 = \mathbf{25\%}$.

A dog’s mass falls from 80 kg to 68 kg. Percentage decrease $= \dfrac{12}{80} \times 100 = \mathbf{15\%}$.
Always divide by the original value, not the new one. Dividing 12 by 68 gives 17.6%, a common wrong answer.

Profit and loss

Percentage profit $= \dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100$. A trader who buys 40 items at £6 (cost £240) and sells 30 at £10 and 10 at £4 (income £340) makes £100 profit: $\tfrac{100}{240} \times 100 = 41.7\%$.

3.2.3 Reverse Percentages (Original Value Problems)

In a reverse percentage problem you know the amount after a percentage change and want the original. Since new $=$ original $\times$ multiplier: $$\text{original} = \frac{\text{new amount}}{\text{multiplier}}$$
After a 15% pay rise, Jo earns £29 900. What did she earn before?

$115\% = 29\,900$, so original $= 29\,900 \div 1.15 = \mathbf{£26\,000}$.
A coat costs £52 after a 35% reduction. Find the original price.

$65\% = 52$, so original $= 52 \div 0.65 = \mathbf{£80}$.
A price of £66 includes VAT at 20%. Price before VAT $= 66 \div 1.2 = \mathbf{£55}$.
Never take the percentage of the new amount and add or subtract it. 20% of £66 is £13.20, and £66 − £13.20 $=$ £52.80 is wrong — the 20% was of the original price.

Successive percentage changes

Multiply the multipliers to get one overall multiplier.

A bike’s price is increased by 10% and then decreased by 10%.

Overall multiplier $= 1.1 \times 0.9 = 0.99$, so the final price is 99% of the original: a 1% decrease, not “no change”.
Prices are cut by 15%, then a further 10% is taken off the sale price.

$0.85 \times 0.9 = 0.765$: total reduction $= 100\% - 76.5\% = \mathbf{23.5\%}$ (not 25%).

3.2.4 Simple Interest

Simple interest is paid only on the original amount (the principal), so the same interest is earned every year.
$$\text{Simple interest} = P \times \frac{r}{100} \times n$$ $P$ = principal, $r$ = rate per year (%), $n$ = number of years
£2400 is invested for 3 years at 3.5% per year simple interest.

Interest per year $= 2400 \times 0.035 = £84$. Total interest $= 84 \times 3 = \mathbf{£252}$.
Value at the end $= 2400 + 252 = £2652$.

3.2.5 Compound Interest, Growth and Decay

Compound interest: interest is added to the account each year and then also earns interest. Growth and decay (e.g. populations, depreciation of cars) work the same way — the same percentage change is applied repeatedly.
$$\text{Amount after } n \text{ periods} = P \times \left(1 \pm \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$ Use $+$ for growth/interest and $-$ for decay/depreciation.
£6000 is invested at 2% per year compound interest for 3 years.

$6000 \times 1.02^3 = 6367.248 \approx \mathbf{£6367.25}$.
A car bought for £18 500 depreciates by 12% each year. Find its value after 4 years.

Multiplier $= 0.88$. Value $= 18\,500 \times 0.88^4 = 11\,094.36\ldots \approx \mathbf{£11\,094}$.
A van costs £24 000. It loses 20% in the first year and 15% in each year after that. Value after 3 years:

$24\,000 \times 0.8 \times 0.85^2 = \mathbf{£13\,872}$.

Comparing simple and compound interest

Bank A: £5000 at 4% simple interest for 3 years. Bank B: £5000 at 3.9% compound interest for 3 years.

A: $5000 + 3 \times 200 = £5600$.   B: $5000 \times 1.039^3 = £5608.11$.
Bank B gives more, even though its rate is lower, because the interest compounds.

Finding the number of years

Use trial and improvement with the formula: test whole numbers of years and show the values either side of the target.

A lake has 1200 fish, decreasing by 8% a year. When will there first be fewer than 800?

$1200 \times 0.92^4 = 859.7$ (still above 800); $1200 \times 0.92^5 = 790.9$ (below 800).
Answer: after 5 years.

Reverse growth and finding the rate

After 2 years of 10% compound growth an investment is worth £4840. Original $= 4840 \div 1.1^2 = 4840 \div 1.21 = \mathbf{£4000}$.
£8000 grows to £9724.05 in 4 years at $r\%$ compound interest. Find $r$.

$1 + \dfrac{r}{100} = \sqrt[4]{\dfrac{9724.05}{8000}} = \sqrt[4]{1.21550625} = 1.05$, so $r = \mathbf{5}$.
“Compound”, “each year” or “per annum, depreciates” means use a power. “Simple” means the same interest every year. Read whether the question wants the total value or just the interest.

3.2.6 General Iterative Processes Higher

Many real situations combine a percentage change with a fixed amount added or removed each period. These are described by an iterative formula (a recurrence relation):

$$u_{n+1} = a\,u_n + b$$ $u_0$ is the starting value, $a$ is the multiplier (percentage change), $b$ is the fixed amount added ($b > 0$) or removed ($b < 0$) each period.
FormulaMeaning
$u_{n+1} = 1.05u_n - 20$grows by 5% each period, then 20 is removed
$u_{n+1} = 1.03u_n + 100$3% interest added each year, then £100 deposited
$P_{n+1} = 0.9P_n + 50$10% of the population is lost, then 50 are added
$B_{n+1} = 1.01B_n - 300$loan with 1% monthly interest and £300 repaid each month
$u_{n+1} = 1.05u_n - 20$ and $u_0 = 500$. Find $u_2$.

$u_1 = 1.05 \times 500 - 20 = 505$
$u_2 = 1.05 \times 505 - 20 = 530.25 - 20 = \mathbf{510.25}$
Island population: $P_{n+1} = 1.2P_n - 150$, $P_0 = 1000$ in 2020. When does it first exceed 1500?

$P_1 = 1050,\; P_2 = 1110,\; P_3 = 1182,\; P_4 = 1268.4,\; P_5 = 1372.08,\; P_6 = 1496.50,\; P_7 = 1645.80$
First exceeds 1500 at $n = 7$, i.e. in 2027.

Long-term behaviour

If the sequence settles down, the limit $L$ satisfies $L = aL + b$.

$u_{n+1} = 0.8u_n + 30$. Long-term value: $L = 0.8L + 30 \Rightarrow 0.2L = 30 \Rightarrow L = \mathbf{150}$.
A £5000 loan at 1% per month with only £50 repaid each month: $B_1 = 1.01 \times 5000 - 50 = 5000$. The interest (£50) exactly cancels the repayment, so the balance never falls — the loan is never repaid.
Use your calculator’s ANS key: type the starting value, press =, then type $1.05 \times \text{ANS} - 20$ and press = repeatedly. Write down each term to show your method, and keep full accuracy until the end.
