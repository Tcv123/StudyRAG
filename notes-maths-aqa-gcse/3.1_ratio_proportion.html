AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Ratio, Proportion & Rates of Change — spec refs R2–R8, R10, R12, R13 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

3.1.1 Ratio Notation and Simplifying

A ratio compares the sizes of two or more quantities. The ratio $a : b$ means “for every $a$ parts of the first, there are $b$ parts of the second”. Order matters: boys : girls $= 3 : 5$ is not the same as girls : boys.

To simplify a ratio, divide every part by the highest common factor (HCF). A ratio is in its simplest form when the parts are whole numbers with no common factor other than 1.

  • Make the units the same first, e.g. $40\text{ cm} : 2\text{ m} = 40 : 200 = 1 : 5$.
  • Clear decimals by multiplying by 10, 100, …, e.g. $1.5 : 4.5 = 15 : 45 = 1 : 3$.
  • Clear fractions by multiplying by a common denominator, e.g. $\tfrac{1}{2} : \tfrac{2}{3} = 3 : 4$ (multiply by 6).
Simplify $2.5\text{ kg} : 750\text{ g}$.

Same units: $2500 : 750$. HCF is 250, so $2500 : 750 = \mathbf{10 : 3}$.
Simplify $45 : 60 : 75$.

HCF of all three is 15: $45 : 60 : 75 = \mathbf{3 : 4 : 5}$.

The form $1 : n$ and $n : 1$

Divide both parts by the number you want to become 1. The answer need not be a whole number. This is useful for comparing scales and concentrations.

Write $3 : 8$ in the form $1 : n$.

Divide both parts by 3: $1 : \tfrac{8}{3} = \mathbf{1 : 2.67}$ (to 2 d.p.).
“Give your answer in its simplest form” means whole numbers with no common factor. A ratio such as $1 : 2.5$ is not simplest form — write $2 : 5$.

3.1.2 Ratios, Fractions and Multiplicative Relationships

One quantity as a fraction of another

To write $A$ as a fraction of $B$, write $\dfrac{A}{B}$ (same units) and simplify. The fraction can be greater than 1.

(a) Write 15 as a fraction of 40: $\dfrac{15}{40} = \mathbf{\dfrac{3}{8}}$.
(b) Write 50 cm as a fraction of 20 cm: $\dfrac{50}{20} = \mathbf{\dfrac{5}{2}}$ — the first length is $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ times the second.

Ratio to fractions

If boys : girls $= 3 : 5$ then there are $3 + 5 = 8$ parts altogether:

  • the fraction of the class who are boys is $\tfrac{3}{8}$; girls is $\tfrac{5}{8}$;
  • the number of boys is $\tfrac{3}{5}$ of the number of girls, and the number of girls is $\tfrac{5}{3}$ of the number of boys.
Part : part and part : whole
If $A : B = a : b$, then $A$ is $\dfrac{a}{a+b}$ of the whole, and $\dfrac{A}{B} = \dfrac{a}{b}$, i.e. $A = \dfrac{a}{b}B$.

Ratios and linear functions

If $x : y = 3 : 4$ then $\dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{4}{3}$, so $y = \tfrac{4}{3}x$. Every pair of values in a fixed ratio lies on a straight line through the origin; the gradient is the multiplier between the quantities. This links ratio to direct proportion (section 3.1.5).

A ratio $a : b$ is not the fraction $\tfrac{a}{b}$ of the whole. With $3 : 5$, the first share is $\tfrac{3}{8}$ of the total, not $\tfrac{3}{5}$.

3.1.3 Dividing in a Ratio and Ratio Problems

Sharing in a ratio $a : b : c$
1. Total parts $= a + b + c$
2. One part $=$ amount $\div$ total parts
3. Each share $=$ its ratio number $\times$ one part
Share £450 in the ratio $4 : 5$.

$4 + 5 = 9$ parts. One part $= 450 \div 9 = 50$.
Shares: $4 \times 50 = \mathbf{£200}$ and $5 \times 50 = \mathbf{£250}$. Check: $200 + 250 = 450$. ✓

When you know one share or a difference

If you are given one share, or the difference between shares, find the value of one part from that information — do not divide the total.

Boys : girls in a club $= 5 : 3$. There are 14 more boys than girls. How many members are there?

The difference is $5 - 3 = 2$ parts, so 2 parts $= 14$ and 1 part $= 7$.
Total $= 8$ parts $= 8 \times 7 = \mathbf{56}$ members.

Combining ratios

$A : B = 3 : 4$ and $B : C = 6 : 5$. Find $A : B : C$.

Make the $B$ values equal: LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.
$A : B = 9 : 12$ and $B : C = 12 : 10$, so $A : B : C = \mathbf{9 : 12 : 10}$.

Ratio problems with algebra

When a ratio changes (counters added or removed, ages increasing), call the parts $3k$, $7k$, … and form an equation.

Red : blue counters $= 3 : 7$. Kim removes 12 blue counters and the ratio becomes $1 : 2$. How many counters were there at first?

Red $= 3k$, blue $= 7k$. After: $3k : (7k - 12) = 1 : 2$, so $2 \times 3k = 7k - 12$.
$6k = 7k - 12 \Rightarrow k = 12$. Original total $= 10k = \mathbf{120}$.

Real contexts: mixing, recipes, conversion

For limiting-ingredient problems, work out how much of the product each ingredient allows, then take the smallest.

Concrete: cement : sand : gravel $= 1 : 2 : 4$ (7 parts). A builder has 50 kg cement, 120 kg sand, 160 kg gravel.

Cement allows 1 part $= 50$ kg $\Rightarrow 350$ kg concrete. Sand: 1 part $= 60$ kg $\Rightarrow 420$ kg. Gravel: 1 part $= 40$ kg $\Rightarrow 280$ kg.
Greatest amount $= \mathbf{280\text{ kg}}$ (gravel runs out first).
Always check your shares add up to the total, and re-read which share the question asks for (smaller, larger, or the difference).

3.1.4 Unitary Method, Best Buys, Scales and Maps

The unitary method and best buys

Find the value of one item (or one gram, one litre, …) and then multiply. For best buys, compare the cost per unit (lower is better) or the amount per £1 (higher is better).

Juice: 1 litre for £1.35, 1.5 litres for £1.95, 2 litres for £2.70. Which is best value?

Cost per litre: £1.35, $1.95 \div 1.5 = £1.30$, $2.70 \div 2 = £1.35$.
The 1.5 litre carton is best value.
Exchange rate £1 $=$ €1.15. Convert €506 to pounds.

Euros $\to$ pounds: divide. $506 \div 1.15 = \mathbf{£440}$.

Scale factors, scale drawings and maps

A map scale $1 : n$ means 1 unit on the map represents $n$ of the same unit in real life. Real $=$ map $\times n$; map $=$ real $\div n$.

Scale $1 : 50\,000$. Two villages are 7.5 km apart. How far apart are they on the map?

$7.5\text{ km} = 750\,000\text{ cm}$. Map distance $= 750\,000 \div 50\,000 = \mathbf{15\text{ cm}}$.
A field is drawn at a scale of $1 : 2000$ as a 6 cm by 4.5 cm rectangle. Find its real area.

Real lengths: $6 \times 2000 = 12\,000$ cm $= 120$ m and $4.5 \times 2000 = 9000$ cm $= 90$ m.
Area $= 120 \times 90 = \mathbf{10\,800\text{ m}^2}$. (Convert the lengths first — do not multiply the area by 2000.)
Map and scale questions often combine with bearings and constructions. Keep units consistent: 1 m $=$ 100 cm, 1 km $=$ 100 000 cm.

3.1.5 Direct and Inverse Proportion

Two ratios are in proportion when they are equal, e.g. $6 : 15 = 10 : 25$ (both $= 2 : 5$).
$y$ is directly proportional to $x$, written $y \propto x$, if $y = kx$ for a constant $k$: when $x$ doubles, $y$ doubles.
$y$ is inversely proportional to $x$, written $y \propto \dfrac{1}{x}$, if $y = \dfrac{k}{x}$, i.e. $xy = k$: when $x$ doubles, $y$ halves.

Solving proportion problems numerically

Direct: $y \propto x$ and $y = 18$ when $x = 4$. Find $y$ when $x = 10$.

$k = 18 \div 4 = 4.5$, so $y = 4.5x$. When $x = 10$: $y = \mathbf{45}$.
Inverse: 6 workers harvest a field in 15 hours. How long would 9 workers take?

Total work $= 6 \times 15 = 90$ worker-hours (constant). Time $= 90 \div 9 = \mathbf{10}$ hours.
To finish in 5 hours you would need $90 \div 5 = 18$ workers. (Assumes all work at the same rate.)

3.1.6 Proportion Equations Higher

At Higher tier you must construct the equation for a proportion relationship involving powers and roots, find $k$, and use it.

Method
1. Write the relationship with $k$, e.g. $y = kx^2$, $y = \dfrac{k}{\sqrt{x}}$, $F = \dfrac{k}{d^2}$
2. Substitute the given pair of values and solve for $k$
3. Rewrite the formula using the value of $k$
4. Substitute to find the required value

$y$ is directly proportional to $x^2$. When $x = 4$, $y = 48$. Find $y$ when $x = 2.5$.

$y = kx^2 \Rightarrow 48 = 16k \Rightarrow k = 3$, so $y = 3x^2$.
$y = 3 \times 2.5^2 = 3 \times 6.25 = \mathbf{18.75}$.
The force $F$ between two magnets is inversely proportional to the square of the distance $d$. $F = 20$ when $d = 3$. Find $d$ when $F = 5$.

$F = \dfrac{k}{d^2} \Rightarrow 20 = \dfrac{k}{9} \Rightarrow k = 180$, so $F = \dfrac{180}{d^2}$.
$5 = \dfrac{180}{d^2} \Rightarrow d^2 = 36 \Rightarrow d = \mathbf{6}$.
$T$ is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{L}$. $T = 6$ when $L = 16$. Find $T$ when $L = 36$.

$T = \dfrac{k}{\sqrt{L}} \Rightarrow 6 = \dfrac{k}{4} \Rightarrow k = 24$. When $L = 36$: $T = \dfrac{24}{6} = \mathbf{4}$.

Percentage changes in proportion problems

If $y \propto x^3$ and $x$ increases by 20%, then $y$ is multiplied by $1.2^3 = 1.728$, an increase of 72.8%.

Always write the formula with the value of $k$ as a separate line (“$y = 3x^2$”) — it usually earns its own mark, and it is the answer if the question asks you to “find a formula”.

3.1.7 Comparing Lengths, Areas and Volumes

Ratio notation can compare lengths, areas and volumes. For simple shapes you can calculate each area or volume and write the ratio. Enlarged (similar) shapes have corresponding lengths in the same ratio, which links ratio to similarity and to the trigonometric ratios (in similar right-angled triangles, ratios such as $\tfrac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}$ are equal for the same angle).

Rectangle A is 3 cm by 4 cm and rectangle B is 6 cm by 8 cm.

Lengths ratio $= 3 : 6 = 1 : 2$. Areas: $12 : 48 = \mathbf{1 : 4}$. The area ratio is the square of the length ratio.

Similar shapes Higher

If the length scale factor is $k$ (length ratio $a : b$):
area scale factor $= k^2$ (area ratio $a^2 : b^2$)
volume scale factor $= k^3$ (volume ratio $a^3 : b^3$)
Two similar bottles are 12 cm and 18 cm tall. The smaller holds 400 ml and has surface area 270 cm².

$k = 18 \div 12 = 1.5$.
Capacity of larger $= 400 \times 1.5^3 = 400 \times 3.375 = \mathbf{1350\text{ ml}}$.
Surface area of larger $= 270 \times 1.5^2 = 270 \times 2.25 = \mathbf{607.5\text{ cm}^2}$.
Two similar cones have volumes 54 cm³ and 250 cm³. Find the ratio of their heights.

Volume ratio $54 : 250 = 27 : 125$. Length ratio $= \sqrt[3]{27} : \sqrt[3]{125} = \mathbf{3 : 5}$.
Going from area or volume back to length, take the square root or cube root of the scale factor first. Never multiply a volume by the length scale factor.
