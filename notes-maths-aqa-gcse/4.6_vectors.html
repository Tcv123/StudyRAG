4.6 Vectors

AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Geometry & Measures — spec refs G24, G25 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

4.6.1 What Is a Vector?

A vector is a quantity that has both magnitude (size) and direction. A scalar has size only — it is just a number, such as $3$, $-2$ or $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

Vectors can be written in three ways:

  • Column vector: $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ — the top number is the movement in the $x$-direction (right is positive, left is negative) and the bottom number is the movement in the $y$-direction (up is positive, down is negative).
  • Bold lower-case letter: $\mathbf{a}$. In handwriting you cannot write in bold, so underline it: $\underline{a}$.
  • Between two points: $\overrightarrow{AB}$ means the vector that starts at $A$ and finishes at $B$.
Column vector from $A(x_1, y_1)$ to $B(x_2, y_2)$: $$\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} x_2 - x_1 \\ y_2 - y_1 \end{pmatrix}$$ Reversing a vector: $\overrightarrow{BA} = -\overrightarrow{AB}$
$A$ is the point $(2, 1)$ and $B$ is the point $(5, -3)$. Write down $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BA}$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 - 2 \\ -3 - 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \end{pmatrix}$    and    $\overrightarrow{BA} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$

Equal and Negative Vectors

Two vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and the same direction — they do not have to be in the same place. In a parallelogram $ABCD$, $\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{DC}$ even though they are opposite sides.

The vector $-\mathbf{a}$ has the same length as $\mathbf{a}$ but points the opposite way. If $\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$ then $-\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$.

4.6.2 Translations as Vectors (G24)

A translation slides every point of a shape the same distance in the same direction. It is described fully by the word translation and a column vector. The image is congruent to the object and has the same orientation.
Translating the point $(x, y)$ by the vector $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$ gives the image $(x + a,\; y + b)$.

Triangle $A$ is mapped onto triangle $B$ by the translation $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. To go back from $B$ to $A$ use $\begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$.

A triangle has vertices $(1, 1)$, $(3, 1)$ and $(1, 4)$. It is translated by $\begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Find the vertices of the image.

Add $-4$ to every $x$-coordinate and $2$ to every $y$-coordinate:
$(1, 1) \to (-3, 3)$,   $(3, 1) \to (-1, 3)$,   $(1, 4) \to (-3, 6)$
Shape $P$ is translated by $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix}$ and then by $\begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find the single translation that has the same effect.

Add the vectors: $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -5 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$
“Describe fully” a translation needs two things: the word translation and the column vector. Do not write it as a coordinate like $(3, 2)$ and never use words like “moved” or “shifted” instead of “translation”. Count squares carefully from one vertex to its image, not from the edge of one shape to the edge of the other.

4.6.3 Adding, Subtracting and Multiplying by a Scalar (G25)

Column Vectors

Addition: $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} c \\ d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a + c \\ b + d \end{pmatrix}$     Subtraction: $\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} c \\ d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} a - c \\ b - d \end{pmatrix}$

Multiplying by a scalar $k$: $k\begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} ka \\ kb \end{pmatrix}$
$\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Work out $2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b}$.

$2\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 10 \end{pmatrix}$,   $3\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 9 \end{pmatrix}$
$2\mathbf{a} - 3\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} 4 - (-3) \\ 10 - 9 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{\begin{pmatrix} 7 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}}$
$\mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{q} = \begin{pmatrix} -3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Find $\mathbf{r}$ such that $\mathbf{p} + 2\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{q}$.

Rearrange like an equation: $2\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{q} - \mathbf{p} = \begin{pmatrix} -8 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$, so $\mathbf{r} = \begin{pmatrix} -4 \\ 1.5 \end{pmatrix}$
Find $m$ and $n$ such that $m\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} + n\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$.

Top: $2m + n = 8$.   Bottom: $-m + 3n = 3$, so $m = 3n - 3$.
Substitute: $2(3n - 3) + n = 8 \Rightarrow 7n = 14 \Rightarrow n = 2$, then $m = 3$.
Check: $3\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} + 2\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ ✓

Vectors on Diagrams: the Triangle Law

Adding two vectors means following one and then the other. The resultant $\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ goes straight from the start of $\mathbf{a}$ to the end of $\mathbf{b}$. Subtracting $\mathbf{b}$ means adding $-\mathbf{b}$ (travelling backwards along $\mathbf{b}$).

If $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$ then $$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{OB} = -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$$ “End minus start” — exactly like the column vector between two points.
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. Find $\overrightarrow{OB}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}$ and $\overrightarrow{CB}$.

Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal vectors, so $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{c}$ and $\overrightarrow{CB} = \mathbf{a}$.
$\overrightarrow{OB} = \overrightarrow{OA} + \overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$
$\overrightarrow{AC} = \overrightarrow{AO} + \overrightarrow{OC} = -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c} = \mathbf{c} - \mathbf{a}$
$ABCD$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD} = \mathbf{d}$. $E$ is the midpoint of $CD$. Find $\overrightarrow{BE}$.

$\overrightarrow{DC} = \mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{DE} = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{AE} = \mathbf{d} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}$.
$\overrightarrow{BE} = \overrightarrow{BA} + \overrightarrow{AE} = -\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{d} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{d} - \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}$

Vector expressions simplify just like algebra: collect like terms and expand brackets.

Simplify $3(\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}) - 2(\mathbf{a} - \mathbf{b})$.

$= 3\mathbf{a} + 6\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{a} + 8\mathbf{b}$
To find any vector on a diagram, pick a route along vectors you know. Going with an arrow uses $+$, going against an arrow uses $-$. Write the route first (e.g. $\overrightarrow{BE} = \overrightarrow{BA} + \overrightarrow{AE}$) — this earns method marks even if you slip later.

4.6.4 Parallel Vectors and Collinear Points Higher

Two vectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other. For example $6\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b} = 3(2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$, so $6\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$ is parallel to $2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}$ and three times as long. A negative multiple means parallel but in the opposite direction.

Points are collinear if they lie on the same straight line. To prove $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear:

  1. Find two vectors joining the points, e.g. $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$.
  2. Show one is a multiple of the other (so they are parallel).
  3. State that they share a common point ($B$), so the points lie on one line.
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC} = 6\mathbf{a} + 4\mathbf{b}$. Prove that $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear.

$\overrightarrow{BC} = 2(3\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b}) = 2\overrightarrow{AB}$, so $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is parallel to $\overrightarrow{BC}$.
They share the point $B$, so $A$, $B$ and $C$ are collinear. (Also $AB : BC = 1 : 2$.)
For what value of $k$ is $2\mathbf{a} + 3\mathbf{b}$ parallel to $k\mathbf{a} + 12\mathbf{b}$?

$12\mathbf{b} = 4 \times 3\mathbf{b}$, so the multiplier is $4$ and $k = 4 \times 2 = \mathbf{8}$.

4.6.5 Midpoints and Ratios Along a Line Higher

If $P$ lies on $AB$ with $AP : PB = m : n$, then $$\overrightarrow{AP} = \frac{m}{m+n}\,\overrightarrow{AB}$$ For the midpoint $M$ of $AB$: $\overrightarrow{AM} = \tfrac{1}{2}\overrightarrow{AB}$, and with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$: $\;\overrightarrow{OM} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b})$.
$\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OB} = \mathbf{b}$ and $P$ is on $AB$ with $AP : PB = 2 : 3$. Find $\overrightarrow{OP}$.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{AP} = \tfrac{2}{5}(\mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a})$
$\overrightarrow{OP} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{b} - \tfrac{2}{5}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{\tfrac{3}{5}a + \tfrac{2}{5}b}$
A ratio $m : n$ splits the line into $m + n$ parts. The most common error is using $\tfrac{m}{n}$ instead of $\tfrac{m}{m+n}$ — for $AP : PB = 1 : 3$, $P$ is $\tfrac{1}{4}$ of the way along, not $\tfrac{1}{3}$.

4.6.6 Vector Proofs Higher

Vector proof questions usually ask you to show that two lines are parallel, that points are collinear, or to find a ratio. The method is always the same: express every relevant vector in terms of the given vectors, simplify, then look for a scalar multiple.

$OAB$ is a triangle with $\overrightarrow{OA} = 3\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{OB} = 3\mathbf{b}$. $P$ is on $OA$ with $OP : PA = 1 : 2$ and $Q$ is on $AB$ with $AQ : QB = 2 : 1$. Prove that $PQ$ is parallel to $OB$.

$\overrightarrow{PA} = \tfrac{2}{3} \times 3\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{a}$
$\overrightarrow{AB} = 3\mathbf{b} - 3\mathbf{a}$, so $\overrightarrow{AQ} = \tfrac{2}{3}(3\mathbf{b} - 3\mathbf{a}) = 2\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a}$
$\overrightarrow{PQ} = \overrightarrow{PA} + \overrightarrow{AQ} = 2\mathbf{a} + 2\mathbf{b} - 2\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{b}$
$\overrightarrow{OB} = 3\mathbf{b}$, so $\overrightarrow{PQ} = \tfrac{2}{3}\overrightarrow{OB}$: a scalar multiple, hence $PQ$ is parallel to $OB$.
$OABC$ is a parallelogram with $\overrightarrow{OA} = \mathbf{a}$, $\overrightarrow{OC} = \mathbf{c}$. $M$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $\overrightarrow{OD} = 2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c}$. Prove that $O$, $M$ and $D$ are collinear.

$\overrightarrow{AB} = \mathbf{c}$, so $\overrightarrow{OM} = \mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{c}$
$\overrightarrow{OD} = 2\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{c} = 2(\mathbf{a} + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbf{c}) = 2\overrightarrow{OM}$
$\overrightarrow{OM}$ and $\overrightarrow{OD}$ are parallel and share the point $O$, so $O$, $M$ and $D$ are collinear ($M$ is the midpoint of $OD$).
Proof marks are lost by not finishing the argument. Always end with a sentence: “$\overrightarrow{OD}$ is a multiple of $\overrightarrow{OM}$, so they are parallel; they share point $O$, so $O$, $M$, $D$ are collinear.” Showing the vectors are multiples on its own only proves they are parallel.
