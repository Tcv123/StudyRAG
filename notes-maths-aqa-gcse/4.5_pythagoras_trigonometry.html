4.5 Pythagoras & Trigonometry

AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Geometry and measures — spec refs G20–G23 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

This topic is about finding missing lengths and angles in triangles. Foundation students use Pythagoras' theorem and the three trigonometric ratios in right-angled triangles in two dimensions, and learn the exact values of sin, cos and tan for the special angles. Higher students extend this to three-dimensional problems and to non-right-angled triangles with the sine rule, the cosine rule and the formula $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ for area.

4.5.1 Pythagoras' Theorem

Pythagoras' theorem: in any right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (the longest side, opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. $$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$ where $c$ is the hypotenuse. The specification requires you to know this formula.
Finding the hypotenuse (square, add, square root):
$c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$

Finding a shorter side (square, subtract, square root):
$a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$
A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

$c^2 = 9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225$
$c = \sqrt{225} = \mathbf{15}$ cm
A 5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall with its foot 1.4 m from the wall. How far up the wall does it reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse, so subtract: $h^2 = 5^2 - 1.4^2 = 25 - 1.96 = 23.04$
$h = \sqrt{23.04} = \mathbf{4.8}$ m

Is the Triangle Right-Angled?

The converse of Pythagoras: if the sides satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ (with $c$ the longest side), the triangle has a right angle opposite $c$. If $a^2 + b^2 \ne c^2$ it is not right-angled.

Show that a triangle with sides 20 cm, 21 cm and 29 cm is right-angled.

$20^2 + 21^2 = 400 + 441 = 841$ and $29^2 = 841$.
These are equal, so the triangle is right-angled (the right angle is opposite the 29 cm side).

Distance Between Two Points

Draw a right-angled triangle using the horizontal and vertical differences between the coordinates:

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$$
Find the distance between $A(-3, 4)$ and $B(5, -2)$.

Horizontal difference $= 5 - (-3) = 8$; vertical difference $= -2 - 4 = -6$
$d = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{100} = \mathbf{10}$ units

Isosceles and Equilateral Triangles

The line of symmetry of an isosceles triangle cuts it into two congruent right-angled triangles, so Pythagoras gives the perpendicular height.

An isosceles triangle has sides 13 cm, 13 cm and base 10 cm. Find its area.

Half the base is 5 cm. Height $= \sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{144} = 12$ cm
Area $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 12 = \mathbf{60}$ cm²
Always identify the hypotenuse first. A very common error is adding the squares when you need a shorter side. Check your answer: the hypotenuse must be the longest side.

4.5.2 Trigonometric Ratios (SOHCAHTOA)

In a right-angled triangle, label the sides relative to the angle $\theta$ you are using:
  • Hypotenuse (H) — the longest side, opposite the right angle
  • Opposite (O) — the side opposite $\theta$
  • Adjacent (A) — the side next to $\theta$ that is not the hypotenuse
The trigonometric ratios are fixed for each angle because all right-angled triangles with the same angle $\theta$ are similar.
SOH
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
CAH
$\cos\theta = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$
TOA
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$

Method

  1. Label the sides O, A, H relative to the angle.
  2. Tick the two sides involved (the one you know and the one you want).
  3. Choose the ratio that uses those two sides.
  4. Substitute and rearrange. To find an angle, use the inverse function: $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$.
Missing side (on top): In triangle ABC, angle B = 90°, angle A = 52° and AC = 15 cm. Find BC.

BC is opposite 52° and AC is the hypotenuse, so use SOH: $\sin 52° = \dfrac{BC}{15}$
$BC = 15 \sin 52° = 11.820\ldots \approx \mathbf{11.8}$ cm
Missing side (underneath): The side adjacent to 25° is $x$ and the opposite side is 80 m. Find $x$.

TOA: $\tan 25° = \dfrac{80}{x}$, so $x = \dfrac{80}{\tan 25°} = 171.56\ldots \approx \mathbf{172}$ m
Missing angle: The adjacent side is 6 cm and the hypotenuse is 11 cm. Find $\theta$.

CAH: $\cos\theta = \dfrac{6}{11}$, so $\theta = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{6}{11}\right) = 56.94\ldots \approx \mathbf{56.9°}$

Angles of Elevation and Depression

Both are measured from the horizontal. The angle of elevation looks up; the angle of depression looks down. By alternate angles, the angle of depression from A to B equals the angle of elevation from B to A.

Make sure your calculator is in degrees mode (a small D on the display). Keep the full calculator value for later steps and only round your final answer.

4.5.3 Exact Trigonometric Values

You must know these values without a calculator (spec ref G21). They come from two special triangles.

The 30°–60°–90° triangle is half of an equilateral triangle of side 2: the height is $\sqrt{2^2 - 1^2} = \sqrt{3}$. The 45° triangle is a right-angled isosceles triangle with shorter sides 1, so the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{2}$.

Note that $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$; either form is accepted. A useful pattern: the sin row reads $\frac{\sqrt{0}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{1}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{4}}{2}$, and the cos row is the same list backwards.

Without a calculator, find the exact length of the side opposite 60° in a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 8 cm.

opposite $= 8 \sin 60° = 8 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \mathbf{4\sqrt{3}}$ cm
Work out $\sin 30° + \cos 60° + \tan 45°$.

$= \tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2} + 1 = \mathbf{2}$
On Paper 1 (non-calculator), any trigonometry question will use these special angles. "Give an exact answer" means leave surds such as $4\sqrt{3}$ — do not convert to decimals.

4.5.4 Pythagoras and Trigonometry in 3D Higher

Find a right-angled triangle that lies in a flat plane inside the 3D shape, draw it separately, then use Pythagoras or SOHCAHTOA. You often need two triangles: one in the base, then one standing up.

Space diagonal of a cuboid with edges $a$, $b$, $c$: $$d = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}$$
Cuboid ABCDEFGH has AB = 5 cm, BC = 4 cm and CG = 3 cm. Find the length AG and the angle between AG and the base ABCD.

Base: $AC^2 = 5^2 + 4^2 = 41$
Triangle ACG (right angle at C): $AG = \sqrt{41 + 3^2} = \sqrt{50} = 7.07\ldots \approx \mathbf{7.1}$ cm
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{CG}{AC} = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{41}}$, so $\theta = \mathbf{25.1°}$

Angle Between a Line and a Plane

Drop a perpendicular from the top of the line down to the plane. The angle you want is between the line and the line joining its foot to where the perpendicular lands (its projection).

A pyramid has a square base of side 6 cm. The apex is directly above the centre of the base and each sloping edge is 9 cm. Find the height and the angle a sloping edge makes with the base.

Half of the base diagonal $= \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{6^2 + 6^2} = 3\sqrt{2} = 4.24\ldots$ cm
Height $= \sqrt{9^2 - (3\sqrt{2})^2} = \sqrt{81 - 18} = \sqrt{63} \approx \mathbf{7.94}$ cm
$\cos\theta = \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{9}$, so $\theta \approx \mathbf{61.9°}$
Sketch each 2D triangle separately and mark the right angle. In a pyramid, the apex is above the centre of the base, so you need half the diagonal, not the whole diagonal.

4.5.5 The Sine Rule Higher

For triangles without a right angle, label the vertices $A$, $B$, $C$ and the sides opposite them $a$, $b$, $c$.

Sine rule (the specification requires you to know it): $$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C} \qquad\text{or}\qquad \frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$ Use it when you know a side and its opposite angle (a matching pair), plus one other side or angle.
In triangle ABC, $A = 48°$, $B = 73°$ and $a = 12$ cm. Find $b$.

$\dfrac{b}{\sin 73°} = \dfrac{12}{\sin 48°} \Rightarrow b = \dfrac{12 \sin 73°}{\sin 48°} = 15.44\ldots \approx \mathbf{15.4}$ cm
In triangle ABC, $a = 10$ cm, $b = 13$ cm and $B = 70°$. Find angle $A$.

$\dfrac{\sin A}{10} = \dfrac{\sin 70°}{13} \Rightarrow \sin A = \dfrac{10 \sin 70°}{13} = 0.7228\ldots$
$A = \sin^{-1}(0.7228\ldots) = \mathbf{46.3°}$ (the alternative $133.7°$ is impossible because $133.7° + 70° > 180°$)

The Ambiguous Case

Because $\sin\theta = \sin(180° - \theta)$, an equation such as $\sin B = 0.8264$ has two solutions between 0° and 180°: $B = 55.7°$ or $B = 180° - 55.7° = 124.3°$. When you are given two sides and an angle that is not between them, check whether both fit with the angle sum. For $a = 7$, $b = 9$, $A = 40°$: $40° + 124.3° < 180°$, so two different triangles are possible.

Put the unknown on top: use $\frac{a}{\sin A}$ to find a side and $\frac{\sin A}{a}$ to find an angle. You only ever need two of the three fractions.

4.5.6 The Cosine Rule Higher

Cosine rule (the specification requires you to know it): $$a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$$ Rearranged to find an angle: $$\cos A = \frac{b^2 + c^2 - a^2}{2bc}$$ Use it when you know two sides and the included angle (SAS) or all three sides (SSS).
In triangle ABC, $AB = 11$ cm, $AC = 8$ cm and angle $BAC = 37°$. Find $BC$.

$BC^2 = 8^2 + 11^2 - 2 \times 8 \times 11 \times \cos 37° = 185 - 140.55\ldots = 44.44\ldots$
$BC = \sqrt{44.44\ldots} = 6.666\ldots \approx \mathbf{6.67}$ cm
A triangle has sides 7 cm, 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the largest angle.

The largest angle is opposite the longest side (12 cm):
$\cos\theta = \dfrac{7^2 + 9^2 - 12^2}{2 \times 7 \times 9} = \dfrac{-14}{126} = -0.111\ldots$
$\theta = \cos^{-1}(-0.111\ldots) = \mathbf{96.4°}$ — a negative cosine means the angle is obtuse.
Work out $b^2 + c^2$ and $2bc\cos A$ separately before subtracting — typing it all at once often goes wrong. Never use the cosine rule on a right-angled triangle; Pythagoras is quicker.

4.5.7 Area of a Triangle: $\tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ Higher

For any triangle with two sides $a$ and $b$ and the included angle $C$ between them: $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$$
A triangle has sides 7 cm and 12 cm with an included angle of 50°. Find its area.

Area $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 12 \times \sin 50° = 42 \sin 50° = 32.17\ldots \approx \mathbf{32.2}$ cm²
A triangle has area 30 cm² and two sides 8 cm and 10 cm with an acute angle between them. Find that angle.

$30 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 10 \times \sin C = 40\sin C \Rightarrow \sin C = 0.75$
$C = \sin^{-1}(0.75) = \mathbf{48.6°}$
Multi-step bearings problem. A ship sails 12 km from P on a bearing of 040° to Q, then 9 km on a bearing of 110° to R. Find PR and the bearing of R from P.

The back bearing of P from Q is 220°, so angle $PQR = 220° - 110° = 110°$.
$PR^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 - 2 \times 12 \times 9 \cos 110° = 298.87\ldots$, so $PR \approx \mathbf{17.3}$ km
$\sin QPR = \dfrac{9 \sin 110°}{17.28\ldots} = 0.4892\ldots \Rightarrow QPR = 29.3°$
Bearing of R from P $= 040° + 29.3° = \mathbf{069.3°}$
