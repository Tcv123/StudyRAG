1.4 Powers, Roots, Surds & Standard Form

AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Number — spec refs N6–N9 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

1.4.1 Powers and Roots

A power (or index) tells you how many times a number is multiplied by itself: $a^n = a \times a \times \cdots \times a$ ($n$ times). In $5^3$, 5 is the base and 3 is the index.

A root is the inverse of a power: $\sqrt{49} = 7$ because $7^2 = 49$; $\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$ because $4^3 = 64$; $\sqrt[4]{81} = 3$ because $3^4 = 81$.

Every positive number has two square roots, one positive and one negative ($\pm 7$ for 49), but the $\sqrt{\ }$ symbol means the positive one. Cube roots of negative numbers are negative: $\sqrt[3]{-8} = -2$.

Estimating Powers and Roots Higher

To estimate a root without a calculator, find the square (or cube) numbers either side, then refine.

Estimate $\sqrt{70}$ to 1 decimal place.

$8^2 = 64$ and $9^2 = 81$, so $8 < \sqrt{70} < 9$.
$8.3^2 = 68.89$ and $8.4^2 = 70.56$. 70 is closer to 70.56, so $\sqrt{70} \approx \mathbf{8.4}$
Estimate $2.9^4$.

$2.9 \approx 3$, so $2.9^4 \approx 3^4 = 81$ (a slight overestimate, as 2.9 < 3).

1.4.2 Laws of Indices

$$a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n} \qquad a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n} \qquad (a^m)^n = a^{mn}$$ $$a^0 = 1 \qquad a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n} \qquad a^1 = a$$ The laws only work when the bases are the same.
Simplify $4^7 \times 4^{-2} \div 4^3$, giving your answer as a power of 4.

$4^{7 + (-2) - 3} = \mathbf{4^2}$ (which equals 16)
Simplify $(2x^3)^4$.

Raise every part of the bracket to the power 4: $2^4 \times x^{12} = \mathbf{16x^{12}}$

Zero and Negative Indices

Why is $a^0 = 1$? By the division law, $5^3 \div 5^3 = 5^0$; but any non-zero number divided by itself is 1, so $5^0 = 1$.

Similarly $5^2 \div 5^4 = 5^{-2}$, and $\dfrac{25}{625} = \dfrac{1}{25}$, so $5^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{5^2}$. A negative index means reciprocal.

Work out (a) $2^{-3}$   (b) $\left(\tfrac{3}{4}\right)^{-2}$

(a) $\dfrac{1}{2^3} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{8}}$    (b) flip the fraction: $\left(\tfrac{4}{3}\right)^2 = \mathbf{\dfrac{16}{9}}$
Find $n$ when $3^n = 81 \times 3^2$.

$81 = 3^4$, so $3^n = 3^4 \times 3^2 = 3^6$, giving $\mathbf{n = 6}$
A negative index does not make the answer negative: $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$, not $-8$. And $(-2)^2 = 4$ but $-2^2 = -4$ — brackets matter.

Fractional Indices Higher

$$a^{\frac{1}{n}} = \sqrt[n]{a} \qquad\qquad a^{\frac{m}{n}} = \left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m$$ The denominator is the root, the numerator is the power, and a minus sign means reciprocal.
Evaluate $8^{\frac{2}{3}}$.

Root first: $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2$. Then power: $2^2 = \mathbf{4}$
Evaluate $16^{-\frac{1}{2}}$.

Reciprocal: $\dfrac{1}{16^{\frac{1}{2}}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{16}} = \mathbf{\dfrac{1}{4}}$
Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{27}{8}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}$.

Reciprocal: $\left(\dfrac{8}{27}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$. Cube root: $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Square: $\mathbf{\dfrac{4}{9}}$
Solve $9^x = \dfrac{1}{27}$.

Write both sides as powers of 3: $3^{2x} = 3^{-3}$, so $2x = -3$ and $\mathbf{x = -\tfrac{3}{2}}$
With a fractional index, take the root first — the numbers stay small. $64^{\frac{2}{3}} = 4^2 = 16$ is much easier than $\sqrt[3]{4096}$.

1.4.3 Calculating Exactly with Multiples of $\pi$

"Give your answer in terms of $\pi$" means leave $\pi$ in the answer as a symbol. This gives an exact answer instead of a rounded decimal.

A circle has diameter 10 cm. Find its exact area and circumference.

$r = 5$: Area $= \pi \times 5^2 = \mathbf{25\pi \text{ cm}^2}$; Circumference $= \pi \times 10 = \mathbf{10\pi \text{ cm}}$
A circle of diameter 10 cm is cut from a square of side 10 cm. Find the exact area left.

$100 - 25\pi$ cm² — this cannot be simplified further, because $100$ and $25\pi$ are not like terms.
Treat $\pi$ like a letter in algebra: $3\pi + 5\pi = 8\pi$, $\;2 \times 6\pi = 12\pi$, but $18 + 3\pi$ stays as it is.

1.4.4 Surds Higher

A surd is a root that cannot be written exactly as a fraction or terminating decimal, e.g. $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{5}$, $\sqrt{12}$. Leaving answers in surd form keeps them exact. ($\sqrt{9} = 3$ is not a surd.)
$$\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{b} \qquad \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} \qquad \sqrt{a} \times \sqrt{a} = a$$ But $\sqrt{a + b} \ne \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$.

Simplifying Surds

Find the largest square factor of the number under the root.

Simplify $\sqrt{12}$ and $\sqrt{72}$.

$\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \times 3} = \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{3} = \mathbf{2\sqrt{3}}$
$\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = \mathbf{6\sqrt{2}}$
Simplify $\sqrt{75} - \sqrt{27} + \sqrt{12}$.

$5\sqrt{3} - 3\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} = \mathbf{4\sqrt{3}}$   (collect like surds just like like terms)

Expanding Brackets with Surds

Expand and simplify $(2 + \sqrt{3})^2$.

$(2 + \sqrt{3})(2 + \sqrt{3}) = 4 + 2\sqrt{3} + 2\sqrt{3} + 3 = \mathbf{7 + 4\sqrt{3}}$
Expand and simplify $(3 + \sqrt{2})(3 - \sqrt{2})$.

$9 - 3\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} - 2 = \mathbf{7}$   (difference of two squares: $a^2 - b$)

Rationalising the Denominator

To rationalise means to remove the surd from the denominator of a fraction.

$$\frac{a}{\sqrt{b}} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{b}} \times \frac{\sqrt{b}}{\sqrt{b}} = \frac{a\sqrt{b}}{b}$$ $$\frac{a}{c + \sqrt{b}} \;\text{: multiply top and bottom by}\; c - \sqrt{b}\text{, so the denominator becomes } c^2 - b$$
Rationalise $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}}$.

$\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = \mathbf{2\sqrt{3}}$
Rationalise $\dfrac{7}{3 + \sqrt{2}}$.

$\dfrac{7(3 - \sqrt{2})}{(3 + \sqrt{2})(3 - \sqrt{2})} = \dfrac{21 - 7\sqrt{2}}{9 - 2} = \dfrac{21 - 7\sqrt{2}}{7} = \mathbf{3 - \sqrt{2}}$
"Give your answer in the form $a\sqrt{b}$" means $b$ must be as small as possible: $\sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}$, not $2\sqrt{18}$ or $3\sqrt{8}$.

1.4.5 Standard Form

A number is in standard form when it is written as $$A \times 10^n \quad \text{where } 1 \le A < 10 \text{ and } n \text{ is an integer}$$ Large numbers have a positive power; numbers between 0 and 1 have a negative power.
Ordinary numberStandard form
320 000$3.2 \times 10^5$
56 000 000$5.6 \times 10^7$
0.0007$7 \times 10^{-4}$
0.0000362$3.62 \times 10^{-5}$

Multiplying and Dividing

Deal with the number parts and the powers of 10 separately, then adjust so that $1 \le A < 10$.

Work out $(6 \times 10^4) \times (5 \times 10^3)$.

$6 \times 5 = 30$ and $10^4 \times 10^3 = 10^7$, giving $30 \times 10^7$.
30 is not between 1 and 10: $30 \times 10^7 = 3 \times 10^1 \times 10^7 = \mathbf{3 \times 10^8}$
Work out $(8.4 \times 10^6) \div (2.1 \times 10^{-2})$.

$8.4 \div 2.1 = 4$ and $10^6 \div 10^{-2} = 10^{6-(-2)} = 10^8$, so the answer is $\mathbf{4 \times 10^8}$

Adding and Subtracting

The powers must match, so convert to ordinary numbers (or to the same power of 10) first.

Work out $(4.2 \times 10^5) - (3.5 \times 10^4)$.

$420\,000 - 35\,000 = 385\,000 = \mathbf{3.85 \times 10^5}$

Calculator Displays

Calculators may show standard form as 4.7E-3 or 4.7×10⁻³. Both mean $4.7 \times 10^{-3} = 0.0047$. Always rewrite these properly; "4.7E-3" is not accepted as an answer.

Light travels at $3 \times 10^5$ km/s. The Sun is $1.5 \times 10^8$ km from the Earth. How long does sunlight take to reach the Earth?

$\text{time} = \dfrac{1.5 \times 10^8}{3 \times 10^5} = 0.5 \times 10^3 = 500$ seconds $= \mathbf{8 \text{ minutes } 20 \text{ seconds}}$
After multiplying, check that $A$ is between 1 and 10. Answers such as $30 \times 10^7$ or $0.5 \times 10^3$ are correct in value but are not in standard form and will lose the final mark.
