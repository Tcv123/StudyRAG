2.5 Equations & Inequalities

AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Algebra — spec refs A17–A22 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

2.5.1 Solving Linear Equations (A17)

An equation states that two expressions are equal and is true only for particular values of the unknown. To solve it, find the value(s) that make it true. A linear equation has no powers of $x$ higher than 1.

Golden rule: whatever you do to one side, do exactly the same to the other side (use inverse operations).

Two-step equations and brackets

Solve $4x + 9 = 37$.

Subtract 9: $4x = 28$    Divide by 4: $x = \mathbf{7}$
Solve $3(x - 2) = 18$.

Either expand: $3x - 6 = 18 \Rightarrow 3x = 24 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{8}$
or divide first: $x - 2 = 6 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{8}$

Unknown on both sides

Collect the $x$ terms on the side with the larger number of $x$s (this avoids negatives), and the numbers on the other side.

Solve $7x - 4 = 3x + 16$.

Subtract $3x$: $4x - 4 = 16$
Add 4: $4x = 20$
$x = \mathbf{5}$    Check: $7(5) - 4 = 31$ and $3(5) + 16 = 31$ ✓

Equations with fractions

Multiply every term by the lowest common denominator to clear the fractions. Put brackets round numerators.

Solve $\dfrac{2x - 1}{3} = \dfrac{x + 4}{2}$.

Multiply both sides by 6: $2(2x - 1) = 3(x + 4)$
$4x - 2 = 3x + 12$
$x = \mathbf{14}$
Solve $\dfrac{x + 1}{2} + \dfrac{x - 2}{3} = 4$.

Multiply every term by 6: $3(x + 1) + 2(x - 2) = 24$
$3x + 3 + 2x - 4 = 24 \Rightarrow 5x - 1 = 24 \Rightarrow 5x = 25 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{5}$

Approximate solutions from a graph

The solution of an equation such as $2x + 1 = 6$ is the $x$-coordinate where the line $y = 2x + 1$ meets the line $y = 6$. Reading from a graph gives an approximate solution — state it to a sensible accuracy (usually to the nearest small square).

Always show the inverse operation on each line. A wrong final answer with clear correct working still earns method marks. Check your answer by substituting back into the original equation.

2.5.2 Forming and Solving Equations (A21)

Many questions are set in context: you must translate the words into algebra, solve, and then interpret the answer in the context (with units).

  1. Choose a letter for the unknown (say what it stands for).
  2. Write expressions for the quantities in the question.
  3. Use a fact to form an equation (angles in a triangle sum to 180°, perimeter, total cost, ages…).
  4. Solve, then answer the actual question asked.
The angles of a quadrilateral are $x°$, $2x°$, $(3x - 20)°$ and $(x + 30)°$. Find the largest angle.

Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360°: $x + 2x + 3x - 20 + x + 30 = 360$
$7x + 10 = 360 \Rightarrow 7x = 350 \Rightarrow x = 50$
Angles: 50°, 100°, 130°, 80°. Largest angle $= \mathbf{130°}$.
Ben is three times as old as Amy. In 5 years' time the sum of their ages will be 46. How old is Amy now?

Let Amy be $a$, so Ben is $3a$. In 5 years: $(a + 5) + (3a + 5) = 46$
$4a + 10 = 46 \Rightarrow 4a = 36 \Rightarrow a = 9$. Amy is $\mathbf{9}$ years old.
Remember to add the time to both people in age problems, and to finish by answering the question — a question asking for "the largest angle" is not answered by "$x = 50$".

2.5.3 Quadratic Equations (A18)

A quadratic equation can be written as $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a \neq 0$. It can have two, one (repeated) or no real solutions (roots). The roots are where the graph $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ crosses the $x$-axis.

Solving by factorising

If two things multiply to give zero, at least one of them must be zero. So rearrange to "$= 0$", factorise, then set each bracket equal to zero.

Solve $x^2 - 9x + 20 = 0$.

Two numbers that multiply to $+20$ and add to $-9$: $-4$ and $-5$.
$(x - 4)(x - 5) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{4}$ or $x = \mathbf{5}$
Solve $x^2 + 5x = 0$.

Common factor: $x(x + 5) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{0}$ or $x = \mathbf{-5}$
(Do not divide by $x$ — you would lose the solution $x = 0$.)

Equations needing rearrangement Higher

Solve $x^2 = 5x + 14$.

Rearrange to $= 0$: $x^2 - 5x - 14 = 0$
$(x - 7)(x + 2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{7}$ or $x = \mathbf{-2}$
Solve $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$.

$ac = -6$: find numbers multiplying to $-6$ and adding to $5$: $6$ and $-1$.
$2x^2 + 6x - x - 3 = 2x(x + 3) - 1(x + 3) = (2x - 1)(x + 3) = 0$
$x = \mathbf{\tfrac{1}{2}}$ or $x = \mathbf{-3}$

Completing the square Higher

$$x^2 + bx + c = \left(x + \frac{b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2 + c$$ Halve the coefficient of $x$ to get the number in the bracket, then subtract its square.
Solve $x^2 - 6x + 4 = 0$ by completing the square, giving answers in surd form.

$(x - 3)^2 - 9 + 4 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 3)^2 = 5$
$x - 3 = \pm\sqrt{5} \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{3 \pm \sqrt{5}}$

When $a \neq 1$, take $a$ out as a factor of the $x^2$ and $x$ terms first: $2x^2 - 12x + 5 = 2(x^2 - 6x) + 5 = 2[(x - 3)^2 - 9] + 5 = 2(x - 3)^2 - 13$.

The quadratic formula Higher

For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$: $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$ The discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$: positive ⇒ two roots; zero ⇒ one repeated root; negative ⇒ no real roots.
Solve $2x^2 - 5x - 4 = 0$, giving answers to 2 decimal places.

$a = 2,\ b = -5,\ c = -4$
$x = \dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{(-5)^2 - 4(2)(-4)}}{2(2)} = \dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{25 + 32}}{4} = \dfrac{5 \pm \sqrt{57}}{4}$
$x = \mathbf{3.14}$ or $x = \mathbf{-0.64}$ (2 d.p.)
If a question says "give your answers to 2 decimal places" or "in surd form", the quadratic will not factorise — go straight to the formula or completing the square. Put negative values of $b$ and $c$ in brackets when substituting; $-b$ when $b = -5$ is $+5$.

Approximate solutions from a graph

The solutions of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the $x$-coordinates where $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ crosses the $x$-axis. To solve $x^2 - 2x - 4 = 0$ graphically, read off where the curve cuts $y = 0$: approximately $x \approx -1.2$ and $x \approx 3.2$.

2.5.4 Simultaneous Equations (A19)

Simultaneous equations are two equations in two unknowns that are both true at the same time. The solution is a pair of values (an $x$ and a $y$) — graphically, the point where the two graphs intersect.

Elimination method

Make the coefficients of one variable the same size, then add (if the signs are different) or subtract (if the signs are the same) to eliminate it.

Solve $2x + 3y = 13$ and $5x - 2y = 4$.

Multiply the first by 2: $4x + 6y = 26$
Multiply the second by 3: $15x - 6y = 12$
Add (signs of $y$ are different): $19x = 38 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{2}$
Substitute: $2(2) + 3y = 13 \Rightarrow 3y = 9 \Rightarrow y = \mathbf{3}$
Check in the second equation: $5(2) - 2(3) = 4$ ✓

Substitution method

Solve $y = 3x - 1$ and $2x + y = 9$.

Substitute: $2x + (3x - 1) = 9 \Rightarrow 5x = 10 \Rightarrow x = \mathbf{2}$, $y = 3(2) - 1 = \mathbf{5}$

Graphical solution

The lines $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = 7 - x$ cross at $(2, 5)$, so the solution of the simultaneous equations is $x = 2$, $y = 5$. Graphical solutions are approximate unless the intersection is on grid points.

Linear and quadratic simultaneous equations Higher

Rearrange the linear equation to make $x$ or $y$ the subject, substitute it into the quadratic, solve the resulting quadratic, then substitute each answer back into the linear equation to find the partner values. There are usually two pairs of solutions (two intersection points of a line and a curve).

Solve $y = 2x - 1$ and $y = x^2 - 4x + 4$.

$x^2 - 4x + 4 = 2x - 1 \Rightarrow x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0 \Rightarrow (x - 1)(x - 5) = 0$
$x = 1 \Rightarrow y = 1$;   $x = 5 \Rightarrow y = 9$
Solutions: $\mathbf{(1, 1)}$ and $\mathbf{(5, 9)}$
Solve $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ and $y = 2x - 5$.

$x^2 + (2x - 5)^2 = 25 \Rightarrow x^2 + 4x^2 - 20x + 25 = 25 \Rightarrow 5x^2 - 20x = 0$
$5x(x - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0$ or $x = 4$
$x = 0 \Rightarrow y = -5$;   $x = 4 \Rightarrow y = 3$. Solutions: $\mathbf{(0, -5)}$ and $\mathbf{(4, 3)}$
Pair your answers clearly, e.g. "$x = 1, y = 1$ and $x = 5, y = 9$". Always substitute back into the linear equation — substituting into a quadratic like $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ gives extra, wrong partner values.

2.5.5 Iteration Higher (A20)

Iteration finds an approximate solution to an equation by repeatedly using a formula of the form $x_{n+1} = g(x_n)$. Start with $x_1$, substitute to get $x_2$, substitute $x_2$ to get $x_3$, and so on. The values usually get closer and closer to a solution. The suffix $n$ tells you the position: $x_{n+1}$ is the term after $x_n$.

Showing a root lies in an interval

If $f(x)$ changes sign between $x = a$ and $x = b$ (and the graph is continuous), then $f(x) = 0$ has a root between $a$ and $b$.

(a) Show that $x^3 - 3x - 5 = 0$ has a solution between 2 and 3.
$x = 2$: $8 - 6 - 5 = -3$    $x = 3$: $27 - 9 - 5 = 13$. Sign change, so a solution lies between 2 and 3.

(b) Rearrange: $x^3 = 3x + 5 \Rightarrow x = \sqrt[3]{3x + 5}$

(c) Use $x_{n+1} = \sqrt[3]{3x_n + 5}$ with $x_1 = 2$:
$x_2 = \sqrt[3]{11} = 2.2239\ldots$
$x_3 = \sqrt[3]{3(2.2239\ldots) + 5} = 2.2683\ldots$
$x_4 = 2.2769\ldots$,   $x_5 = 2.2786\ldots$   → converging to about $\mathbf{2.28}$
Use the ANS key on your calculator: type 2, press =, then type the formula using ANS in place of $x_n$ and press = repeatedly. Write down each value to at least 4 decimal places but carry the full value in the calculator.

2.5.6 Linear Inequalities (A22)

An inequality compares two quantities using $<$ (less than), $>$ (greater than), $\le$ (less than or equal to) or $\ge$ (greater than or equal to). Its solution is usually a range of values.

Solving linear inequalities

Solve exactly like an equation, except: if you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

Solve $5 - 2x \le 13$.

Subtract 5: $-2x \le 8$
Divide by $-2$ and reverse the sign: $x \ge \mathbf{-4}$
$n$ is an integer with $-6 < 2n \le 8$. List the possible values of $n$.

Divide every part by 2: $-3 < n \le 4$
$n = \mathbf{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4}$ (not $-3$, because the inequality is strict)

Number lines

SymbolMeaningNumber line
$<$ or $>$strict — boundary not includedopen (empty) circle
$\le$ or $\ge$boundary includedclosed (filled) circle

Set notation Higher

Solution sets can be written in set notation: $\{x : x > 5\}$ means "the set of all $x$ such that $x$ is greater than 5". Two separate regions are joined with the union symbol: $\{x : x < -2\} \cup \{x : x > 3\}$. A range such as $-1 \le x < 4$ is written $\{x : -1 \le x < 4\}$.

2.5.7 Quadratic Inequalities Higher

  1. Rearrange so one side is 0 and solve the equation to find the critical values.
  2. Sketch the ∪-shaped graph through the critical values.
  3. "$< 0$" means the part of the curve below the $x$-axis (between the roots); "$> 0$" means above (outside the roots — two separate inequalities).
Solve $x^2 - x - 6 < 0$.

$(x - 3)(x + 2) = 0$ gives critical values $x = -2$ and $x = 3$.
The curve is below the axis between the roots: $\mathbf{-2 < x < 3}$
Solve $2x^2 - 7x - 15 > 0$, giving the answer in set notation.

$(2x + 3)(x - 5) = 0 \Rightarrow x = -\tfrac{3}{2}$ or $x = 5$
Above the axis outside the roots: $x < -\tfrac{3}{2}$ or $x > 5$
Set notation: $\mathbf{\{x : x < -\tfrac{3}{2}\} \cup \{x : x > 5\}}$
Never write "$-2 > x > 3$" for the outside region — no number is both less than $-2$ and greater than 3. Write two inequalities joined by "or". Also, $x^2 > 9$ is not just $x > 3$: the full answer is $x < -3$ or $x > 3$.

2.5.8 Inequalities in Two Variables (Regions) Higher

An inequality such as $x + y < 6$ describes a region of the coordinate grid. Draw the boundary line $x + y = 6$, then decide which side is wanted by testing a point not on the line (the origin is easiest: $0 + 0 < 6$ is true, so the origin's side is the region).

Boundary conventions: dashed line for $<$ or $>$ (points on the line are not included); solid line for $\le$ or $\ge$ (points on the line are included).

Region R satisfies $y > 1$, $x + y < 6$ and $y \le 2x$. The point $(2, 3)$ lies in R: $3 > 1$ ✓, $2 + 3 = 5 < 6$ ✓, $3 \le 4$ ✓.

When asked for integer coordinates in a region, check each candidate point against every inequality, and remember that points on a dashed boundary do not count.
