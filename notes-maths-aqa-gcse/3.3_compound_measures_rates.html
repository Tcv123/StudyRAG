3.3 Compound Measures & Rates of Change

AQA GCSE Mathematics 8300 — Ratio, Proportion & Rates of Change — spec refs R1, R11, R14, R15 — Papers 1 (non-calculator), 2 and 3 (calculator)

3.3.1 Converting Between Standard Units

Before you can use any compound measure you must have the quantities in consistent units. Many marks are lost simply because a time was left in minutes when the speed was in km per hour, or an area was in cm² when the pressure was wanted in N/m².

Length, mass, capacity and time

QuantityKey facts
Length10 mm = 1 cm    100 cm = 1 m    1000 m = 1 km
Mass1000 mg = 1 g    1000 g = 1 kg    1000 kg = 1 tonne
Capacity1000 ml = 1 litre    100 cl = 1 litre    1 ml = 1 cm³    1000 litres = 1 m³
Time60 s = 1 min    60 min = 1 h    3600 s = 1 h    24 h = 1 day
Time is not decimal. 2.25 hours is 2 hours 15 minutes (0.25 × 60 = 15), not 2 hours 25 minutes. To change minutes into hours, divide by 60: 40 minutes = $\frac{40}{60} = \frac{2}{3}$ hour.

Area and volume units

Area and volume conversions use the square and cube of the length conversion factor, because every dimension is converted.

Area: 1 cm² = 100 mm²   1 m² = 10 000 cm²
Volume: 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³   1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³

If 1 m = 100 cm, then 1 m² = $100^2$ cm² and 1 m³ = $100^3$ cm³.
Convert 4500 cm³ into m³.

1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³, so divide by 1 000 000:
$4500 \div 1\,000\,000 = \mathbf{0.0045}$ m³
Convert 3.2 m² into cm².

$3.2 \times 10\,000 = \mathbf{32\,000}$ cm²

3.3.2 Compound Units

A compound unit combines two (or more) units to describe a rate — how much of one quantity there is per unit of another. Examples: km/h (kilometres per hour), m/s, £/hour (rate of pay), pence per gram (unit price), g/cm³ (density), N/m² (pressure).

The word “per” means “divide”. A rate of pay of £12 per hour means $\text{pay} \div \text{hours} = 12$. Read the compound unit and it tells you the formula: km/h means kilometres ÷ hours.

Converting compound units

Convert each part of the unit separately.

Convert 72 km/h into m/s.

72 km = 72 000 m and 1 hour = 3600 s
$72\,000 \div 3600 = \mathbf{20}$ m/s

Shortcut: km/h → m/s divide by 3.6; m/s → km/h multiply by 3.6.
Convert a density of 2.7 g/cm³ into kg/m³.

1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³, so 1 m³ has mass $2.7 \times 1\,000\,000 = 2\,700\,000$ g = 2700 kg
Density = $\mathbf{2700}$ kg/m³

3.3.3 Speed, Distance and Time

$$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$$ $$\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} \qquad \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}$$ Cover the quantity you want in the triangle to see the formula.
A car travels for 45 minutes at 64 km/h. How far does it go?

45 minutes = 0.75 hours
Distance = $64 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{48}$ km
A lorry travels 84 miles at an average speed of 56 mph. It leaves at 10:45. When does it arrive?

Time = $84 \div 56 = 1.5$ hours = 1 hour 30 minutes
Arrival time = 10:45 + 1:30 = 12:15

Average speed for a journey in several parts

Average speed is total distance ÷ total time. It is not the mean of the separate speeds.

A car travels 30 km at 60 km/h and then 30 km at 40 km/h. Find the average speed.

Time 1 = $30 \div 60 = 0.5$ h    Time 2 = $30 \div 40 = 0.75$ h
Average speed = $\dfrac{60}{1.25} = \mathbf{48}$ km/h   (not 50 km/h)
Include any stops in the total time when asked for the average speed “for the whole journey”. Always state units, and check the answer is sensible (a person walks at about 5 km/h, a car on a motorway about 110 km/h).

3.3.4 Rates of Pay and Unit Pricing

Rates of pay (£ per hour) and unit prices (pence per gram, £ per litre) are compound measures used in everyday contexts. AQA expects you to make comparisons — for example deciding which pack is the best buy.

Pack A: 500 g for £1.80. Pack B: 750 g for £2.55. Which is better value?

Pack A: $180 \div 5 = 36$p per 100 g
Pack B: $255 \div 7.5 = 34$p per 100 g
Pack B is better value because it costs less per 100 g.
Amy earns £11.40 per hour. Ben earns £21 600 a year, working 40 hours a week for 48 weeks. Who has the higher hourly rate?

Ben's hours = $40 \times 48 = 1920$    Ben's rate = $21\,600 \div 1920 = £11.25$ per hour
Amy earns 15p per hour more.
When comparing, compare like with like (cost per gram for every option, or amount per £1 for every option) and finish with a clear statement that uses your figures. A correct choice with no working scores nothing.

3.3.5 Density

Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance. Common units are g/cm³ and kg/m³.
$$\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}$$ $$\text{Mass} = \text{Density} \times \text{Volume} \qquad \text{Volume} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Density}}$$
A block of wood measures 12 cm by 5 cm by 4 cm and has mass 168 g. Find its density.

Volume = $12 \times 5 \times 4 = 240$ cm³
Density = $168 \div 240 = \mathbf{0.7}$ g/cm³
300 cm³ of liquid A (density 1.2 g/cm³) is mixed with 200 cm³ of liquid B (density 0.9 g/cm³). Find the density of the mixture.

Mass of A = $300 \times 1.2 = 360$ g    Mass of B = $200 \times 0.9 = 180$ g
Density = $\dfrac{360 + 180}{300 + 200} = \dfrac{540}{500} = \mathbf{1.08}$ g/cm³
For mixtures, never average the two densities. Find the total mass and the total volume, then divide.

3.3.6 Pressure

Pressure is the force acting per unit area. Force is measured in newtons (N), so pressure is measured in N/m² (also called pascals, Pa) or N/cm².
$$\text{Pressure} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}} \qquad \text{Force} = \text{Pressure} \times \text{Area} \qquad \text{Area} = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Pressure}}$$

For the same force, a smaller area gives a greater pressure. A block resting on its smallest face exerts the greatest pressure.

A box of weight 72 N has a rectangular base 40 cm by 30 cm. Find the pressure on the floor in N/m².

Area = $0.4 \times 0.3 = 0.12$ m²
Pressure = $72 \div 0.12 = \mathbf{600}$ N/m²
A cuboid 20 cm × 10 cm × 5 cm has weight 40 N. Find the greatest pressure it can exert.

Smallest face = $10 \times 5 = 50$ cm²
Greatest pressure = $40 \div 50 = \mathbf{0.8}$ N/cm²
Watch the area units. If the answer is wanted in N/m², convert the lengths to metres before multiplying: 40 cm × 30 cm = 0.4 m × 0.3 m = 0.12 m² (not 1200 m²).

3.3.7 Gradient of a Straight Line as a Rate of Change

When a graph is a straight line, its gradient tells you how fast the $y$-quantity changes for each unit of the $x$-quantity. The units of the gradient are “$y$-units per $x$-unit”.

$$\text{Gradient} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
GraphGradient meansUnits
Distance–timeSpeedm/s, km/h
Velocity–timeAccelerationm/s²
Cost–time (e.g. plumber)Charge per hour£ per hour
Depth of water–timeRate of filling or emptyingcm per minute
Conversion graph (e.g. £ to €)Exchange rate€ per £
A plumber's charge is shown by a straight line through (0, 45) and (3, 141).

Gradient = $\dfrac{141 - 45}{3 - 0} = \dfrac{96}{3} = 32$ → the plumber charges £32 per hour.
The $y$-intercept, 45, is a fixed call-out charge of £45.
Cost of a 5-hour job: $32 \times 5 + 45 = \mathbf{£205}$
The depth of water in a tank falls in a straight line from 80 cm at $t = 0$ to 20 cm at $t = 20$ minutes.

Gradient = $\dfrac{20 - 80}{20} = -3$ → the depth decreases by 3 cm per minute.
A negative gradient means the quantity is decreasing. When asked to “interpret” a gradient, give the number and the units in context, e.g. “the water level falls by 3 cm every minute”.

3.3.8 Graphs of Direct and Inverse Proportion

  • Direct proportion ($y = kx$): a straight line through the origin. The gradient is the constant $k$ (the rate). Doubling $x$ doubles $y$.
  • Inverse proportion ($y = \dfrac{k}{x}$): for $x > 0$ a curve that falls and gets closer and closer to both axes without touching them. Doubling $x$ halves $y$, and $xy = k$ is constant.
A straight line that does not pass through the origin (like the plumber's charge) shows a linear relationship but not direct proportion.

3.3.9 Average and Instantaneous Rates of Change Higher

On a curved graph the rate of change is different at every point, so we need two ideas:

Chord: a straight line joining two points on a curve. Its gradient is the average rate of change between those two points.

Tangent: a straight line that touches the curve at one point (without crossing it there). Its gradient is the instantaneous rate of change at that point — this is the gradient of the curve at that point.
Average rate of change from $x = a$ to $x = b$ (gradient of chord): $$\frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$ Instantaneous rate of change at $x = a$: draw the tangent at $x = a$, choose two points far apart on it, and find rise ÷ run.
Find the average rate of change of $y = x^2$ between $x = 1$ and $x = 4$.

$\dfrac{4^2 - 1^2}{4 - 1} = \dfrac{16 - 1}{3} = \mathbf{5}$

Estimating a gradient from a tangent

  1. Place a ruler so it touches the curve at the point, with the curve the same distance away on each side.
  2. Draw a long straight line (the tangent).
  3. Pick two points on the tangent that are far apart and easy to read.
  4. Gradient = change in $y$ ÷ change in $x$. Interpret it with units in context.
A car's distance–time graph is a curve. The tangent at $t = 5$ s passes through (2, 0) and (8, 54), with distance in metres.

Gradient = $\dfrac{54 - 0}{8 - 2} = \dfrac{54}{6} = 9$
The car's (instantaneous) speed at $t = 5$ s is about $\mathbf{9}$ m/s.
The volume of water in a container is $V = 2t^2$ litres after $t$ minutes.

Average rate from $t = 1$ to $t = 3$: $\dfrac{18 - 2}{3 - 1} = 8$ litres per minute.
The tangent at $t = 2$ passes through (1, 0) and (3, 16): gradient $= \dfrac{16}{2} = 8$ litres per minute.
So the instantaneous rate at $t = 2$ is $\mathbf{8}$ litres per minute.

If a distance–time curve gets steeper, the object is accelerating (speed increasing); if it gets less steep, the object is decelerating. A horizontal tangent means the rate of change is zero at that instant (e.g. a ball at its highest point).

Gradients from a hand-drawn tangent are estimates, so mark schemes accept a range of answers. Draw the tangent long, use points far apart, and always say whether your value is an average rate (chord) or an instantaneous rate (tangent).
